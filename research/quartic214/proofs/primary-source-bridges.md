## Faltings: the actual field and Tate objects

I retrieved and read the published second edition of *Rational Points* (1986), including the printed title/copyright pages and Faltings, “Complements to Mordell”, Theorem 1(a)–(b), printed p.204 (PDF page 212). I inspected the page image as well as its extracted text. The exact downloaded PDF has SHA-256 `3122011c0f12cc991fc0ff1601bb77cc1cd3e5ab2a0db023c0bf224ea576b6aa`; access URL: <https://yhzhumath.github.io/Mordell2024/RP.pdf>. This is a scan of the published edition, not an identification inferred from a search result. The publisher's edition identifier is <https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-663-06812-9>.

The theorem concerns an abelian variety over a finitely generated field of characteristic zero and any prime ell. It gives semisimplicity of the rational Tate representation and the endomorphism comparison (indeed the printed statement gives the integral comparison). Apply it with the **same** number field L' constructed in H1 and ell=5. A number field is finitely generated over Q; after inverting its discriminant, its ring of integers supplies the smooth finitely generated Z-algebra in the source's setup. This applies to A_a, (A_f) over L', and their finite products. No field obtained by splitting an evaluation fiber is substituted for L'.

H1 has shown that rho_5 restricted to G_L' is the actual covariant Tate action of A_a: the isogeny and scalar normalizations are 1 there. Under the retained assertion that all geometric endomorphisms are defined over L', the rational endomorphism algebra is B. After extending scalars to Qbar_5 it becomes M_2(Qbar_5). The semisimple representation V_5(A_a) therefore has one absolutely irreducible constituent W with multiplicity two: the endomorphism algebra of a semisimple representation is a product of matrix algebras, and here that product is the single M_2. This is exactly the W consumed in H2, rather than a finite scalar twist. Semisimplicity remains valid after the coefficient extension because the finite-dimensional semisimple algebra generated by the image is separable in characteristic zero.

For completeness, the isogeny conclusion in H2 follows from the stated endomorphism theorem, without needing a differently normalized “isogeny theorem”. Apply (b) to X times Y and use the two factor idempotents. The off-diagonal block gives Hom_L'(X,Y) tensor Q_5 = Hom_G_L'(V_5(X),V_5(Y)). Take X=((A_f)_L')^2 and Y=A_a^e. H2's trace and semisimple-character comparison identifies their rational Tate representations; that comparison remains a separately stated input. The invertible maps form a nonempty open subset of this Hom space. Rational combinations of a basis of Hom_L'(X,Y) tensor Q are dense in its Q_5 extension, so one such combination is invertible on Tate modules. Clear its denominators. Its image has full dimension, and its kernel has dimension zero, hence it is an L'-isogeny. This argument neither assumes integral Tate lattices agree nor introduces an extra field extension.

## Poincare reducibility over the stated ground field

I read the author-hosted scan of Milne, “Abelian varieties”, *Arithmetic Geometry* (1986), Proposition 12.1 and the following decomposition paragraph, printed p.122 (PDF page 20). Its PDF SHA-256 is `86259344a576e4ef75a9d81e9bf43143f872a72b14cf8fcc2703f3aea6349136`; source: <https://www.jmilne.org/math/articles/1986b.pdf>. This scan is image-only; the page image was inspected directly. The theorem constructs a complement using an ample sheaf and the reduced connected kernel of the associated dual morphism. Those data are over the original field, so the complement is over that field, not just over its algebraic closure.

For the surjection J over Q to A_f already supplied in H2, let K be the connected kernel. Apply Proposition 12.1 to K inside J over Q. Its complement B' maps onto A_f with finite kernel; thus B' is a Q-defined subvariety Q-isogenous to A_f. Base change to L' gives the B_0 used in H2–H3. Separately, apply the same proposition over L' to decompose varieties into simple isogeny factors. Since A_a is absolutely simple under H0 and ((A_f)_L')^2 is L'-isogenous to A_a^e, every simple factor of (A_f)_L' is L'-isogenous to A_a. Dimensions give e=2r and (A_f)_L' is L'-isogenous to A_a^r. The division-algebra and absolute-simplicity premise remains H0's actual surface statement; Poincare reducibility does not supply that premise.

## Li's Fricke condition for the modular quotient

I read Li, “Newforms and functional equations”, *Mathematische Annalen* 212 (1975), pp.294–296, in Göttingen's digitized published volume <https://gdz.sub.uni-goettingen.de/id/PPN235181684_0212>. The library's IIIF manifest labels canvas 00000304 as printed p.296. `PRIMARY_SOURCE_ACCESS.json` records the exact public page-image URLs and hashes. Page294 defines normalized primitive newforms; page296 defines the Fricke operator H_M and coefficient-conjugation operator K and, immediately before Lemma6, gives F|H_M=gamma F|K for such a form. The character is arbitrary; no trivial-nebentypus assumption occurs there.

Use Li's M=N from H2 and Li's second level parameter 1. The final form f in H1 is the primitive weight-two form, after the permitted finite twist has been absorbed; it is that same final form to which the formula applies. For each coefficient embedding tau, f^tau is a normalized primitive form at the same level with character epsilon^tau, as in the inherited modular-factor construction. Its Fricke image is a nonzero scalar times its coefficient conjugate. Conjugating the embedding gives another embedding of the coefficient field into C, so the latter form is in the span of all coefficient conjugates. Fricke is invertible, hence this span is Fricke-stable. Any nonzero scalar difference between normalized slash conventions preserves that span; no numerical value of gamma is consumed. This supplies the specific Fricke-span hypothesis of the already checked published Shimura Theorem1 used in H2. It does not prove the existence/modularity of f, nor replace the independent normalized modularity application in H0–H1.

The nine obligations for this identification are: (1) a normalized primitive weight-two form with finite character, supplied by H1; (2) the same f and all its coefficient conjugates, not an untwisted predecessor; (3) every coefficient embedding tau, including conjugate embeddings; (4) the operator H_N and coefficient conjugation K, with no discarded scalar affecting span; (5) the exact stable span consumed by Shimura; (6) Li's (M,1)=(N,1) and complex coefficient conventions, with only span used; (7) no added good-prime condition or character-conductor restriction; (8) nonzero forms and invertible Fricke, hence nonzero gamma, including nontrivial nebentypus; and (9) the resulting modular quotient/Tate constituent in H2, with the retained Galois-conjugacy and modularity premises explicit. This is a source/application bridge awaiting the new independent review, not a new assertion that those premises were all audited here.

## Original-normalization lower bound used in H3

I also checked the publisher PDF of Gaudron–Rémond, “Théorème des périodes et degrés minimaux d'isogénies”, *Commentarii Mathematici Helvetici* 89 (2014), 343–403, DOI <https://doi.org/10.4171/CMH/322>, from <https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/43351?nt=1>. The precise downloaded hash is in `PRIMARY_SOURCE_ACCESS.json`. Definition2.1 and §2.3, printed pp.351–352, define a stable height h and identify hF=h-(dim A/2)log pi. Corollary8.4, printed p.400, proves h(A)>=-(dim A/2)log(2pi) without a polarization hypothesis. I checked both page images. Combining the two gives hF(A)>=-dim A log(pi sqrt2)>=-3dim A/2; the final numerical relaxation is also stated on p.352.

This applies over the same number field L' to every quotient C_i and J/B_0 in H3. These are abelian varieties defined over L', with no principal polarization or new ramification condition required. All i=1,...,r are covered, including growing r; for a zero-dimensional quotient the bound is 0. No isogeny-invariance assertion is used. Thus the lower bound consumed when isolating hF(B_0) has the required original stable normalization. This closes that particular Bost-normalization source/application record at author level, pending review; it does not supply Rémond's superadditivity inequality.

The nine obligations are supplied by: (1) an abelian variety over a number field, (2) the actual C_i and J/B_0, (3) every i and both positive- and zero-dimensional quotients, (4) the displayed stable-height normalization conversion, (5) the identical hF in H3, (6) the dimension-dependent correction exactly retained, (7) no extra local hypothesis, (8) no principal-polarization or positive-dimension exception, and (9) the bound sum hF(C_i)<=hF(B_0)<=hF(J)+3(g-2r)/2 when combined with the separately retained superadditivity input.
