## 2. From the different to the last upper break

Here is the ramification calculation with conventions explicit. Let
K/Q_p be a finite Galois extension with ramification index e, maximal
unramified subfield K_0, and uniformizer pi. Normalize v_K(pi)=1.
The minimal polynomial f(X)=X^e+sum_(i=0..e-1) a_i X^i over K_0
is Eisenstein and O_K=O_K0[pi]. Therefore the different exponent is
delta=v_K(f'(pi)). This also follows from the monogenic trace-dual
description. Each nonzero term i a_i pi^(i-1) in the derivative has
valuation congruent to i-1 modulo e; these residues, including the
leading derivative term e pi^(e-1), are distinct. The minimum valuation
cannot cancel, so

    delta <= v_K(e pi^(e-1)) = e v_p(e)+e-1.            (5)

The unramified base field is essential to v_K(e)=e v_p(e).
The assertion includes e=1, with delta=0.

For completeness, the upper integral formula can be derived directly
from the same derivative. Put G=Gal(K/Q_p), with G_0 inertia and
G_i={sigma in G_0: v_K(sigma pi-pi)>=i+1} for integers i>=1.
Since f'(pi)=product_(sigma in G_0, sigma!=1)(pi-sigma pi), counting
each positive valuation by its integer levels gives

    delta=sum_(i>=0)(|G_i|-1).

Use G_t=G_ceiling(t) for real t>0 and define the Herbrand function
phi(t)=integral_0^t |G_s|/e ds. Its inverse defines G^u=G_phi^-1(u).
Changing variables u=phi(t) now gives, with no missing tame term,

    delta/e = 1-1/e + integral_0^infinity (1-1/|G^u|) du.  (6)

These definitions and quotient compatibility are the standard ones:
see [Milne ANT v3.08, Proposition7.55, Remark7.56 and Theorem7.58](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/ANT.pdf)
and [Milne CFT v4.03, I.4, Proposition4.4](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CFTc.pdf).
They are also the conventions invoked at M:8363–8378. The displayed
calculation supplies the particular integral used here, instead of
substituting into the manuscript's coarse numerical bound.

Let b_last be the last positive upper break, or0 when there is no
wild ramification. Until b_last the upper group is nontrivial, so the
integrand in(6) is at least1/2, except at irrelevant endpoints. Equations
(5)–(6) imply

    b_last <=2 v_p(e).                                 (7)

No abelian or Hasse–Arf assumption is used. Large tame inertia cancels
in the integral change of variables and causes no extra term in(7).
