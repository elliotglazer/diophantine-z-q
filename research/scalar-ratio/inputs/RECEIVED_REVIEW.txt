## 1. The formal target now starts from the actual matrices

The development keeps the common-strict presentation
\[
D_F=[D_0\mid A_F],
\qquad
D_S=[D_0\mid A_S],
\]
the actual cycles
\[
z_F=(h_F,v),\qquad z_S=(h_S,Jv),
\]
and the actual closed functionals
\[
D_Fz_F=D_Sz_S=0,\qquad
\lambda_FD_F=\lambda_SD_S=0.
\]

Crucially, the determinant coordinate is specified by the **full matrix identity**
\[
\boxed{z\lambda=u\,\operatorname{adj}(D).}
\]

It is **not defined to equal the formula we want to prove**.

For a fractional complement \(x\), set
\[
\eta=\det[v,x]\ne0,\qquad
a=A_Fx,\qquad b=A_SJx,
\]
\[
\theta=\det[D_0\mid a\mid b]\ne0,\qquad
\delta=\det J,
\]
and define the cross-boundary scalars by literal evaluation:
\[
q_F=\lambda_F(b),\qquad q_S=\lambda_S(a).
\]

The candidate theorem `common_strict_coordinate_formula` derives
\[
\boxed{
u_F=-\frac{\eta q_F}{\theta},
\qquad
u_S=\frac{\delta\eta q_S}{\theta}.
}
\]

Separate lemmas establish that the normal-frame hypotheses force a nonzero adjugate and that the coordinate satisfying the matrix identity is unique. Thus the theorem can be applied to coordinates supplied independently by their defining specification.

## 2. A cleaner proof makes the sign and common factor explicit

I simplified the normal-form argument.

Use the source frames
\[
B_F=[e_1,\ldots,e_m,z_F,(0,x)],
\qquad
B_S=[e_1,\ldots,e_m,z_S,(0,Jx)].
\]

Instead of using two different final \(2\times2\) normal blocks, order the target complement columns differently:
\[
T_F=[D_0\mid b\mid a],
\qquad
T_S=[D_0\mid a\mid b].
\]

Both complexes then have the **same** normal differential:
\[
D_FB_F=T_FN,\qquad D_SB_S=T_SN,
\qquad
N=\operatorname{diag}(I_m,0,1).
\]

The determinant factors are
\[
\det B_F=\eta,\qquad
\det B_S=\delta\eta,\qquad
\det T_F=-\theta,\qquad
\det T_S=\theta.
\]

Closedness determines the normalized rows:
\[
\lambda_FT_F=q_Fe_{m+1}^{*},
\qquad
\lambda_ST_S=q_Se_{m+1}^{*}.
\]

The scalar formulas now follow from one change-of-frame theorem and one diagonal-adjugate calculation. The candidate uses the pinned Mathlib adjugate and determinant interfaces for those steps.  

**This is an arbitrary-size calculation, not merely a formalization of the \(2\times2\) counterexample.** The entire common factor \(\theta\), including any nonunit strict contribution, remains present until cancellation.

## 3. The integral criterion is stronger than the quotient formula

The familiar quotient statement is
\[
\frac{\delta u_F}{u_S}=-\frac{q_F}{q_S},
\]
when the denominator is nonzero.

But the more useful formal interface avoids division by either functional:
\[
\boxed{q_S(\delta u_F)+q_Fu_S=0.}
\]

For the actual integral subring \(R\subset L\), define
\[
\operatorname{UnitRelated}_R(a,b)
\iff
\exists \epsilon\in R^\times,\quad a=\epsilon b.
\]

This must use **units of \(R\)**. Testing `IsUnit` in the fraction field would declare every nonzero discrepancy acceptable and destroy the arithmetic content.

With \(\delta\) an integral unit, the candidate derives
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{UnitRelated}_R(u_F,u_S)
\iff
\operatorname{UnitRelated}_R(q_F,q_S).
}
\]

This formulation allows a common nonunit factor and includes the case where both functionals—and hence both coordinates—vanish. It does not cover a zero local class: \(\eta\ne0\) remains an explicit hypothesis.

For a DVR, this is the desired equality-of-valuations criterion. The development also includes a consumer for a multiplicative valuation that is trivial on integral units.

## 4. The arithmetic adapter now has a precise, noncircular statement

The adapter uses the **same** \(\lambda_F,\lambda_S\) stored in the common-strict data. It requires the full boundary–cup identities
\[
\lambda_F(A_Ss)=c_F\beta(v,s)
\qquad\text{for every }s,
\]
\[
\lambda_S(A_Ff)=-c_S\beta(f,Jv)
\qquad\text{for every }f,
\]
where
\[
\beta(f,s)=\varepsilon\,\omega(f,Js),
\qquad J^2=1,
\]
and \(\omega\) is the alternating local area form.

These are lower-level naturality hypotheses. Neither \(q_F=q_S\) nor determinant preservation is an input.

Evaluation at \(s=Jx\) and \(f=x\) gives
\[
q_F=c_F\varepsilon\eta,
\qquad
q_S=c_S\varepsilon\eta.
\]

Therefore the normalization defect is exactly
\[
\boxed{\frac{\delta u_F}{u_S}=-\frac{c_F}{c_S}.}
\]

There are two useful consequences.

**Common normalization:** when \(c_F=c_S\),
\[
\delta u_F=-u_S.
\]
For \(\delta=-1\), this gives
\[
\boxed{u_F=u_S}
\]
in the fixed cofactor/orientation convention—not merely equality of valuations. A consistent change of orientation may alter a unit sign.

**Normalization up to an integral unit:** when
\[
c_F=\epsilon_Rc_S,\qquad \epsilon_R\in R^\times,
\]
the integral-unit criterion gives the required valuation preservation. The individual normalization factors need not be units.

That is the connecting theorem we were aiming for: **actual boundary–cup naturality plus compatible normalization implies the determinant comparison.**

It remains necessary to establish those inputs for the manuscript’s actual arithmetic construction. The adapter does not construct Poitou–Tate duality or certify H19–H22.

## 5. The ratio is not an artefact of choosing a complement

The ordinary derivation also proves complement independence.

Both cross-functionals vanish on \(v\), because the cycle equations place \(A_Fv\) and \(A_SJv\) in the common strict image, which the closed functionals annihilate.

Every other complement has the form
\[
x'=rx+sv,\qquad r\ne0.
\]
Then
\[
q_F'=rq_F,\qquad q_S'=rq_S,\qquad
\eta'=r\eta,\qquad \theta'=r^2\theta.
\]

Consequently, the coordinates and the ratio are unchanged.

**The defect belongs to the two induced functionals on the one-dimensional quotient \(F/Lv\), not to an arbitrary fractional-complement choice.** This quotient interpretation is supplied as an ordinary proof and tested exactly; it is not a separately completed Lean construction in this packet.
