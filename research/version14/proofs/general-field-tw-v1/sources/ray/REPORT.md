# Finite ray-class input for the auxiliary 2-extension

This is a new source/application proposal by the compiler agent, pending independent review. It supplies only the finiteness sentence in theta003, Section 6, line 90. It does not reapprove the Selmer calculation, selection of Q, exponent estimate, or the whole auxiliary-prime theorem.

Lagarias, *Ray class groups and ray class fields for orders of number fields*, Essential Number Theory **4** (2025), 1–65, DOI [10.2140/ent.2025.4.1](https://doi.org/10.2140/ent.2025.4.1). The final publisher PDF was obtained from <https://msp.org/ent/2025/4-1/ent-v4-n1-p01-s.pdf>. This is a publisher-formatted journal article, with the journal, year, volume, DOI and copyright on printed page 1. It is not an arXiv version or a claim of edition equivalence based on an author copy.

Theorem 6.9, printed pages 44–45, states that the ray class group attached to a level datum (O; m, Sigma) is finite. Here O is an order in a number field, m is a nonzero integral ideal, and Sigma is a subset of the real places. We use only the specialization O=O_F. Definitions 5.2–5.4 on printed page 33 identify this group with the fractional ideals prime to m, modulo principal ideals generated by elements congruent to 1 modulo m and positive at Sigma. For the maximal order all nonzero fractional ideals are invertible. The empty Sigma is expressly allowed. The empty Q below means m=O_F, also allowed.

The proof on pages 44–45 specializes the exact sequence of Theorem 6.5. Its finite residue-unit/sign term and finite ordinary class group imply ray-class finiteness. Thus the needed assertion is an explicit published theorem with a proof, rather than an inference that global reciprocity alone proves class-number finiteness. We retain the published theorem's ordinary class-number and exact-sequence inputs; we do not claim to have supplied new proofs of them. The cardinality formula and unit-rank assertion in Theorem 6.9 are not needed.

## Exact application to theta003

Use the fixed F, S and finite Q of theta003, lines 5–7. F is totally real, S includes all real places and all places above 2, and Q is disjoint from S. Let L/F be any finite subextension of the maximal abelian pro-2 extension unramified outside Q and split at S. Set m equal to the product of the distinct prime ideals in Q. Set Sigma empty.

At a prime v in Q, let ell_v be its odd residue characteristic and let p_v denote the maximal ideal of its local integer ring. The successive quotients of U_v^i=1+p_v^i, i>=1, are additive subquotients of the residue field. Completeness therefore makes U_v^1 a pro-ell_v group. Under local reciprocity its image in the finite 2-group Gal(L/F) is trivial. Thus local conductor exponent at v is at most 1. Outside Q inertia is trivial by definition, so local units act trivially. In particular no dyadic conductor occurs. Splitting at S also kills the full decomposition group at each dyadic place. At each real place splitting kills the sign contribution, so no infinite part is needed in the modulus. These statements apply separately to every finite L; their modulus m is the same.

For completeness, one can pass from these local kernel statements to a finite bound without assuming the existence theorem for ray class fields. The Artin map on fractional ideals prime to m sends a prime to its Frobenius in Gal(L/F). It is surjective by the already retained Chebotarev theorem. If alpha is congruent to 1 modulo m, all its local factors at Q act trivially by the preceding principal-unit calculation. The infinite factors act trivially because L splits at all real places. Global reciprocity for the principal idele alpha says that the product of the remaining local factors is trivial. At places outside Q these local factors are precisely the corresponding Frobenius powers. Consequently the principal ideal (alpha) is in the kernel. There is a surjection

    Cl_(m,empty)(O_F) -> Gal(L/F).

The choice of arithmetic or geometric Frobenius replaces this map by its inverse and has no effect on its kernel, surjectivity, or the finiteness assertion. Hence [L:F] is at most the finite order of Cl_(m,empty)(O_F), by published Theorem 6.9. Choose among the finite subextensions one of maximal degree. Its compositum with any other finite subextension is still an abelian 2-extension with the same ramification and splitting conditions. The same degree bound and maximality force that compositum to equal the chosen field. All finite subextensions are therefore contained in one finite extension. The maximal pro-2 extension and its group Theta_Q are finite.

This proves exactly the finiteness required before the invariant-factor argument on theta003 line 90. The finite bound may depend on F and Q; no uniform bound as Q varies is asserted. Imposing additional splitting conditions can only pass to a quotient and does not invalidate the argument.

## Related signed-modulus use

The same published Definitions 5.2–5.4 and Theorem 6.9 allow any Sigma of real places. They therefore supply a finite ray-group source for a separate argument that a given character factors through a finite modulus with signs. This packet does not assert that an arbitrary unitary Hecke character has finite order: a norm character can have infinite order.
