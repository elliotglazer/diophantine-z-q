The literal nice-prime definition asks about induction from a character valued in the residue fraction field. Geometric induction can instead become visible only after extension. The compact example in COEFFICIENT-CAUTION.md demonstrates that distinction: its pro-5 subgroup has no index-two quotient, the index-two inducing subgroup is unique, and the displayed matrix has nonsquare discriminant. This is an abstract illustration, with no claim of a Galois or Hecke realization.

For the actual p=5 setup an element outside the inducing subgroup has trace zero even after scalar extension. Reduction of the original lattice trace gives 1+bar-chi=0 on that coset, and multiplication by a fixed coset representative gives bar-chi=1 on the subgroup. This proves L=F(bar-chi) without assuming a K-valued inducing character. Every real place has complex conjugation outside G_L by oddness, so L is totally imaginary. Since 5 splits completely in F, its local completion at 5 is Q5. The Eisenstein cyclotomic polynomial at 5 gives total ramification of Q(zeta5) and its nontrivial subfields, so F intersects Q(zeta5) trivially. The unique quadratic subfield after adjoining zeta5 is the totally real F(sqrt5), different from L. This checks geometric disjointness for every point already in the stated Section 4 domain and imposes no new point-construction condition.

The induced lines and their character values are defined over a finite coefficient extension. Their reductions on G_L are trivial since both eigenvalues reduce to one, so the compact rotation image is pro-5. The other coset acts by inversion, and squaring on the abelian pro-5 subgroup is bijective. Thus its full image is the commutator subgroup and the only possible nontrivial abelian quotient of the representation image is quadratic. Any quadratic cyclotomic subextension lies in the first layer because the subsequent kernel is pro-5. This gives the direct product of the representation and cyclotomic images. A central cyclotomic element with nontrivial mod-5 character acts on the twisted adjoint by a scalar whose difference from one is a unit. Its commutation identity for cocycles gives a coboundary directly over the original lattice and also kills finite-level invariants.

The actual p53 consumer needs two element choices. Surjectivity of the cyclotomic kernel onto the representation image allows a nontrivial rotation whose fixed adjoint line is diagonal and a reflection whose fixed adjoint line is off-diagonal. Their eigenvalues are distinct, by pro-5 oddness in the first case and trace zero with determinant -1 in the second. This supplies the two constituents used in Pan's proof of 4.3.10. Coefficient extension preserves rank, the discriminants are already integral in the original coefficient ring, and finite generation plus equal full rank yields finite-length kernel and cokernel in the stated 4.3.8 transfer. The nongeometric part of 4.3.5 uses the separate invariant-free generic adjoint assertion and the fixed minor bound, which has the correct finite-level conclusion.
