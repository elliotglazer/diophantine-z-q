For h in the source's nontrivial coset, choose the eigenvalue beta reducing to1. The other is -beta^(-1). Since p is odd, the limit of h^(1+p^m) has eigenvalues beta,beta^(-1). Its discriminant is exactly the square of tr(rho(h)). Nonvanishing of that trace modulo T^M implies nonvanishing of the discriminant modulo T^(2M). The latter is a closed condition, since it is specified in a finite quotient. It therefore persists on the closure of phi(H_M). Each element of that closure has distinct eigenvalues. Its reduction lies in the residual unipotent p-group, so both eigenvalues reduce to1, in a splitting extension if needed. A finite-order principal unit in characteristic p is1: its prime-to-p torsion is excluded by reduction, and p-power torsion is excluded by the field having no nonzero nilpotents. Distinctness and determinant1 therefore force both eigenvalues to have infinite order. This verifies the note's closure assertion, including its use for tau in X_r.

The strengthened matrix choice is correct. The actual source requires b_tau*c_tau nonzero. If every matrix in the positive-measure X_r had bc=0, its intersections with the upper- and lower-triangular closed subgroups would cover X_r. One intersection has positive measure. The subgroup it generates preserves a line and has finite index, by the positive-measure subgroup argument in the compact group. Taking its normal core gives a finite-index normal subgroup preserving that line, contradicting the retained finite-index irreducibility input of6.4. Thus tau can be chosen with both entries nonzero. A single nonzero off-diagonal entry would not have sufficed for the subsequent two equations.

For any determinant-one matrix with distinct eigenvalues beta,beta^(-1), polynomial interpolation, or Cayley--Hamilton, writes its positive s-th power as u_s times the matrix plus a scalar matrix, where u_s=(beta^s-beta^(-s))/(beta-beta^(-1)). Thus both off-diagonal entries acquire the same factor. Infinite order makes u_s nonzero for every positive integer s, including those divisible by p. Hence both entries of tau^s are nonzero, as required in the source's6.16 on p97. The formula can be checked in a splitting field; its equality of matrices descends to the original coefficient field.

The uniformity claim is also correct. M and the closure of phi(H_M) are fixed by rho. One then chooses sigma_0, a positive-measure intersection X_r, tau with the two nonzero entries, and a positive-measure intersection Y_s using only those fixed group subsets. These choices do not depend on n or the cohomology class. The subgroup generated by Y_s and a finite-index normal core are fixed. The valuations used to clear the diagonal and off-diagonal denominators, and the invariant annihilator attached to that core, are consequently fixed. The changing infinitesimal conjugating matrices do not require these valuation exponents to vary. This is exactly the uniformity needed for the source's finite-level annihilator.
