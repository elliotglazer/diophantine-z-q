The trace pairing on sl2 is nondegenerate in odd characteristic. A proper invariant plane has an invariant orthogonal line. A nilpotent invariant line forces an invariant vector-space line; an invertible traceless matrix gives a quadratic character and, over its splitting coefficients, the two exchanged eigenlines. Absolute irreducibility follows from odd complex conjugation's distinct K-rational eigenlines. Thus the proposed replacement establishes the required adjoint irreducibility in the geometrically nondihedral branch, without claiming Pan's nice prime has SW99's local infinite-order ordinary quotient.

The subsequent proof uses the residual extension, finite odd determinant and pro-p image after killing the determinant. SW99's H-plus and H-prime projections are injective because a residual complex-conjugation ambiguity cannot fix the odd determinant. The irreducibility argument for a finite-index normal subgroup works over splitting coefficients: an infinite-order semisimple element leaves at most two invariant lines, and permutation by the ambient group would force its reducibility or geometric dihedrality. The same argument rules out an induced restriction by passing to a normal finite-index subgroup of its inducing subgroup. It supplies the adjoint irreducibility required in6.5, rather than conflating it with irreducibility of the original two-dimensional representation.

The compact primitive-vector argument gives a uniform lattice-spanning exponent for a fixed subgroup. In6.9 the positive-measure set, diagonal element and later intersections are selected using rho, not the cocycle or finite level. The final finite-index subgroup is therefore fixed for the required bound. Its invariant-module annihilator controls the inflation term. For the first term of6.12 only a fixed annihilator is needed; a general assertion about uniformly bounded cardinality of arbitrary cohomology groups is unnecessary.

One precision correction was requested. The p96 image requires b_tau*c_tau nonzero, whereas the proposal's prose only says a nonzero off-diagonal entry. If every element of the positive-measure intersection had bc=0, its upper- and lower-triangular subsets would cover that intersection. One subset has positive measure and generates a finite-index triangular subgroup, contradicting6.4. Both entries are needed in6.16.
