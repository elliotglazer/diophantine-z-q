## Identifying the quadratic field from the residual trace

Suppose r over an algebraic closure of K is induced from a character theta of G_L, where L/F is quadratic. For every g outside G_L, the induced matrix interchanges the two inducing lines, so tr(r(g))=0. Taking the trace of the given A-lattice and reducing gives

`1 + bar-chi(g) = 0`.

Thus bar-chi is -1 on the entire nontrivial coset. Fix one such g. For h in G_L, gh is in that same coset, so

`-1 = bar-chi(gh) = -bar-chi(h)`.

Therefore bar-chi is 1 on G_L and -1 outside it. In particular it is the quadratic character cutting out L, and L=F(bar-chi). This uses neither a K-valued theta nor any ordinary hypothesis at p. It also proves that no geometric dihedral branch exists if bar-chi is not quadratic.

Every complex conjugation c has bar-chi(c)=-1. Therefore none lies in G_L. Since F is totally real and L/F is quadratic, L is totally imaginary at each real place of F.

## The cyclotomic comparison at 5

The polynomial Phi_5(X)=X^4+X^3+X^2+X+1 has

`Phi_5(Y+1)=Y^4+5Y^3+10Y^2+10Y+5`.

It is Eisenstein at 5. Thus Q(zeta_5)/Q has degree 4 and is totally ramified at 5; its Galois group is cyclic, generated by zeta_5 mapped to zeta_5^2. Every nontrivial intermediate field is ramified at 5. Since 5 splits completely in F, no such intermediate field can be contained in F. Hence F intersects Q(zeta_5) in Q, and Gal(F(zeta_5)/F) is cyclic of order 4.

Writing t=zeta_5+zeta_5^{-1}, one has t^2+t-1=0 and (2t+1)^2=5. The unique quadratic intermediate field is therefore F(sqrt(5)), which is totally real. It cannot equal the totally imaginary quadratic field L found above. Because L/F has degree 2, it follows that

`L intersect F(zeta_5) = F`.

This proves the geometric nice-prime disjointness condition at p=5 for every representation in the stated Section 4 domain, whether its inducing character is initially K-valued or not. No exclusion of extra points and no new existence or dimension argument are needed.

The same real/imaginary argument is unavailable in this form when p is congruent to 3 modulo 4. No conclusion for that general case is claimed here. In particular this successor must not silently change a generic-prime source theorem to a stronger one.

## Finite coefficient extension and descent

The two inducing lines are defined over a finite extension K'/K. One can take an eigenbasis for a nonscalar element of r(G_L); its degree-two characteristic polynomial splits over a finite extension, and the two distinct eigenspaces are the inducing lines. Such an element exists, because otherwise G_L acts by scalars and the complex-conjugation eigenspaces give a reducible representation. The character values on the lines are then in K'. Let A' be the integral closure of A in K'. It is a finite free, faithfully flat complete discrete valuation ring over A. All coefficient enlargements here are proof devices; no new Hecke prime or number-field extension is chosen.

On G_L the determinant is 1, so the two characters are theta and theta^{-1}. Their reductions are both 1, because the original residual semisimplification restricts to 1 plus 1. Thus the compact image of theta is pro-5. Conjugation by a complex conjugation interchanges the characters and acts on this rotation subgroup by inversion. Squaring is an automorphism of a pro-5 abelian group, so commutators with that involution exhaust the rotation subgroup. Consequently the only possible nontrivial abelian quotient of im(r) is the quadratic one cutting out L.

Let E be the fixed field of ker(r). The extension E intersect F(zeta_(5^infinity)) is abelian over F and hence is either F or L by the preceding quotient calculation. Any quadratic subextension of F(zeta_(5^infinity))/F lies in F(zeta_5): the kernel above that first layer is pro-5 and has no quotient of order 2. The proved disjointness excludes L. Therefore

`E intersect F(zeta_(5^infinity)) = F`.

Both extensions are Galois, so Gamma=Gal(E F(zeta_(5^infinity))/F) is their direct product. Take a central element z of its cyclotomic factor with nontrivial mod-5 cyclotomic character omega(z). On M=ad^0(r)(1), the element acts by the scalar omega(z), and omega(z)-1 is an A-unit. If f is a continuous 1-cocycle, comparing f(zg) with f(gz) yields

`(omega(z)-1) f(g) = (g-1) f(z)`.

Thus f is a coboundary and H^1(Gamma,M)=0. This proof works on the original lattice, since the cyclotomic factor acts trivially on r there; diagonalizing r does not alter the action of z. Equivalently one may prove it after A' and descend: finite free coefficient base change commutes with the continuous cochain complex, and faithful flatness detects zero.

For the other uses of coefficient enlargement in 4.3.10, a finite A-module has finite length if its finite faithfully flat base change does. The rank of a map over K is unchanged by K'/K. Distinct eigenvalues can be checked after K', while their squared difference equals the discriminant `(tr r(sigma))^2-4 det r(sigma)` in K, and for integral r lies in A. No square root is treated as a new A-coordinate. These facts transport the finite kernel/cokernel assertions back to the original coefficient ring.

## The two places the connection is consumed

In the non-dihedral part of 4.3.11, use “geometrically non-dihedral” for the case distinction. The trace-pairing argument in the predecessor proposal then gives adjoint irreducibility without a local ordinary hypothesis. If the representation is instead geometrically dihedral but its character is not K-valued, it belongs to the dihedral argument above, not incorrectly to the adjoint-irreducible branch. Its H^1 is zero. This covers all cases at p=5 and resolves C1 in the finite-length transfer.

Pan 4.3.10 also needs a distinct-eigenvalue element in the cyclotomic kernel in its non-dihedral branch. This does not follow merely by citing the nice-prime condition about a dihedral case. The transferred Skinner–Wiles 6.3(i), as explained in the predecessor, supplies an infinite-order diagonalizable element in the image of G_(F') with F'=F(det(r),zeta_(p^infinity)). Lifting that element gives an element of G_(F(zeta_(p^infinity))). Its eigenvalues beta and beta^{-1} are distinct, since beta has infinite order. Thus the required element follows from that explicit retained prerequisite. The independent status of the predecessor's 6.1–6.3 transfer is not replaced by the p=5 quadratic-field calculation here.

The geometric condition is also consumed in 4.3.10 and its continuation on page 53. It cannot be omitted merely because H^1(Gamma,M)=0 has been proved. The two irreducible adjoint constituents there require different elements. The established disjointness makes G_(F(zeta_(5^infinity))) surject onto im(r). Hence one can choose an element in that group whose image is a nontrivial rotation, and another whose image is in the nontrivial quadratic coset. For the first, theta is different from 1 and has pro-5 image, so theta and theta^{-1} are distinct. Its fixed adjoint line is the quadratic constituent. For the second, the induced matrix has trace zero and determinant -1, and has distinct eigenvalues 1 and -1; its adjoint fixed line lies in the other constituent. These are the two choices used in Pan's proof. Existence of the first follows also because a trivial rotation image would make r reducible. A finite coefficient extension used to name these constituents does not change the group elements or the descended full-rank map.

The connected chain is therefore: residual trace and oddness identify L; complete splitting of 5 forces the quadratic cyclotomic subfield to be real; disjointness controls both the cohomology and the element selection. Proving only the cohomology part would leave a genuine later-use obligation.

There is an earlier related use in 4.3.5. In the geometric dihedral case the same central element kills invariants of M/T^n M for every n, so their lengths are zero. In the geometrically non-dihedral case M over K is irreducible of dimension 3 and has no invariants. Choose finitely many group elements for which the combined map x mapped to ((g_i-1)x)_i is injective over K. A nonzero maximal minor of this integral matrix shows that its kernel modulo T^n is killed by a fixed T-power; as a submodule of (A/T^n)^3 its length is uniformly bounded. Thus this repeated invariant-bound use is also justified. It is not an assertion that the invariants of every arbitrary representation vanish.

For the finite kernel/cokernel conclusion of 4.3.8, after the preceding 4.3.10 element choices the source's induction produces a map from H^1(G_(F,P),M) to the direct sum of its rank-one coinvariant quotients. Its scalar extension has full rank r, the K-dimension specified by the source, hence the original map has full rank r as well. Both sides are finitely generated A-modules with the same rank, so kernel and cokernel have finite length. The scalar extension does not alter r. Finitely many selected elements yield finitely many discriminant valuations, giving the fixed bound used for the selected point. This records the coefficient descent at the actual rank/length conclusion, not only at an individual matrix identity. The later global duality and prime-selection applications retain their own source obligations.
