# A uniformity detail in the unchanged Skinner–Wiles proof

This note supplements the predecessor's reading of final printed pages 95–97. Source reviewer `/root/astra_v14_source_review` requested the explicit explanation; `/root/astra_quartic_opt05_anchor` checked the displayed matrices and provides it here. It does not change the source or declare the rest of Pan proved.

For h in H^+ minus H', the eigenvalues in the source's basis are beta and -beta^{-1}. The limit phi(h)=lim h^(1+p^m) has eigenvalues beta and beta^{-1}. Therefore

`discriminant(phi(h)) = (beta-beta^{-1})^2 = (tr rho(h))^2`.

The defining condition for H_M is tr rho(h) nonzero modulo T^M. Consequently the discriminant of phi(h) is nonzero modulo T^(2M). This is a closed condition in the T-adic topology, so it persists on the closure Sbar_M. Every element of that closure has distinct eigenvalues. It lies in the pro-p subgroup H', so its eigenvalues reduce to 1. A finite-order element of 1 plus the maximal ideal of a characteristic-p complete discretely valued field is 1. Hence the two eigenvalues have infinite order. This applies to every tau chosen in X_r, since X_r is contained in Sbar_M.

The actual source condition on tau is b_tau c_tau nonzero, not merely b_tau nonzero. If no such tau occurred on the positive-measure set X_r, one of its upper- or lower-triangular parts would have positive measure. The subgroup generated by that part has finite index and preserves a line, contradicting the finite-index irreducibility input. Thus the required tau exists.

For a matrix with eigenvalues beta and beta^{-1}, its off-diagonal entries under a positive power s are multiplied by

`(beta^s-beta^{-s})/(beta-beta^{-1})`.

This factor is nonzero when beta has infinite order. Thus both off-diagonal entries of tau^s remain nonzero, as used on printed page 97. The positive-measure sets, sigma_0, r, tau and s are selected from the fixed representation, not from n or the cohomology class. The resulting finite-index subgroup and its invariant annihilator are fixed. This is the uniformity needed by Lemma 6.9.
