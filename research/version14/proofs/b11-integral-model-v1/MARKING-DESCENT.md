# Markings and descent for the B11 construction

Author proposal, 8 November 2026. This supplies the scheme-theoretic connections in the current manuscript lines 762--797. It does not prove the division-order lattice calculation, the absence of polarized automorphisms preserving the three-marking, or the initial Buzzard moduli theorem. Those are separate incoming assertions. No independent decision is claimed here.

## 1. Exact domain and incoming data

Put R = Z[1/30]. The incoming moduli construction gives a smooth projective R-scheme X_5 representing the rigid full-five moduli problem, with its universal abelian surface, its specified principal polarization and O-action, including all the determinant components required by that problem. Here rigid means that the moduli groupoid is represented by the scheme: an automorphism preserving a full-five object over any test scheme is the identity. This is an explicit initial-model input; mere representability of a coarse set of geometric isomorphism classes would not suffice. The initial V_1(5) theorem and the precise meaning of its marking are supplied separately by the direct Buzzard application. Throughout, morphisms of polarized abelian schemes preserve the identity section. For a general torsion statement below, A/S is an abelian scheme of relative dimension g and n is a positive integer invertible on S.

The separate division-order/Tate calculation says that every geometric fibre needed in the construction has A[3] isomorphic, as an O-module scheme, to the constant module O/3O. It is an input to surjectivity, not a consequence of finite etaleness. The separate automorphism calculation says that a geometric-fibre automorphism preserving the polarization, O-action and three-marking is the identity. Section 7 supplies the arbitrary-test-scheme consequence from this and the rigid full-five input. Thus the group G_5=(O/5O)^* has trivial geometric stabilizers when it changes only the five-marking. Section 4 passes from this geometric assertion to freeness as a group-scheme action.

The precise conclusion is that adding the three-marking produces a finite etale cover X_35 of X_5, and that Y=X_35/G_5 is a smooth projective R-scheme. The universal abelian scheme, the original principal polarization, O-action and three-marking descend to Y. The quotient represents the full three-marking problem with the five-marking forgotten. Consequently the auxiliary five-marking does not multiply the generic-fibre degree attributed to the three-marking problem.

## 2. Finite etale torsion

For an abelian variety over a field, multiplication by n is finite locally free of degree n^(2g), by Stacks 0BFG, and is etale when n is invertible, by 0BFH. Apply these statements to every geometric fibre of [n]:A -> A. Both copies of A are flat and locally of finite presentation over S. The fibrewise flatness criterion 039E therefore makes [n] flat. It is locally of finite presentation: a morphism between S-schemes of finite presentation, with target separated over S, has this property here by its graph and projection. The etale-fibre criterion 02GM now makes [n] etale.

Multiplication is proper, since its graph is closed in A x_S A and the second projection is proper. Its geometric fibres are finite, so it is quasi-finite. Thus it is finite by 02LS. Pulling [n] back along the zero section proves that A[n]/S is finite etale, locally of rank n^(2g). In the actual application this rank is 3^4, respectively 5^4. The argument applies to the residue characteristics remaining over R; it is not restricted to characteristic zero.

## 3. The O-linear marking sheaf

Stacks 03RV identifies finite locally constant sheaves of sets with finite etale schemes. The finite etale group A[n] is therefore locally a constant finite set in the etale topology. Refine such a cover until the addition table, the zero section, and the action of a finite Z-module generating set of O are constant as well. There are only finitely many functions between the finite sets involved. The sheaf

    Isom_O(M, A[n]), where M is the specified constant O/nO-module,

is on that cover the constant finite subset of all bijections satisfying these finitely many equations. The equations include preservation of addition and of the O generators; they then imply preservation of every O element. Consequently this Isom sheaf is finite locally constant and is represented by a finite etale S-scheme, again by 03RV. It is surjective exactly when every geometric fibre contains an isomorphism. This last condition is the separate module calculation in section 1. No assumption that every abelian surface has the required O-module torsion has been made.

Apply this to n=3 over X_5. The resulting X_35 -> X_5 is finite etale and surjective. A finite morphism is projective, by 0B3I. Since the terminal base R is affine, it is quasi-compact and quasi-separated. The composition theorem 29.44.14 in 01W7 therefore applies to X_35 -> X_5 -> Spec(R), and proves that X_35/R is projective. The unrestricted composition assertion is not being used. It is smooth because it is etale over the smooth X_5. In particular X_35 is regular and noetherian. These statements use the full incoming five-marking scheme, not an arbitrarily selected determinant component.

## 4. The quotient and its projectivity

First pass from the geometric automorphism input to scheme-theoretic freeness. For each nonidentity element g of the finite constant group G_5, the equalizer of its action on X_35 and the identity is a closed subscheme, because X_35/R is separated. A geometric point of this equalizer would give a nontrivial stabilizer, contradicting section 1. A nonempty scheme has a geometric point, so this equalizer is empty. The equalizer for the identity element is all of X_35. A section of the constant group over any test scheme T is locally a fixed element g. The preceding equalizer calculation therefore says that its stabilizer functor contains only the identity, also for nonreduced T. Equivalently G_5 x X_35 -> X_35 x X_35 is a monomorphism, which is the free-action hypothesis. This argument does not invert the group order.

Stacks 07S7 applies to the projective R-scheme X_35 and finite locally free constant group scheme G_5. It gives a scheme Y representing the fppf quotient and makes q:X_35 -> Y a G_5-torsor. Since G_5 is finite etale, trivializing the torsor fppf-locally and applying descent of finite and etale morphisms, 02LA and 02VN, shows that q is finite etale and surjective. This argument does not require |G_5| to be invertible on R.

Smoothness is smooth-local on the source, by 036U, so Y/R is smooth. In particular it is locally of finite presentation. The inverse image of its diagonal under the faithfully flat cover X_35 x_R X_35 -> Y x_R Y is X_35 x_Y X_35. This is the disjoint union of the graphs of the elements of G_5. Each graph is closed because X_35/R is separated. The diagonal is therefore a closed immersion by fpqc descent, 02L6. Thus Y/R is separated. The surjection q from the proper R-scheme X_35 makes Y universally closed over R, by 03GN; it also makes Y quasi-compact. Hence Y/R is proper.

Because R is affine and X_35 is projective over R, X_35 has an ample invertible sheaf. Stacks 0BD4 descends the existence of an ample invertible sheaf along the finite locally free surjection q. Its norm construction is used to prove projectivity of the base quotient only. Applying 0B45 to the proper R-scheme Y now proves Y/R projective. In particular 07S7 has not been credited with a projectivity conclusion it does not state.

## 5. Projectivity before duality, and effective abelian-scheme descent

There is no circular use of the projective-case Picard theorem. First use the independent regular-base statement explicitly invoked in BLR, Neron Models, section 8.4, proof of Corollary 6, printed page 234: an abelian scheme over the regular base in that proof is projective over that base. BLR names Murre [2], page 16, as its antecedent. Our bases are regular noetherian schemes of finite type over R. We use precisely this domain, not an assertion over every possibly nonnormal base. This regular-base projectivity assertion is the immediate published input; this packet does not independently reproduce Murre's proof.

The universal abelian scheme A_35/X_35 is therefore projective before any invocation of its dual or Poincare bundle. The relabeling action on its moduli problem gives descent isomorphisms over X_35 x_Y X_35, satisfying the cocycle identity. Stacks 0CCJ makes this projective-scheme descent datum effective, since q is finite locally free and surjective. Denote its descent by A/Y. Stacks 023Q gives full faithfulness for morphisms: the zero section, multiplication and inverse descend, and their identities hold because they hold after the faithfully flat cover q. Properness and smoothness descend by 02L1 and 02VL. Geometric connectedness of the fibres follows after passage to an algebraically closed field and a point of the nonempty finite etale fibre of q. Thus A/Y is an abelian scheme. Y is regular by section 4, so the same independent regular-base assertion makes A/Y projective.

## 6. Descent of the exact principal polarization and O-action

We may now invoke projective-case Picard theory. BLR section 8.2 Theorem 1, printed page 210, represents the relative Picard functor for a projective flat finitely presented family with reduced irreducible geometric fibres. Those hypotheses hold for A/Y and A_35/X_35. Their zero sections rigidify the line bundles. BLR section 8.2 Proposition 4, page 211, gives the normalized universal Poincare bundle; its prerequisite f_*O=O universally is stated for these hypotheses on the same page. BLR section 8.4 Theorem 5, page 234, identifies Pic^tau with the projective dual abelian scheme and identifies the double dual with the original scheme.

The fibrewise definition of Pic^0 and Pic^tau on printed page 233 commutes with base change. Together with the representing property this gives the canonical identification

    (A^vee) x_Y X_35 = (A_35)^vee.

Its canonical character makes it compatible with the descent isomorphisms. The incoming polarization lambda_35:A_35 -> (A_35)^vee is preserved by relabeling the five-marking. Full faithfulness, 023Q, therefore descends this particular morphism to lambda:A -> A^vee. Since lambda_35 is an isomorphism, lambda is an isomorphism by 02L4. Each geometric fibre is a polarization: after choosing a point of the finite etale cover its map is precisely the given polarization of the corresponding fibre of A_35. Thus lambda is the original principal polarization in descent. No norm multiple of lambda has been substituted, and its degree remains one.

For every a in O, its endomorphism of A_35 likewise descends by 023Q. The ring-action identities can be checked after q, so this is the same O-action on A. The three-marking descends as an O-linear isomorphism of finite etale group schemes, because it is unchanged by G_5. Its being an isomorphism is again fpqc-local. These assertions concern actual maps and their identities, not merely isogeny classes or cycle classes.

## 7. The forgotten marking and the degree

First verify rigidity on arbitrary test schemes. Let u be an automorphism of a full-three object over an R-scheme T. Its restriction to every geometric fibre is the identity by the separate geometric automorphism input. It therefore acts trivially on the finite etale group scheme A[5]. Indeed, after an etale trivialization, morphisms between finite etale schemes are locally constant maps of finite sets; equality at all geometric points implies equality, including when T is nonreduced. On the finite etale cover of T parametrizing five-markings, u consequently preserves the chosen full-five marking. The rigid full-five moduli input then makes u the identity on that cover. Equality of morphisms descends by023Q, so u is the identity over T. No theorem identifying geometric and infinitesimal automorphism groups has been assumed.

For any R-scheme T and an object of the specified full three-marking problem over T, the sheaf of compatible full five-markings is the finite etale Isom sheaf of section 3. It is locally nonempty by the incoming five-torsion marking condition and is a G_5-torsor: two such markings differ by a unique O-linear automorphism of O/5O. On this cover the object maps to X_35. The two pullbacks differ by the G_5 action, so the map descends to Y. Conversely a map T -> Y pulls back the descended universal object from sections 5--6. These two constructions are inverse after the faithfully flat marking cover and hence inverse on T. The arbitrary-test-scheme rigidity just established makes this an identification with the fine moduli functor rather than only with a coarse set of points.

This proves the precise generic-fibre identification claimed in the manuscript. The cover q is an intermediate marking cover; the final object is its quotient. Therefore the auxiliary full-five group does not enter the degree of the full-three moduli problem. The separate group-index argument supplies the numerical bound 4*81=324. This packet neither recomputes that index nor changes any later height constant.
