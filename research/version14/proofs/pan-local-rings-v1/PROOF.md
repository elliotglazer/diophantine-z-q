# Local matrix connections for Pan 4.2.10 at p=5

This is an authored supplement, not an independent verdict. Source and consumer pins are in SOURCES.json and LOCATIONS.json. The proof separates a source comparison from the additional algebra needed to use the source on the exact fixed-determinant lifting rings.

## 1. Objects and conventions

Let O be Pan's coefficient DVR, with fraction field E of characteristic zero, uniformizer lambda, and finite residue field k of characteristic p=5. Fix v in S minus Sigma_p, q=N(v), and d=chi(Frob_v) in O^*. Pan 4.1.1 gives q congruent to 1 modulo p and d congruent to 1 modulo lambda. Let xi be the specified p-power-order character of k(v)^*. For a tame pro-p inertia generator t, put alpha=xi(t), beta=alpha^-1. Thus alpha and beta are (q-1)st roots of unity congruent to 1 modulo lambda. If xi is nontrivial, alpha is nontrivial because t generates the relevant cyclic quotient; alpha and beta are distinct since an element of odd p-power order whose square is 1 must be 1.

Write M_xi for the affine O-scheme of pairs (Phi,Sigma), with Phi invertible, characteristic polynomial of Sigma equal to (X-alpha)(X-beta), and Phi Sigma Phi^-1=Sigma^q. Write A_xi for its determinant-d fiber, det(Phi)=d. At the residual pair specified by Pan, the completed local ring of A_xi is exactly R_v^{square,xi}. This is the representing-pair identification in Pan pp. 45–46; it retains the same framing. The inertia determinant is alpha beta=1. The source convention for reciprocity can replace both eigencharacters by their inverses, which interchanges this pair and leaves its characteristic polynomial unchanged. The Frobenius relation is the displayed arithmetic-Frobenius relation in both matrix descriptions.

## 2. The determinant connection

There is an exact finite etale cover

    A_xi × G_m  -> M_xi,    (Phi_0,Sigma,s) |-> (s Phi_0,Sigma).

Its coordinate description over M_xi adjoins s with s^2=det(Phi)/d. The inverse recovers Phi_0=s^-1 Phi. Both s and 2 are units, so the derivative 2s is a unit. The cover is finite free of degree two and surjective. This is not a global isomorphism M_xi=A_xi × A^1. In particular the unconstrained invertible determinant has not been replaced by an arbitrary affine-line coordinate.

At the residual pair with determinant d modulo lambda, choose the sheet s=1. For every Artin local O-algebra B in the lifting category and each Phi reducing to the prescribed residual matrix, det(Phi)/d lies in 1+m_B. There is a unique square root s in 1+m_B: lift the root 1 successively through the nilpotent powers of m_B using the invertible derivative 2, or apply the finite iterative correction to s^2-det(Phi)/d. These roots are functorial in B. Consequently the unrestricted completed lifting ring U_xi and the fixed-determinant ring satisfy

    U_xi ≅ R_v^{square,xi}[[z]],    s=1+z.

The same identification after completed tensor with S is U_xi completed-tensor S ≅ (R_v^{square,xi} completed-tensor S)[[z]]. This follows either on Artin quotients or by adjoining the same independent formal parameter before the inverse limit. In particular a connected generic fiber of the former implies connectedness of the latter: an idempotent in the latter would give a nontrivial idempotent in its formal-variable extension. An integral/irreducible formal-variable extension also forces the corresponding property for its coefficient ring, and dimension decreases by one. Generic regularity descends along this faithfully flat regular formal-variable map in the excellent rings in question. Equivalently one can use the finite-type etale cover above and the same completed local neighborhoods. These observations transfer the distinct-character connectedness, dimension and regularity assertions to the fixed determinant, but do not manufacture O-flatness from irreducibility.

For completeness, the finite-type trivial-character component statement also transfers, rather than only its completed version. Let B be the coordinate ring of M_1 and let B_red be its reduction. Taylor's final Lemma 3.2(1) says that the reduced components of B are O-flat of relative dimension four, with pairwise-distinct generically reduced special-fiber components. At each generic point of the special fiber of B_red, exactly one reduced component occurs, so its local ring modulo lambda is a field. Its local ring is a one-dimensional domain with maximal ideal generated by lambda, hence a DVR. Here the dimension is one because all components have relative dimension four and their special fibers have that same dimension.

Pass to the finite etale algebra C=B[s,s^-1]/(s^2-det(Phi)/d). Its reduction is etale over B_red and is A_1,red[s,s^-1]. Etale base change commutes with reduction here; the base change of the reduced ring is reduced and its kernel in C is nilpotent. Every component of C_red is O-flat, and finite etale dimension theory gives relative dimension four. At a prime minimal over lambda, contraction is minimal over lambda in B_red; its local ring is etale over the preceding DVR, thus regular of dimension one with maximal ideal generated by lambda. This proves that only one characteristic-zero component passes through that prime and that its special fiber is reduced there. A prime minimal over lambda on an individual component is also a generic point of the total special fiber: the component has dimension five, its special-fiber components have dimension four by the principal ideal theorem and the dimension formula for finite-type flat O-domains, and all total special-fiber components have dimension four. Thus this proves generic reducedness for each component's special fiber, as required, including possible intersections away from their generic points.

The components of A_1,red[s,s^-1] are precisely those of A_1,red with a Laurent variable adjoined. Their special-fiber generic points and unique-component incidence have the same correspondence. Removing that variable gives relative dimension three, hence total dimension four, and the componentwise O-flatness, generic reducedness and unique-component incidence asserted in Pan 4.2.10(1). Taking the reduction never changes the component quotient A_1/p. This transfer works for the chosen residual point whether or not its full unipotent residual pair is trivial. Taylor's finite-type lemma is global in the matrix scheme. Taylor 2.7 then provides the stated completed-local component comparison where needed.

## 3. Nilreduction and the exact downstream information

For a Noetherian ring R its nilradical I is nilpotent: I is finitely generated by nilpotent elements, and a sufficiently long product of generators contains a vanishing power of one generator. The quotient R -> R/I therefore gives a homeomorphism on prime spectra and the same minimal-prime incidence. After tensoring with another algebra the quotient still has kernel generated by I, which is nilpotent. In the Noetherian completed tensor products used here the same statement holds after completion: completion is exact on finite modules, and the quotient by the finitely generated ideal I commutes with completion. Localization and subsequent completion preserve the nilpotent-kernel assertion as well.

It follows that replacing the trivial-xi factors by reductions preserves the equidimensional component statement, characteristic of generic points, and the unique-minimal-prime-over-special-fiber relation in Pan 4.2.4(2). Component quotients are unchanged. Pan p. 47 performs precisely that reduction before its tensor calculation. The fact that the resulting reduced factors have suitable special-fiber generic points comes from the component statement of Section 2, not from invariance of scheme-theoretic generic reducedness under nilreduction.

The adverse example R=O[e]/(e^2) makes the distinction explicit. R has the same prime spectrum as O and is even O-flat, but R/lambda R has a nonzero nilpotent at its unique generic point. Its nilreduction O has reduced special fiber. Thus neither topology nor O-flatness permits replacing the corrected assertion by generic reducedness of the original special fiber.

## 4. Direct O-flatness of the nontrivial-xi, dimension-two ring

This section supplies the extra flatness conclusion not contained in the read statement of Thorne 3.15(2). It is independent of the generic-reducedness correction.

Assume alpha≠beta as in Section 1. Monic division of X^q-X by P(X)=(X-alpha)(X-beta) has remainder aX+b. Both alpha and beta are roots of X^q-X. Hence a(alpha-beta)=0. O is a domain and alpha≠beta, so a=b=0. Cayley–Hamilton now gives Sigma^q=Sigma on the entire matrix scheme, even over nonreduced O-algebras. Thus the matrix relation is exactly Phi Sigma=Sigma Phi. It has not been deduced by inverting alpha-beta, which would lose the integral assertion.

Set h=(alpha+beta)/2 and delta=(alpha-beta)/2. Since 2 is a unit, every pair with the specified traces can be written uniquely as

    Sigma = h I + [[x,y],[z,-x]],
    Phi   = w I + [[a,b],[c,-a]].

The condition on det(Sigma) is x^2+yz=delta^2, since h^2-delta^2=alpha beta=1. Commutation gives the three equations

    yc-zb=0,   xb-ya=0,   za-xc=0.

They are precisely the 2×2 minors of the matrix with rows (x,y,z) and (a,b,c). The fixed determinant is w^2-a^2-bc=d. Because d is a unit, this last equation already makes Phi invertible. No additional localization changes the ring.

Let D=O[x,y,z,a,b,c]/(xb-ya,xc-za,yc-zb). We record an elementary basis argument. Denote its six variables by X_i,Y_i for 1≤i≤3, and map X_i to u v_i and Y_i to t v_i in O[u,t,v_1,v_2,v_3]. Use a minor relation to exchange X_j Y_i for X_i Y_j whenever i<j. Repetition terminates because it decreases the sum of column indices assigned to X. For a monomial with r X-factors and total column multiset J, the final monomial assigns the r smallest elements of J to X and the rest to Y, counting multiplicities. Hence these ordered monomials span D. Their images are the distinct monomials u^r t^s product(v_i^{m_i}), so they are O-linearly independent. This proves that D is O-free, that its displayed map into a polynomial domain is injective, and that the identical basis proof holds over k. In particular D/lambda D is a domain.

Let f=x^2+yz-delta^2. Since alpha,beta are congruent to 1, delta is in lambda O. The reduction f_bar=x^2+yz is nonzero in D/lambda D; its image is u^2(v_1^2+v_2 v_3), a nonzero polynomial also in characteristic five. It is therefore a non-zero-divisor. The quotient D/(f) is O-torsion-free. Indeed, if lambda g=fh in D, reduction modulo lambda gives f_bar h_bar=0, hence h=lambda h_1. Cancellation of lambda in the O-free module D then gives g=fh_1. Iterating proves absence of any lambda-power torsion. Over the DVR O, torsion-free modules are flat.

Finally,

    A_xi = (D/(f))[w]/(w^2-a^2-bc-d).

This is free of rank two over D/(f), with basis 1,w, because the polynomial is monic. Thus A_xi is O-flat. Localization at the residual pair remains flat, and its Noetherian maximal-ideal completion is flat over the localization. The composed map O -> R_v^{square,xi} is flat. This proves flatness of the actual fixed-determinant lifting ring, not merely its reduction. The same calculation can be made without fixing det(Phi), by adjoining w and inverting det(Phi); it too is O-flat.

The only use of nontrivial xi in this calculation is the divisibility P(X)|(X^q-X). If alpha=beta=1 it need not hold, since (X-1)^2 need not divide X^q-X in characteristic zero. Consequently this proof has not silently imposed the commuting relation in the trivial-xi branch. The odd-prime assumption is needed for the trace decomposition and determinant square-root comparison; no assertion at p=2 is made.

## 5. Exact scope of the assembled connection

For nontrivial xi and trivial residual local representation, Thorne's read 3.15(2)/3.16 give the generic-fiber and component inputs. Section 2 transfers their fixed-determinant form and Section 4 supplies the otherwise unsupported O-flatness assertion. Pan's own regular-morphism argument on p. 46 then supplies normality after the stated S base changes. Its use of excellent-ring completion and its completed-tensor-product lemmas remains an inherited input; this packet is not a replacement proof of those foundations.

For trivial xi, Taylor's final component statement, Section 2, and Pan's reduction in Section 3 supply the corrected type of information required by the component calculation. The published Allen–Newton–Thorne correction agrees with this distinction. It is unnecessary and incorrect to assert generic reducedness of the unreduced full special fiber.
