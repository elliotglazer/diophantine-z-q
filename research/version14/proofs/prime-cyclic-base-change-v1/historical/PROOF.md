# The automorphic part of H-D4

Author: /root/astra_v14_baseline_theta. This is a new connecting proof submitted for independent review. It neither changes the manuscript nor approves its own imported theorems.

## 1. Exact assertion and retained inputs

Let F be totally real, let l be a rational prime (including 2), and let r:G_F -> GL_2(E) be continuous, with E a finite extension of Q_l and a stable lattice whose residual image is nonsolvable. Let M/F be a finite solvable Galois totally real extension. Assume r restricted to G_M is attached to a cuspidal Hilbert automorphic representation of parallel weight two. Then r itself is attached to a cuspidal Hilbert automorphic representation of parallel weight two. A finite coefficient extension is allowed. Conversely, if r is so attached over F, its restriction is so attached over each intermediate field of M/F.

Here "attached" means equality of characteristic polynomials at all but finitely many finite primes, in the arithmetic-Frobenius convention specified in Section 2. It does not mean that equality of just projective representations, determinants, or traces at split primes is sufficient. The proof below obtains an actual isomorphism of two-dimensional l-adic representations over the field in question.

The automorphic input is Langlands, Base Change for GL(2), Annals of Mathematics Studies 96 (1980): cyclic prime-degree base change, invariant cuspidal descent, and strong multiplicity one. The inspected source is the IAS retypeset text. Its front matter says it appeared originally as Study 96; that is not a line-by-line authentication of the original edition. Exact statements used are Chapter 2(A,B,F), Lemma 11.3(b), Proposition 11.4, Lemma 11.6(b), and Lemma 3.1. Chapter 2(C,E) records the fibre and central-character descriptions; the representation-theoretic ambiguity actually needed here is proved in Section 5, not assumed from a slogan about descent.

Other retained inputs are: existence of the l-adic representation of a parallel-weight-two cuspidal Hilbert eigenform over each totally real intermediate field, with unramified Hecke compatibility; global class field theory for finite-order characters; and Chebotarev density with the characteristic-zero Brauer-Nesbitt theorem. These are not new consequences of the field-construction packet. The Galois attachment is the Taylor/Carayol input already used in H-D5 (final two-adic Kisin 3.2.3, and final finite-flat Kisin 3.4.1). Its full Hilbert-form scope, rather than only the displayed definite quaternionic special case, is required here. No modularity-lifting theorem is used to prove this descent step.

## 2. Normalizations

Fix compatible algebraic coefficient embeddings into C and Q_l-bar. For a weight-two form write the arithmetic Hecke polynomial at an unramified v as X^2-a_v X+q_v s_v, with q_v the residue cardinality. The attached r_pi has this polynomial on arithmetic Frobenius. The unitary automorphic Satake eigenvalues are the two roots divided by q_v^(1/2). This convention is equivalent to specifying the arithmetic Hecke normalization, without using a geometric-Frobenius dual by accident.

At an unramified extension of residue degree f, unitary Satake eigenvalues are raised to their f-th powers. Since q_w=q_v^f, the arithmetic roots are also raised to their f-th powers. Thus unramified base change has exactly the same characteristic polynomial as restriction of r_pi. At a split place the local representation is repeated without change, as in Langlands Chapter 1, p.2. These two assertions alone suffice for the global identification arguments below; all finitely many ramified places can be excluded from Chebotarev comparisons.

Use arithmetic global reciprocity for finite characters: the Hecke character corresponding to chi:G_K -> Q_l-bar^* is chosen to take a uniformizer at an unramified v to chi(Frob_v). Twisting an automorphic representation means twisting by this character composed with determinant. Its arithmetic roots are multiplied by chi(Frob_v), so its attached representation is r_pi tensor chi. This fixes the direction of the twist in Section 5. No square root of a determinant character is taken.

The covariant Tate convention, when invoked for an abelian variety, gives the cyclotomic character Hodge-Tate weight +1, as in the KW/H source. The manuscript's opposite Hodge-Tate sign convention gives weight -1. The representation and arithmetic Frobenius do not change in this proof; only the naming convention for Hodge-Tate weights differs. All forms here have the same parallel-weight-two infinity type.

## 3. Residual nonsolvability and towers

Put G=bar-r(G_F) and N=bar-r(G_M). Then N is normal in G and G/N is a quotient of Gal(M/F), hence solvable. If N were solvable, G would be solvable. Thus N is nonsolvable. For every intermediate K, bar-r(G_K) contains N, and is nonsolvable too. A finite subgroup of GL_2 over an algebraic closure which preserves a line is triangular and solvable. Consequently every residual restriction here is absolutely irreducible.

Every characteristic-zero restriction r|G_K is absolutely irreducible as well. Indeed a characteristic-zero invariant line is defined over some finite coefficient extension. Intersect it with the extended stable lattice and take the saturated rank-one intersection. Its reduction is a nonzero proper invariant line in the reduced two-dimensional representation, a contradiction. This argument also applies to any representation isomorphic to the restriction, independent of the lattice chosen initially.

A solvable finite Galois group has a subnormal series with cyclic prime-order factors. The corresponding fields give F=F_0 < ... < F_t=M with F_(i+1)/F_i cyclic of prime degree. Every F_i is totally real. If the original construction supplies only a tower of cyclic extensions, use its solvable totally real normal closure, whose existence and preservation of prescribed local completions are proved in the separately reviewed Sorensen field packet. This is not an assertion that each intermediate field is normal over F.

## 4. Ascent stays cuspidal

Suppose r|G_K is attached to a weight-two cuspidal pi and L/K is one of these cyclic prime-degree steps. Langlands Lemma 11.3(b) and Proposition 11.4 give a cuspidal base change unless the step is quadratic and pi is induced from an idele-class character of this same L. We exclude precisely that exception, rather than assuming base change of every cuspidal representation is cuspidal.

In the exceptional case the induced two-dimensional Weil representation has a self-twist by the nontrivial quadratic character eta of Gal(L/K): in a coset basis the intertwiner is diag(1,-1). Hence pi and pi tensor eta have equal unramified Hecke polynomials. The attachment and Chebotarev/Brauer-Nesbitt imply r|G_K is isomorphic to r|G_K tensor eta. Let A be an invertible intertwiner. Then A r(g)=eta(g) r(g) A. Its square commutes with r, so absolute irreducibility makes A^2 scalar. After extending coefficients and scaling A, A^2=1. A cannot be scalar since eta is nontrivial. Thus A has two one-dimensional eigenspaces of eigenvalues +1,-1, both stable under G_L. This contradicts Section 3. No appeal to the separate, omitted proof of Langlands Lemma 11.7 is needed for this exclusion.

The resulting cuspidal base change Pi has weight two at every real place: L and K are totally real, so at infinity every extension is the split real algebra and the local components are copies of those of pi. At almost every finite place the calculation of Section 2 shows r_Pi and r|G_L have equal characteristic polynomials. Their semisimplifications agree by Chebotarev/Brauer-Nesbitt. Since the latter is irreducible of dimension two, r_Pi is itself irreducible and isomorphic to it. This proves ascent, including the base change from an initially nonnormal field to a normal closure. It uses the Hilbert-form Galois attachment input stated in Section 1.

## 5. One cyclic descent and its exact twist

Suppose now Pi over L is cuspidal of weight two and r_Pi is isomorphic to r|G_L. For a generator sigma of Gal(L/K), conjugation on G_L by a lift of sigma carries r|G_L to an isomorphic representation, because r already exists on G_K. At almost all finite places this implies equality of the Hecke polynomials of Pi and Pi^sigma. Strong multiplicity one (Langlands Lemma 3.1) gives Pi^sigma isomorphic to Pi. This proves the invariance hypothesis, rather than presuming it from potential modularity.

Langlands Lemma 11.6(b) supplies a cuspidal descendant pi over K whose base change is Pi. The theorem explicitly gives cuspidal descendants; it is not enough to quote only local invariant descent. At real places the extension is split, so pi has the required weight-two component. Its central character has finite order: the weight-two unitary infinity type is trivial on the real connected multiplicative groups, and the finite character is smooth with an open conductor; after quotienting by that conductor and the infinity components it factors through a finite ray class group. Thus this is in the algebraic Hilbert-form attachment scope of Section 1.

Attach R=r_pi. Section 2 and Chebotarev give (R|G_L)^ss isomorphic to r|G_L. As the latter is irreducible and both have dimension two, R|G_L is already irreducible and isomorphic to r|G_L. Choose a single intertwiner and use it to identify the two restrictions. For g in G_K, the matrix C(g)=R(g)r(g)^(-1) commutes with this common restriction: conjugating any h in G_L by g stays in G_L and both representations implement the same conjugation. Schur's lemma makes C(g) scalar. The multiplication law now gives C(gg')=C(g)C(g'), and C(h)=1 for h in G_L. We have R=r tensor chi for a character chi of Gal(L/K), with finite order dividing [L:K].

Let chi-hat be its Hecke character under the convention of Section 2. Replace pi by pi tensor chi-hat^(-1). Its attached representation is exactly r|G_K. Its base change is still Pi since chi|G_L=1. Its weight is unchanged: the decomposition group at every real place is trivial in Gal(L/K), so chi is trivial there. Cuspidality is preserved by twisting. This proves descent without assuming a predetermined descendant already has the target determinant, and without equating representations merely from their equality on a finite-index subgroup.

Iterating this one-step argument from M down to F proves Section 1. At every stage the representation to which one descends is the already given restriction of r, so the invariance and absolute-irreducibility arguments repeat. The finite corrective characters need not be combined into one global character of Gal(M/F), which may be nonabelian.

## 6. Exact local scope in the H construction

The field-existence proof uses local monodromy/type inputs to choose its extensions. The automorphic passage on unramified Hecke parameters is established above. For full local comparisons retain the actual H-D5 theorem identifying the relevant local Galois/Weil-Deligne parameter with the Hilbert form and its local base change. Langlands Chapter 2(e) in the inspected text states the Weil-parameter comparison for reducible or dihedral parameters, not all arbitrary ramified p-adic representations. It must not be advertised as a complete modern local-global theorem.

For principal-series factors the local characters pull back by Norm, exactly Chapter 2(i). On Galois characters this is restriction by local reciprocity. Killing both finite inertial characters therefore makes that local factor unramified. Zero monodromy by itself is insufficient. For a special (Steinberg) factor the retained local comparison has nonzero rank-one monodromy; after restriction to a finite local extension its nonzero monodromy is multiplied by a nonzero integer under the usual tame-generator conventions and remains nonzero. It does not become unramified merely because the finite inertial character was killed. At a place kept split, the local representation does not change at all.

At places above 2, statements about actual Barsotti-Tate status, potential ordinarity, and type changes remain the local p-adic Hodge and H-D5 inputs already recorded. Once the local field has been enlarged to attain BT, further restriction preserves it. Our descent theorem is not a claim that BT descends through every ramified extension; after global identification with the original r, the local properties asserted over the original field are exactly the properties that r had there (potential BT in the initial H application).

For H003 Section 6 step 3, preserve the prescribed split Q2 places until the ordinary lemma is applied. For step 4, ascent transports the modular comparison form to the field where both target and comparison become BT. For step 5, it transports the comparison form through the cleaning/determinant/quadratic-character fields. Nonsolvability makes these ascents cuspidal. This treats both original H consumers in KW I Section 9, identified separately in H003 Section 2.
