# From the modular Jacobian to the classical tower

This connection concerns the compact curve, its Jacobian, the open modular curve and the rational pullbacks between the fixed finite levels. The modular-curve tower and the separate classical constituent decomposition are retained inputs. The argument gives a Galois-equivariant injection and uses no Hecke-stability assumption on the abelian factor.

## Curve and Jacobian

Let X be the smooth projective curve over Q at the initial level and let J be its Jacobian. Work on geometric fibers with ell=5. The Kummer calculation identifies H^1(X_bar,mu_(ell^n)) with Pic^0(X_bar)[ell^n], naturally in X and the ground field. Taking inverse limits identifies H^1(X_bar,Z_ell(1)) with T_ell J as G_Q-modules. This is the degree-one part of the curve Kummer statement. [Stacks Project, Lemma 59.69.1](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/03RQ)

Choose a geometric point of X for the theta construction. Milne's Jacobian autoduality theorem gives the isomorphism lambda from J_bar to its dual. Changing the chosen point translates the theta divisor, and the associated homomorphism is invariant under translation, as used in the proof of that theorem. Thus every Galois conjugate of lambda is lambda itself. The morphism, and its inverse, descend to Q. This argument needs a geometric point and imposes no rational cusp hypothesis. [Milne, Jacobian Varieties, Theorem 6.6 and proof, printed pp. 186–187](https://www.jmilne.org/math/articles/1986c.pdf)

The canonical duality of the integral Tate modules of an abelian variety and its dual gives T_ell(J^dual)=Hom(T_ell J,Z_ell(1)), equivariantly. The pairings at the finite torsion levels are compatible with the transition maps. Composing with lambda gives this same module from T_ell J. Milne's canonical H^1 identification on the abelian variety J gives Hom(T_ell J,Z_ell)=H^1(J_bar,Z_ell), with the Galois action described in Remark 15.5. Combining these identifications with the curve calculation and untwisting both sides gives a G_Q-equivariant isomorphism H^1(X_bar,Z_ell)=H^1(J_bar,Z_ell). [Milne, Abelian Varieties, Theorem 15.1(a), Remark 15.5 and Section 16](https://www.jmilne.org/math/articles/1986b.pdf)

The separately supplied rational isogeny decomposition of J with factor Y0 gives an inclusion of H^1(Y0_bar,Q_ell) as a Galois direct summand. Indeed, isogenies induce isomorphisms on rational Tate modules, and the preceding degree-one duality identifies the corresponding summands in cohomology. No choice of Hecke action on Y0 enters this inclusion.

## The finite cusp complement

Write U for the open modular curve and Z for the reduced finite complement of cusps in X. Over Qbar, let j and i denote the open and closed inclusions and put Lambda_n=Z/ell^n. The extension-by-zero exact sequence on X_bar has terms j_!Lambda_n, Lambda_n, and i_*Lambda_n. In the proper compactification, H^1_c(U_bar,Lambda_n) is H^1(X_bar,j_!Lambda_n). The group H^1(X_bar,i_*Lambda_n) is zero: on a finite set of geometric points the higher cohomology vanishes, and the closed-immersion pushforward is exact by its stalks and preserves injectives by the exact inverse-image adjoint. Thus compactly supported H^1 surjects onto H^1(X_bar,Lambda_n). [Stacks Project, Lemma 59.70.8](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/095L)

The restriction from H^1(X_bar,Lambda_n) to H^1(U_bar,Lambda_n) is injective. A class that restricts to zero corresponds to a finite etale Lambda_n-torsor with a section on U_bar. On each smooth connected component of X_bar, the component of the covering containing that sheet has generic degree one. Indeed the covering is normal, and the intersection of one integral component with the dense open is irreducible and lies in the one trivial sheet. The component is finite and birational over the normal curve, hence an isomorphism. The section extends, so the original torsor class is zero. [Stacks Project, Lemma 21.4.3 and the finite etale torsor identification in the proof of Lemma 59.80.7](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/09ZB)

The groups H^1(X_bar,Lambda_n) are finite by the curve calculation. The exact sequence also shows that H^1_c(U_bar,Lambda_n) is finite, using H^0 of the finite cusp set. The inverse limit preserves these surjections because compatible finite-level fibers are finite nonempty sets and finite initial lifts can be chosen from a sufficiently high level. The usual degree-one ell-adic realizations agree with these limits, since the degree-zero groups are finite. Tensor with Q_ell. It follows that the image of compactly supported in ordinary H^1 of the open curve is exactly the injected H^1 of its compactification. The argument is componentwise and the maps are canonical for the Q-model and its cusp set.

## Rational pullback to finer levels

Let pi be one of the finite dominant maps of the smooth compactified levels. It is finite locally free on each target component. Over the geometric field this follows over the local discrete valuation rings of the smooth target from finite torsion-free modules being free. Let d be the positive degree on the component. The norm for the finite locally free map induces a homomorphism on Picard groups whose composition with pullback is multiplication by d. The norm is canonical and compatible with field extension. These statements apply to compactified maps with ramification as well. [Stacks Project, Lemma 31.18.6](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BD2) and [Lemma 31.18.2](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BCY)

The norm and pullback preserve ell-power torsion. By the natural curve Kummer identification they give on H^1 with the cyclotomic twist a composite equal to d. Pass through the inverse limit, untwist, and tensor with Q_ell. Multiplication by d is invertible here even when ell divides d. Thus the pullbacks on rational H^1 of the compactifications are injective. Via the natural identification just proved, they give the injective transitions for the parabolic images in the open tower. The base and fine levels are the actual ones in the classical modular-curve system, with the tame level fixed and a sufficiently small level at ell.

In particular the actual finite-dimensional H^1 of the Q-isogeny factor Y0 embeds in the classical parabolic cohomology at a finite level and then in the corresponding direct limit. This supplies the input to the separate classical decomposition theorem. Its cohomological representation is dualized when passed to the Tate representation. The temporary Tate twist in the curve Kummer calculation has already been canceled on both sides of the curve/Jacobian comparison.
