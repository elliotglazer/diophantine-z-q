# Savitt 6.12 correction and the actual RETURN weight calculation

Author proposal, pending independent review. This packet compares the corrected Savitt author v3 with the final published Gee--Savitt Appendix A. It does not identify v3 with the final Duke 2005 article. The elementary application below is conditional on the explicitly selected Savitt 2.17/2.20, 6.9 and 6.11 inputs. It does not prove the construction of the RETURN compatible system or its local type.

## 1. Exact consuming assertion

Khare's author Proposition 3.1(ii), pp.16--19, starts with a minimal lift from Proposition 2.2 and nontrivial nebentype at q. It asserts a member V at q with Hodge--Tate weights {0,1}, potentially Barsotti--Tate, and inertial Weil--Deligne parameter (1 plus omega_tilde^j, N=0), 1<=j<=q-2. Its weight conclusion, when the residual global representation is nonsolvable, is weight j+2, or weight q+1-j after the residual twist chi_q^(-j).

The detailed proof on p.19 invokes Breuil 6.1.1 and Savitt 6.11 after establishing that precise principal-series parameter. The later example on p.24 cites both Savitt 6.11 and 6.12. Both citations are recorded; the broad later citation is not silently deleted. The argument below supplies a narrower route for the actual even-j RETURN cases. Its input is the local parameter itself, not merely the assertion that V becomes Barsotti--Tate over some unspecified extension.

## 2. What the published correction actually changes

Gee--Savitt, Compositio 147 (2011), Appendix A, pp.1082--1084, corrects Savitt 6.12(4). In that theorem the characteristic-zero inertial type is omega_tilde_2^m plus omega_tilde_2^(pm), with p+1 not dividing m. Write m=i+(p+1)a with 1<=i<=p. This a is not the RETURN exponent j.

When i=1 and val_p(b)>0, the two claimed residual inertia characters have equal niveau-one restrictions: omega_2^(m+p)=omega_2^(pm+1)=omega^(1+a). The original lattice actually has split reduction lambda_(c^-1) omega^(1+a) plus lambda_(-c^-1) omega^(1+a), for c^2=bar w. This is a genuine correction to that lattice calculation. It is not justified to describe every reduction in 6.12(4) as irreducible.

After extending the coefficient field to contain a square root of w, the appendix constructs a different lattice using X from Lemma A.6.7(2), g1=e1+X/(p*w)*u^(p*(p-1))*e2 and g2=e2. Proposition A.6.10 checks strong divisibility. The new 6.12(5) gives a nonsplit extension with upper character lambda_(-c^-1) omega^(1+a) and lower character lambda_(c^-1) omega^(1+a). Thus (4) is restricted to i>1 and (5) handles i=1. The reduction, and not the characteristic-zero representation, has changed with the lattice.

The related-use sweep is finite and explicit: 6.12(1)--(3) remain the unit-b branches, including the i=1 and i=p equal-inertia cases; (4) loses i=1; (5) is added. Corollary 6.15(2) must invoke (5) for that case and use the two choices of the square root. Proposition 6.21 gains the O_E[[B]] family, and 6.24 gains the first-order surjectivity calculation. The appendix also corrects phi_1(u^e2) to phi_1(u^e2*g2) and lambda_c to lambda_(c^-1) in the old proof. This packet reads these changes but does not claim an independent reconstruction of all deformation-ring arguments in 6.21/6.24.

## 3. Why the RETURN type does not enter that corrected family

Fix an odd prime P, a finite coefficient extension E/Q_P and a two-dimensional potentially crystalline E-representation V with weights {0,1} and type 1 plus omega_tilde^j, with j even and 2<=j<=P-3. Fix its actual stable lattice T and residual global representation, when present. Extend coefficients only to split the finite characters and make the cited calculations available; compute inertia and weights over an algebraic closure of the residue field.

Both characters in this characteristic-zero type are niveau one: their exponents as powers of omega_tilde_2 are divisible by P+1. In the 6.12 family neither exponent is divisible by P+1, since m is not divisible by P+1 and P is invertible modulo P+1. Therefore the two types cannot be isomorphic. The fact that an exceptional 6.12 residual representation has niveau-one inertia does not identify its characteristic-zero type with the RETURN type. This is the required object comparison.

For the RETURN type, Savitt 2.17/2.20 gives, in the indecomposable case, phi eigenvalues x1,x2 with nonnegative valuations summing to 1, descent characters 1 and omega_tilde^j, and filtration line generated by pi^j e1+e2. The source convention is D_st,2(V)=D_st(V(-1)), covariant; lambda_a sends arithmetic Frobenius to a. Put x1*x2=P*w with w a unit.

If both slopes are strictly between 0 and 1, 6.9 and 6.11(3) give a lattice with residual inertia omega_2^(1+j) plus omega_2^(P*(1+j)). Since 3<=1+j<=P-2, these are distinct niveau-two characters. Frobenius interchanges their eigenspaces, so the residual local representation is absolutely irreducible. Independence of the semisimplified residual representation from the stable lattice then gives the same result for T. In particular its inertia representation is the displayed sum. No trivial-endomorphism hypothesis on the initially supplied local reduction is inserted: this is the semisimplification argument also distinguished in Remark 6.17.

If x1 is a unit, the slope-zero line E e1 is weakly admissible and gives an actual subrepresentation. In the stated covariant convention the exact sequence is

    0 -> epsilon*lambda_(x1^-1) -> V
      -> omega_tilde^j*lambda_(x1/w) -> 0.

If x2 is a unit, it is

    0 -> epsilon*omega_tilde^j*lambda_(x2^-1) -> V
      -> lambda_(x2/w) -> 0.

These statements follow from the filtration, descent characters and rank-one identification, not from permuting residual semisimplification factors. Intersect the actual lattice T with the characteristic-zero line; the intersection is saturated, so reduction preserves a one-dimensional quotient and the same orientation. Its unramified characters may change notation after reduction, but its inertia characters do not. If V is decomposable, its two rank-one summands have weights 0 and 1; the same two alternatives follow from their finite inertia characters. The exceptional endpoint lattices excluded in the construction of 6.9 are therefore not needed for this endpoint argument.

## 4. Exact weight conversion

Use Serre's final Duke 54 (1987), pp.183--187, definitions 2.2--2.4. This is the definition of the Serre weight of a local restriction of an odd irreducible residual global representation, not a theorem asserting that the representation is modular.

In the interior-slope case the exponents of the fundamental characters have digits (0,1+j). Serre 2.2.4 gives k=1+(1+j)=j+2.

In the x2-unit case the inertia extension has upper omega^(1+j) and lower 1. If wild inertia acts nontrivially, the ordered exponents of Serre 2.4 are alpha=0 for the quotient and beta=1+j for the line. Here beta is between 3 and P-2, so beta is not alpha+1. Formula 2.4.5 gives k=j+2. If wild inertia is trivial, 2.3.2 gives the same answer from exponents 0 and 1+j. Thus a split or nonsplit local reduction causes no ambiguity in this range.

In the x1-unit case twist the entire residual representation by chi_P^(-j). Its quotient inertia becomes 1 and its line inertia becomes omega^(1-j)=omega^(P-j). Now alpha=0 and beta=P-j, again between 3 and P-2. The same two Serre formulas give k=P+1-j. This is an actual global cyclotomic twist when the global representation is being considered. It changes the determinant by chi_P^(-2j); it is not an exchange of subobject and quotient. Nonsolvability is preserved by this scalar twist because the projective image is unchanged.

This proves the desired alternative for every even j in 2<=j<=P-3, conditional on the stated local type and Savitt classification inputs. No Breuil 6.1.1 ordinary-converse theorem is needed for this replacement weight calculation. Odd j endpoints, scalar type, an arbitrary niveau-two type, and whole-paper claims about 6.12 are outside this replacement.

## 5. Actual RETURN parameters and retained interfaces

The finite RETURN examples give the following unordered pairs: P=7,j=2 or4 gives {4,6}; P=11,j=4 or6 gives {6,8}; P=19,j=8 or10 gives {10,12}; P=29,j=16 gives {18,14} and j=14 gives {16}; P=31,j=16 gives {18,16}. All satisfy the range in section 3. For the general construction put t=(P-1)/ell^r with ell^r=2m+1, where ell is odd. The prescribed type is chi*eta_P^i: chi is the Teichmuller lift of the existing residual inertia character, and eta_P has order ell^r. It is not asserted that chi*eta_P^i has odd order. The existing type reduces from omega_P^(k-2), with the original k even. Reduction at ell kills precisely the ell-primary part of the finite cyclic character group, so the exponent a of chi satisfies a congruent to k-2 modulo t. The exponent of eta_P^i is a multiple of t. Thus the chosen exponent j satisfies j congruent to k-2 modulo t. Since t is even, j is even. The additional interval imposed by the caller selects j but is not needed for the local calculation beyond 0<j<P-1; hence 2<=j<=P-3. This derives only parity from the prescribed type, not existence of a lift with that type.

The global existence of the compatible system, its same-member identity, its q-local potentially Barsotti--Tate property, and its precise principal-series type remain inputs. This packet does not infer them from a shared determinant. It does not establish their publication status.
