# Published low-weight base cases and the unramified FIRST exit

Author: /root. Author proposal awaiting independent review.
This is a source-to-caller application, not a reproof of the retained
published low-weight theorems or the full Serre induction.

## 1. Exact final published inputs

The source is Khare and Wintenberger, On Serre's conjecture for
2-dimensional mod p representations of Gal(Qbar/Q), Annals of
Mathematics 169 (2009), 229-253, final published text.

On printed229, an S-type representation is continuous, two-dimensional,
odd and absolutely irreducible over a finite field of characteristic p.
N(r) is the prime-to-p Artin conductor; k(r) is the actual Serre weight.
The fixed algebraic coefficient embeddings in that definition are retained.

Theorem5.2(i), printed247, states that no such representation has
N(r)=1 and k(r)=2. Its statement has no additional cyclotomic-restriction
or semistability hypothesis. The proof addresses the exceptional
restriction branch separately; a hypothesis introduced in that proof is
not a hypothesis missing from the theorem's statement.

Theorem5.4, printed247 with proof248, states nonexistence for N(r)=1
and 2<=k(r)<=8. Its further weight14 clause has a p!=11 qualification.
We do not use that clause. The low-weight interval statement applies to
the arbitrary finite coefficient field in the S-type definition.

On printed230, the paper's definition of modular residual representation
also includes odd representations reducible over the algebraic closure
of the residue field. This is the convention used by the already reviewed
conditional FIRST lifting connection. It is not an assertion that an
irreducible solvable-image representation is modular.

## 2. The initial lower-weight cases

Let r be an original level-one representation in any caller of the
selected induction, at odd coefficient prime P, with actual weight
2,4 or6. The caller requires r to be odd and absolutely irreducible
over a finite coefficient field. Unramifiedness at every finite prime
different from P gives N(r)=1 in the source's prime-to-P convention.
Theorem5.4 therefore says that no such r exists.

In particular the required modularity assertion in these initial
weight ranges is vacuous. This also disposes of the actual nonordinary
lower-weight alternatives of normalization18 whenever they land in
those ranges. No nonexistence assertion for a reducible representation
is made. The later weight ranges obtained by induction remain later
steps; Theorem5.4 is not extrapolated beyond its displayed range.

## 3. The unramified-at-P first-family exit

Let A be the actual first compatible system from construction14 and
let ell!=P be the selected odd coefficient prime. Let r be the reduction
of its actual stable lattice at the fixed ell-adic embedding, with
coefficients in its finite residue field. Retain the separately reviewed
finite-flat application17, which gives actual weight k(r)=2 for this
lattice quotient. Construction14 makes A_ell unramified outside P,ell.

Suppose that the current easy-exit branch has r unramified at P.
Then r is unramified at every finite prime other than ell, so N(r)=1.
It is odd: the system is odd, its residual characteristic is not2,
and reduction preserves the determinant -1 on complex conjugation.

If r were absolutely irreducible over the algebraic closure, it would
be an S-type representation in the exact domain of Theorem5.2(i).
That theorem contradicts N(r)=1 and k(r)=2. Thus r is geometrically
reducible. Its global semisimplification remains odd and geometrically
reducible, and is modular in the printed230 convention.

This supplies the residual-modularity hypothesis of the conditional
FIRST lifting theorem for the same A_ell. The theorem's crystalline
weights, determinant, absolute irreducibility in characteristic zero,
finite ramification and ordered local hypotheses are supplied by their
separate construction/local arguments. No equality with an arbitrary
new characteristic-zero lift is asserted.

If a caller defines r as the global semisimplification at the outset,
the reasoning has the same conclusion. In the contradictory absolutely
irreducible case the original lattice reduction was already irreducible,
so its actual weight-two calculation applies unchanged. In the other
case the printed modularity convention applies directly. This avoids
assuming in general that an arbitrary wild extension and its local
semisimplification have the same Serre weight.

## 4. Exact consumption and boundaries

The weight2/4/6 input is the beginning of W8 and the lower-weight induction
used by normalization18 and RETURN. The unramified FIRST exit applies
at each selected pair: (P,ell)=(7,3),(11,5),(19,3),(29,7),(31,5), and
the general odd ell dividing P-1. It has no large-prime restriction
excluding ell=3. Construction14 is used only at its actual P>=7 domain.

The base theorem is an input to the induction, not a consequence of
that same induction in this application. Its published proof retains
the stated minimal-lifting, compatible-system, ordinary modularity and
small-conductor inputs. Their existing source/risk records remain in
force. This packet does not reprove or retire that ancestry.

The separate globally irreducible solvable-image branch is not settled
by this argument if it is ramified at P. It retains its classical
residual-modularity input. Global twisting, subsequent induction
assembly, exact current source-record adoption and TeX incorporation
also remain separate. No whole-route or release approval is asserted.
