## The general parity argument

The notation must distinguish the two primes. The coefficient prime called
p in Khare's Proposition 2.2 is the auxiliary odd prime ell in the RETURN
application; the ramified prime q there is P here. Write

    P-1 = ell^r t, with ell odd and r >= 1.

The relevant characteristic-zero character group has cyclic order P-1.
Use the chosen embeddings to write its original character as omega_P^b,
where b=k-2 and the original k is even. The actual compatible-system/type
identification giving this original character is a retained input; this
small review does not construct the compatible system.

Reduction in residue characteristic ell kills exactly the ell-primary
subgroup of this finite cyclic group. Here is the elementary verification.
Every root of unity of ell-power order reduces to 1, since over a field of
characteristic ell the corresponding power polynomial is a power of X-1.
Reduction is injective on roots of unity of order prime to ell. If such a
root zeta is congruent to 1 and zeta^t=1, factoring zeta^t-1 gives

    (zeta-1)(1+zeta+...+zeta^(t-1))=0.

The second factor reduces to t, which is a unit. Thus zeta=1. Decomposing
the cyclic group into its ell-primary and prime-to-ell factors proves the
claimed kernel. This uses no assertion that the whole original character
has odd order.

Let chi=omega_P^a be the Teichmuller lift of the existing residual character.
It and omega_P^b have the same reduction. Their quotient therefore belongs
to the ell-primary subgroup, generated by omega_P^t. Consequently

    a is congruent to b modulo t.

Khare defines eta_P=omega_P^t. The prescribed new type is chi times a power
of eta_P, so its exponent j satisfies j congruent to a, hence to k-2,
modulo t. Since ell^r is odd and P-1 is even, t is even. The exponent j is
therefore even. For the nontrivial type selected by the caller, represented
with 0<j<P-1, this implies 2<=j<=P-3.

The corrected paragraph states this argument accurately. The earlier claim
that the entire prescribed character has odd order, or that j must be a
multiple of t, is unnecessary and generally false. The correct assertion
is the congruence class modulo t. The proof also correctly avoids identifying
chi with the original characteristic-zero character: Teichmuller lifting
after reduction can discard its ell-primary part.

This verifies parity and the stated range, conditional on the caller's
existing type and nontriviality. It does not prove that the interval contains
a suitable exponent, that the prescribed lift exists, or that the resulting
member has the required potentially Barsotti--Tate property. Those are
separate obligations.

## Nonnegative rather than integral valuations

The literal condition at the end of Savitt Proposition 2.17 is that x1 and
x2 belong to the ring of integers O_E and that val_P(x1*x2)=1. Thus the
normalized valuations are nonnegative and sum to 1. Membership of the
coefficients in O_E does not mean their P-normalized valuations are
integers: for ramified E/Q_P, that value group can contain fractions.

The correction to nonnegative valuations is therefore required and agrees
with the source. Both coefficients are nonzero, since their product has
finite valuation. It leaves open exactly the unit-endpoint and two-positive-
slope cases used in the following argument. Proposition 2.20 explicitly
allows both slopes strictly between zero and one. I am accepting this
wording/source comparison, not independently re-proving the classification
or the subsequent strongly divisible lattice calculations.

## Decision and limits

Both corrections are mathematically valid in the stated scope. There is no
remaining objection from this review to these two pieces. The separate
independent review must still cover the local classification, chosen-lattice
orientation, Serre weight conversion, examples and correction-family
comparison. Global construction, all actual caller hypotheses, source-edition
qualifications, current inventory adoption and Version 14 release remain
outside this decision.
