## Published source and prime selection

The at-most-two-prime construction is valid. If Q=2^a+1 is prime, an odd factor of a would make Q composite, so a is a power of two. A larger prime of that form has exponent at least 2a. It is therefore at least (Q-1)^2+1, which exceeds 2Q for the actual Q>31. The next Nagura prime below 6Q/5 cannot be of that form. Thus the chosen P is non-Fermat and satisfies B<P<36B/25. This reasoning does not enumerate known Fermat primes or assume there are no unknown ones.

The displayed numerical comparison is correct. The difference between (3B-1)/2 and 36B/25 is (3B-25)/50, positive for B>=31. The quantity ((2m+1)B-m)/(m+1)=2B-1-(B-1)/(m+1) increases with m>=1 and equals (3B-1)/2 at m=1. Consequently the exact source inequality holds for every odd prime-power divisor q=2m+1 of P-1, not only for a specially favorable divisor. Exact extraction of q=ell^r leaves t=(P-1)/q even, positive and coprime to ell. The weaker P<2B follows as stated.
