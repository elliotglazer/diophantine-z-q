# Crystalline modularity and the level-one weight transfer

Author proposal by /root/astra_v14_baseline_theta. This packet supplies new connecting arguments. Independent acceptance is required. It preserves the endpoint and DFG checkpoints and does not claim to authenticate Khare's unavailable final Duke text. Named published theorems and their stated ancestors remain inputs.

## 1. Scope and conventions

Let p be odd. Representations have coefficients in a finite extension of Q_p, which may be enlarged. Absolute irreducibility means irreducibility over the algebraic closure. A residual representation is the semisimplification of the reduction of a stable lattice unless a local extension is expressly retained. Use the early Khare-Wintenberger convention in Definition 3.1, printed p237: weights (0,h) mean C_p plus C_p(h), with h positive. Thus D_cris(V dual) has filtration jumps 0,h. This convention is not the manuscript's numerical cyclotomic weight -1 convention; duality reverses that numerical sign.

The main target is the following precise replacement of the consumed Khare Lemma 5.4. Suppose rho is absolutely irreducible, unramified at all finite primes other than p, crystalline at p with weights (0,k-1), with even 2<=k<=p+1, and has modular reduction. Here modular includes an odd geometrically reducible reduction, as in early KW p230. Then rho is attached to a weight-k newform of level 1. The proof below is relative to the named lifting, compatible-system and local comparison inputs, not a proof of those inputs from first principles.

The phrase geometric is used in the precise published Fontaine convention: finite ramification and potential semistability at p. Fontaine, Deforming semistable Galois representations, PNAS94 (1997), p11138, states this definition for representations over a finite extension of Q_p, and separately calls origin in algebraic geometry a conjectural consequence. Our finitely ramified crystalline representation meets the definition, as does its dual. DFG p668 separately uses pseudo-geometric for finite ramification and de Rham; no identification based merely on the words is used. Gaussian supplied the Fontaine source and this terminology connection. A different reviewer must assess that new connection.

## 2. A local ordinary lemma, including weight two

Let V be a two-dimensional crystalline representation of G_Qp with weights (0,h), where 1<=h<=p. If its residual representation is geometrically reducible, then V has a stable line and an unramified quotient of dimension one. The line has inertia character chi_p^h. This is ordinarity in early KW Definition 3.1. It holds for any stable lattice after taking its saturated intersection with that line.

First suppose V is geometrically irreducible. BLZ Section 1.1, PDF pp1-2, writes it as V_(h+1,a) tensor eta, with a of positive valuation and eta crystalline. Comparison of the two weights forces eta to have weight zero. A crystalline character of weight zero is unramified. One way to see the last assertion is to compare its one-dimensional D_cris with that of the unramified character having the same Frobenius eigenvalue. The rank-one Newton-Hodge equality for an admissible module makes this eigenvalue a unit, and the fully faithful crystalline functor identifies the characters. We retain this necessary admissibility condition, as well as strict exactness, among the standard Fontaine crystalline-category inputs; neither is inferred merely from the words fully faithful. We use the exact crystalline equivalence stated in DFG p711, citing Fontaine [36, 3.6.5].

BLZ Corollary 4.1.3(1), PDF p11, applies for h+1<=p+1 and every positive valuation of a. At h=p this uses the explicit m=0 allowance in Remark 4.1.2(1), not the generic floor bound. Proposition 4.1.4 then identifies the reduction, up to the unramified twist, with ind(omega_2^h), since p+1 does not divide h. Its two inertia eigencharacters are omega_2^h and omega_2^(ph). They are distinct precisely because p+1 does not divide h; Frobenius interchanges their eigenspaces. Therefore the reduction is absolutely irreducible, contradicting our hypothesis. This also handles h=1.

Consequently V is reducible after a finite coefficient extension. Its crystalline line and quotient have weights 0 and h in some order. This uses exactness of the crystalline category and strictness of its filtered-module functor. If the line has weight h, the quotient has weight zero and is unramified by the preceding rank-one argument. If instead the line has weight zero, put D=D_cris(V dual). Its subobject D_h has filtration jump h and its quotient D_0 has filtration jump 0. The Frobenius eigenvalues have valuations h and 0, respectively. They are distinct, so the two-dimensional Frobenius module splits as D_h plus its other eigenspace. Strictness gives Fil^j D=D_h for 1<=j<=h. Thus this Frobenius splitting also respects the filtration. The exact fully faithful equivalence identifies V with the direct sum of its two crystalline characters. Choosing its weight-h line now again gives an unramified quotient. There is no appeal here to vanishing of an incorrectly oriented extension group.

For an arbitrary stable lattice T, the intersection T with the chosen line is saturated: if a nonzero scalar multiple of t belongs to that intersection, then t lies in the same vector-space line. Over the coefficient discrete valuation ring the intersection and the quotient are therefore free of rank one. Inertia acts trivially on the quotient because it does so after inverting the uniformizer. This checks the integral definition of ordinary used in the lifting theorem. In particular, a finite-character twist which makes a scalar tame type crystalline of weights (0,1) can use this lemma, provided its residual representation is reducible. The twist and its later removal are separate inputs to that application.

## 3. Determinant and the three residual branches

For rho in Section 1, det(rho) chi_p^(1-k) is crystalline of weight zero at p and unramified away from p. The rank-one argument in Section 2 makes it unramified at p as well. A continuous character of G_Q unramified at all finite primes is trivial: every finite quotient would give an everywhere-finite-unramified extension of Q, whose discriminant has absolute value 1, excluded by Minkowski unless the extension is Q. Compactness then excludes a nontrivial infinite profinite image. Hence det(rho)=chi_p^(k-1). In particular rho is odd, and its residual inertia determinant is omega_p^(k-1), an odd exponent. Alternatively oddness already follows from modular reduction and p odd.

If the global residual representation is geometrically reducible, its restriction at p is reducible. Section 2 makes rho ordinary. Early KW Theorem 5.1, p246, now applies: rho is absolutely irreducible, finitely ramified, ordinary in its integral sense, has the stated weights, has modular reduction, and has odd inertia determinant exponent. Its conclusion is a weight-k newform. This theorem is retained with its Skinner-Wiles ancestry; the argument does not replace those proofs.

Next suppose the residual representation is absolutely irreducible but becomes reducible over K=Q(sqrt((-1)^((p-1)/2)*p)), the quadratic subfield of Q(mu_p). Clifford theory for this index-two subgroup, with p odd, expresses it as an induction of a character, so it is projectively dihedral. The strict-level-one dihedral connection in the separate Anchor packet proves that its local restriction at p is reducible. That packet's hypotheses are exactly oddness, global absolute irreducibility, the dihedral condition, and ramification only at p. Its cyclotomic twist does not affect local reducibility. Section 2 again gives ordinarity of the original rho, with its original ordered unramified quotient, and Theorem 5.1 applies. This packet does not infer characteristic-zero ordinarity merely from a split residual representation; Section 2 is the required bridge. The Anchor dihedral packet has the separate compiler acceptance recorded in INPUTS.json; that acceptance does not cover our characteristic-zero bridge.

Finally suppose the residual restriction to K is absolutely irreducible. At k=p+1, the accepted endpoint argument applies. If the local characteristic-zero representation were nonordinary, early KW Proposition 3.2 and BLZ would give residual Serre weight 2. Global residual oddness and unramifiedness away from p would contradict early KW Theorem 5.2(i). Thus rho is ordinary, and Theorem 5.1 applies again. This uses the already frozen endpoint proof and its nonauthor review.

## 4. The nondegenerate interior lifting application

In the remaining case k<p+1. Since k is even and p odd, k<=p-1 and h=k-1<=p-2. Apply final DFG Theorem 3.6, p723, to V=rho dual. Its restriction at p is ramified: a nonzero Hodge weight h rules out an unramified crystalline representation. Its filtration has jumps 0,h, so Fil^0 D=D and Fil^p D=0. For every nonzero quotient, including a quotient of the underlying Q_p-representation, exactness gives filtration jumps in {0,h}. Twisting that quotient by Q_p(p-1) moves every jump into the strictly negative interval [-(p-1),-1]. It therefore cannot be unramified. This is the full shortness condition of DFG p670, not just a bound on the length of the filtration.

Duality preserves absolute irreducibility over K and takes arithmetic modularity to DFG's cohomological normalization on p715. The geometric premise is verified by Section 1 using the published Fontaine definition. The theorem gives modularity of V. The construction on p721 produces a classical newform, and the internal character twists on p723 are finite and have Hodge weight zero. To identify its weight, use the newform filtration in DFG Section1.6.2: a form of weight w has jumps 0,w-1 in the cohomological convention. Even if modularity is first expressed with a Tate twist t, its ordered jumps are -t,w-1-t. Equality with the two jumps 0,k-1 forces t=0 and w=k. This identifies the characteristic-zero weight without presuming that a reducible residual weight-two extension is peu ramifie. The minimal-conductor and determinant twists appearing inside that proof are not additional hypotheses imposed on rho. We do not apply the DFG theorem in the reducible or K-degenerate branches.

## 5. Exact level one from a full modular compatible family

The local compatibility supplier here is a statement in the final DFG text itself, not an inferred equality of Euler factors. Section 1.6.3, p684, states that for every finite rational place v the newform motive's local Frobenius-semisimple Weil-Deligne representation corresponds to pi_v(f). Initially its coefficient places avoid the finite set S. Footnote 1 on that same page states that this object is the restriction of an object M in the full category PM_K which is L-admissible everywhere, citing the main theorem of Saito [71]. The definition on p669 says that L-admissibility is compatibility of the K-rational Frobenius-semisimple Weil-Deligne representations themselves, and says explicitly that unramified means both trivial inertia and zero monodromy.

This suffices also at an omitted coefficient place. Fix v and choose a coefficient place outside S and away from v. Its local parameter is the one attached to pi_v(f) by p684. Full L-admissibility transfers that same parameter, including monodromy, to every coefficient place of M. The full object's member at the coefficient place used by rho has the same good Frobenius polynomials as the already absolutely irreducible rho dual, in the cohomological normalization established below. Chebotarev and Brauer-Nesbitt identify its semisimplification with rho dual. Since that semisimplification is irreducible of dimension two, the full member itself is irreducible and isomorphic to rho dual. No semisimplicity theorem for arbitrary premotivic objects is assumed. This identification is in characteristic zero, not an identification from congruent residual traces.

For normalization, separate the two sources of modularity. In the ordinary-lifting case, let epsilon be the usual arithmetic nebentype of the resulting f. Its arithmetic representation has determinant epsilon chi_p^(k-1). Section 3 therefore forces epsilon=1 before comparing it with DFG's cohomological object. With trivial nebentype, DFG (18), p684, gives exactly the geometric-Frobenius polynomial of rho dual. In the DFG-lifting case, first undo the finite character twists in its proof using (22), p685, and call the resulting cohomological newform g. The weight calculation in Section 4 has already excluded a nonzero Tate twist. Now (16), p683, gives det(M_g)=M_psi(1-k); comparison with det(rho dual)=chi_p^(1-k) forces psi=1. In either case the finite nebentype is trivial, and dualizing with arithmetic Frobenius yields X^2-a_v X+v^(k-1). For arbitrary nontrivial nebentype, M_f is not silently identified with the dual of the usual arithmetic realization without the appropriate finite-character adjustment.

At v different from p, rho is unramified by hypothesis. At p it is crystalline, so its Weil-Deligne parameter is unramified with zero monodromy. The preceding full comparison implies that every pi_v(f) is unramified. Local Langlands preserves the unramified condition; newform level is the product of the local conductors. Consequently the primitive newform has level 1. This proves the target of Section 1, relative to the explicitly named published inputs and the independently reviewed dihedral input.

DFG is the immediate published supplier for the full-family assertion just used. Saito1997 remains its named antecedent, and this packet does not claim an independent final-edition comparison or a reproof of Saito. The Hilbert modular Saito2009 theorem, whose standing base-field degree exceeds one, is not specialized to Q. No whole-paper equivalence or correction claim is made.

## 6. Residual weights of the member at the transfer prime

Suppose now that rho_p belongs to an odd absolutely irreducible compatible system of weight k, unramified at every finite place, with p odd and even 4<=k<=p+1. If its global residual representation is absolutely irreducible, it has Serre weight k except for the impossible level-one weight-two alternative.

For k<=p-1 and geometrically irreducible rho_p at p, BLZ gives inertia omega_2^(k-1) plus omega_2^(p(k-1)). Serre (2.2.4), pp183-184, with a=0 and b=k-1 gives weight k. If rho_p at p is reducible, the proof of Section 2 makes it ordinary even without a residual-reducibility assumption. Its actual residual lattice representation has unramified quotient and inertia omega_p^(k-1) on its line. If inertia is semisimple, Serre (2.3.2) gives weight k. If wild inertia is nontrivial, Serre (2.4.3)-(2.4.5), pp185-186, has alpha=0 on the quotient and beta=k-1 on the line. Since 3<=k-1<=p-2, beta is not alpha+1, so the exceptional peu/très-ramifie case does not occur, and the formula again gives k. This calculation uses the ordered extension, not just its unordered semisimplification.

At k=p+1, early KW Proposition 3.2 says that an irreducible local crystalline representation gives residual weight 2, while a reducible one is ordinary and gives weight 2 or p+1. The global residual representation has conductor 1. Early KW Theorem 5.2(i) excludes weight 2, leaving p+1. The case k=2 is not included in this weight lemma; all level-one odd absolutely irreducible target residual representations of weight 2 are already excluded by that same theorem.

## 7. The exact compatible-system transfer

Fix an odd p and even 2<=k<=p+1. Assume that every odd absolutely irreducible mod-p representation of conductor 1 and Serre weight k is modular. Let q be a prime with q>=k-1, and let r_q be odd and absolutely irreducible, of conductor 1 and Serre weight k. We show it is modular. If k=2, no such r_q exists by early KW Theorem 5.2(i). For k>=4, q=2 is impossible; q=3 forces k=4 and is excluded by Theorem 5.2(iii). We may therefore take q>3.

If r_q restricted to Q(mu_q) is reducible, use the separately stated known-dihedral case in the proof of early KW Theorem 5.2, p247: Hecke supplies modularity, with the Ribet/Diamond refinements there supplying the prescribed weight and conductor. This is a retained published input, not a consequence of Anchor's local splitting lemma alone. Otherwise apply early KW Theorem 4.2(i), p244, at q. Its assumptions hold: q is odd, 2<=k<=q+1, k is even and therefore not q, and the cyclotomic restriction is absolutely irreducible. It supplies an irreducible compatible system lifting exactly r_q at its chosen embedding.

The definition on pp243-244 makes every member absolutely irreducible and gives a common E-rational Frobenius-semisimple Weil-Deligne parameter at each finite place. Minimality away from q and conductor 1 make those parameters unramified away from q; Theorem 4.2(i) explicitly makes the parameter at q unramified as well. The definition and theorem therefore give a crystalline member of weights (0,k-1) at every odd coefficient prime, including p. Every odd-coefficient-prime member needed here is odd: its determinant has the same weights and is unramified away from its coefficient prime, so Section 3 gives its determinant chi^(k-1). No crystalline or determinant assertion at coefficient prime 2 is needed.

Consider the member rho_p. If its residual representation is geometrically reducible, it is modular in the convention used by the lifting theorem. If it is absolutely irreducible, Section 6 gives weight k and conductor 1, so the induction hypothesis at p makes it modular. Sections 3-5 then make rho_p modular of weight k and level 1. This application uses the geometric terminology connection and the separately accepted dihedral input above, and retains the precise published hypotheses of Theorem 4.2(i).

Here is the transfer back with embeddings specified. Embed the system field E and the coefficient field of the resulting newform f into Q_p-bar using the identification of rho_p. Let L be their compositum there. At every common good rational prime v, equality of the characteristic-zero representations gives equality of their traces and determinants as elements of L, since the embedding into Q_p-bar is injective. Extend the originally fixed q-adic embedding of E to an embedding of L into Q_q-bar. Under that extension, rho_q and the corresponding q-adic realization of f have the same good Frobenius polynomials. Chebotarev and Brauer-Nesbitt identify their semisimplifications; both are already semisimple. Reducing a stable lattice now gives the original r_q, because the embedding of E at q was preserved. Thus r_q is modular. A different embedding of the form's coefficient field simply gives its conjugate newform. No step equates characteristic-zero lifts merely because their reductions coincide.

The weaker conclusion needed for GENERAL-LINK-BACK is now precise: modularity transferred through the second compatible system proves modularity of the residual representation at the fixed joining prime. Any first-system characteristic-zero lifting application must still check its own original local type and residual branch. This packet does not replace the tame potentially Barsotti-Tate connection assigned separately to Anchor.

## 8. The all-weights version and retained sources

If level-one modularity is known for every Serre weight at a fixed p>2, the preceding transfer applies to every even k<=p+1 and q>=k-1. For q<k-1, twist r_q by a power of the global mod-q cyclotomic character so that its Serre weight k' lies between 2 and q+1. This normalization is stated in early KW p237. It preserves oddness, absolute irreducibility and conductor 1; Serre (2.5.1) makes k' even. Now k'<=q+1<=p+1 and q>=k'-1. Apply Section 7 to the twisted representation and untwist its modular form by the corresponding finite Dirichlet character. DFG p685, (22), supplies this twist operation and its attached representation. The conclusion is residual modularity; no assertion that the untwisted intermediate characteristic-zero newform still has level 1 is needed. The q=2 level-one case is already empty by the Tate case quoted in early KW p247.
