## 1. Exact scope

Let l be odd, E a finite extension of Q_l, and V a continuous absolutely irreducible two-dimensional representation of G_Q over E, ramified at finitely many primes. Suppose V is crystalline at l with weights (0,1), in the early KW237 convention C_l plus C_l(1). Suppose det V=epsilon_l psi with psi finite and unramified at l. Let r be the semisimplification of a stable-lattice reduction, after a finite coefficient extension when needed. Assume r is modular in the exact sense of final early KW230. That convention includes odd geometrically reducible representations; it does not make every irreducible residual representation modular.

Then V is modular of weight two. Ramification at another prime P is allowed. The lemma neither constructs V nor supplies modularity of an unknown irreducible residual representation.

The crystalline/BT implication is retained as the actual published weight0,1 interface used in Kisin's proof of Lemma2.3.8 at1122, with its Breuil antecedent. The local crystalline ordinary lemma and BLZ h=1 classification are consumed through Theta completion002 Section2, with its disjoint component decisions and source-version qualification. In particular this packet does not call the held BLZ PDF a fully authenticated final-volume comparison.

## 2. Proof of the first-member lifting lemma

Write K_l=Q(sqrt((-1)^((l-1)/2) l)). If r restricted to G_K_l is absolutely irreducible, the final Q-corollary Kisin3.5.8,1172-1173 applies: V is potentially Barsotti-Tate, its determinant is finite times cyclotomic, and its reduction is modular. Its conclusion is modularity. The weights determine weight two. We apply this Q-corollary itself, not a version of the preceding Hilbert theorem with hypotheses deleted.

If r is globally geometrically reducible, its local restriction is reducible. The accepted local h=1 lemma gives V an unramified quotient. It supplies integral ordinarity by saturating the intersection of a stable lattice with the invariant line.

Suppose instead that r is absolutely irreducible but its restriction to G_K_l is reducible. Clifford theory for this index-two normal subgroup, in odd characteristic, expresses r as the induction of a character of G_K_l. We claim that r is locally reducible at l. Otherwise V is also locally absolutely irreducible: a characteristic-zero invariant line would give an invariant residual line by saturated intersection. The h=1 BLZ calculation used in completion002 then gives, up to an unramified twist, local residual inertia eigencharacters omega_2 and omega_2^l. Their ratio has order l+1.

On the other hand the induced dihedral calculation in the independently reviewed dihedral proof Section4 applies without any assumption of ramification only at l. Its finite projective image has order prime to l; inertia is cyclic and maps nontrivially to the inducing quadratic quotient since K_l is ramified at l. A cyclic subgroup of the dihedral group containing an element outside the rotation subgroup is generated by a reflection and has order two. Thus the same projective inertia would have order two, contrary to l+1>2. Hence r is locally reducible and the h=1 ordinary lemma again applies. No class-number calculation or unique-inducing-field assertion is used.

In either reducible-local case, psi is unramified at l, so det(r)|I_l=omega_l. This is the odd exponent required by final early KW Theorem5.1 at246. V is absolutely irreducible, finitely ramified, ordinary in its integral Definition3.1, and has weights(0,1) and modular reduction. That theorem gives a weight-two newform. Its Skinner-Wiles proof ancestry remains explicit. Global reducibility of r and an irreducible solvable-image r are different: the latter still requires its known residual-modularity input. This proves the conditional lemma.

## 3. Narrow link-back for the actual surviving branch

Fix two weight-two compatible systems A and B over number fields, with specified embeddings into Q_l-bar. Assume their residual l-representations are isomorphic to the same absolutely irreducible r with nonsolvable image. Assume B has already been proved modular. Assume A_l is the original first member, satisfies the crystalline, determinant and finite-ramification hypotheses of Section1, and is absolutely irreducible. These are hypotheses about the actual constructed A; sharing r with B does not prove them.

Modularity of B at the fixed l-embedding gives modularity of r. Moreover r|G_K_l is absolutely irreducible. If it were reducible, the same index-two Clifford argument would make r induced from a character, with projectively dihedral and hence solvable linear image. The scalar kernel is abelian, so this contradicts nonsolvability. Kisin3.5.8 therefore applies directly to A_l. Neither an equality A_l=B_l nor a new local ordinary assertion is required.

To return to A_P, take the coefficient-field compositum of A and the modular form inside Q_l-bar. Equality of characteristic-zero Frobenius polynomials at l is equality of algebraic elements there. Extend the originally fixed P-embedding of the system field to this compositum. Compatibility then makes A_P and the corresponding modular P-adic realization have equal good Frobenius polynomials. Chebotarev and Brauer-Nesbitt identify their semisimplifications. The original A_P is assumed absolutely irreducible and lifts the specified original residual representation at this fixed embedding. Reduction therefore gives its modularity. The classical prescribed level/weight refinement remains separate.

The actual source p28 explicitly says that the two l-residual representations are isomorphic and nonsolvable. Thus this lemma matches the surviving GENERAL-LINK-BACK branch after the first easy exits. The same pattern occurs in the explicit weight8 case on p24. It proves the connection once the two systems and B's modularity are supplied, not the existence of either system or the RETURN weight-drop premise.

## 4. What this does not replace

Khare author Propositions2.1,2.2,3.1 supply different construction outputs. Final early KW3.3/4.2(i) construct a minimal crystalline lift/system of the original residual weight. They do not, merely by their wording, supply an ordinary weight-two lift with finite tame type at P, or an l-adic lift with prescribed nonminimal P-type, or every claimed ramified P-member property of the second system. Their actual use requires a new matching argument. No such argument is asserted here.

For FIRST, residual modularity is available in the easy exits only from the stated known solvable-image theorem or the level-one weight-two base case after unramifiedness at P. In the nonsolvable, ramified-at-P branch it is the objective. The proof must create the second system and establish its modularity by a lower-weight RETURN before Section3 applies.
