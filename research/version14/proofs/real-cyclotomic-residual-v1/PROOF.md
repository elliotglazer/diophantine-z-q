# Finite real cyclotomic inputs for the residual positive complex

This note supplies only the finite-level argument at main14018–14067. It does not prove compatible inverse limits, uniform free models, or the later rank calculation.

Put m=n+2, L=Q(zeta_(2^m)), K_n=L^+, r_n=2^n, and let S_n be the finite primes above 2 and the specified fixed finite odd support. Write s_n=|S_n|; all archimedean places are also allowed in G_(K_n,S_n).

For d=2^(m-1), f(X)=X^d+1 is irreducible by applying Eisenstein at 2 to f(1+T). Its roots are the primitive 2^m-th roots. Thus L/Q is Galois of degree d and K_n/Q has degree d/2. The elementary identity v_2(5^(2^j)-1)=j+2, proved by factoring the next square minus one, shows that the odd units modulo 2^m are generated by -1 and 5; quotienting by conjugation gives cyclic Gal(K_n/Q) of order 2^n.

At every odd prime, f and f_prime=d X^(d-1) are coprime modulo that prime, so Cox Proposition5.11(i), p102, makes L/Q unramified there. At a prime above2, zeta reduces to1. Set t=v(zeta-1)>0 and e=v(2), with integral normalized valuation. In f(1+(zeta-1))=0 every intermediate term has valuation strictly greater than e, because each intermediate binomial coefficient is even. The constant and leading terms must tie: d*t=e. The degree formula (Cox Theorem5.9, p101) then forces e=d,t=1,f_residue=1 and one prime above2. These facts descend to the real subfields; K_n/K_(n-1) is quadratic and totally ramified at its unique dyadic prime.

Use ordinary Hilbert class fields, not narrow ones. Cox pp105–106 defines unramified at infinite primes as well, and Theorem5.18 with Theorem5.23/Corollary5.24 pp108–109 identifies the maximal unramified abelian extension with the ordinary ideal class group. Consequently the Hilbert two-class field has all real places split. Starting with K_0=Q, suppose the two-class group of K_(n-1) is zero. The Hilbert two-class field H of K_n is canonical, hence Galois over K_(n-1), with finite two-group G. Inertia above2 has order2 (Cox Proposition5.10, p102, and the preceding ramification computation), and all other finite and infinite primes are unramified. The quotient of G_ab by the inertia image is trivial by the induction hypothesis. Therefore G_ab is cyclic of order at most2.

A finite p-group with cyclic abelianization is cyclic: choose g generating that abelianization; if <g> were proper, a maximal subgroup containing it would be normal of index p and contain the commutator subgroup, a contradiction. The maximal-subgroup fact follows from the normalizer condition for finite p-groups, proved by their nontrivial centers and induction. Hence |G|<=2; since H contains K_n, H=K_n. The ordinary class group and its S_n-class quotient have odd order.

Both 1-zeta and 1-zeta^3 generate the unique dyadic prime: they are conjugate algebraic integers, have norm2, and the valuation calculation gives valuation1. Their quotient is therefore an algebraic unit. Thus u=zeta^(-1)(zeta^3-1)/(zeta-1)=1+zeta+zeta^(-1) is a real unit. Its conjugates, indexed by odd 0<a<2^(m-1), are 1+2 cos(2*pi*a/2^m). They are negative exactly when 2^m/3<a<2^(m-1). The count 2^(m-2)-floor(2^(m-1)/3+1/2) is odd for m>=3 (separate m even and odd modulo3). So its norm is -1.

The signature vector of u has augmentation1 in F_2[C_(2^n)]. This ring is F_2[T]/((T-1)^(2^n)); every augmentation1 element is invertible by a finite geometric series. Its translates therefore span every signature. At n=0 the unit -1 gives the assertion. This proves signature surjectivity using actual units, without any general unit-rank formula.

Let U=Spec O_(K_n,S_n). Since2 is invertible on U, 1->mu_2->G_m--square-->G_m->1 is exact on the etale site: adjoining a square root of a unit is an etale cover. Its long exact sequence gives 0->O_U^*/O_U^{*2}->H^1(U,mu_2)->Pic(U)[2]->0 and 0->Pic(U)/2->H^2(U,mu_2)->H^2(U,G_m)[2]->0. This is the same Kummer sequence used in the proof of Milne ADT II Proposition2.9, p171. The odd-order class group makes both Picard terms zero. Proposition2.9 p170 identifies these mu_2 cohomology groups with ordinary G_(K_n,S_n) cohomology.

Milne ADT II Proposition2.1 and Remark2.2(a), pp163–165, identify H^2(U,G_m) with the kernel of the sum of local invariants over S_n and the real places. Each finite coordinate has two-torsion F_2 and each real Brauer coordinate is F_2. Thus H^2(G_(K_n,S_n),F_2) is the sum-zero hyperplane in F_2^(s_n+r_n), of dimension s_n+r_n-1. Its map to all real coordinates is onto because s_n>=1; its kernel has dimension s_n-1.

For completeness, the numerical H^1 count requires no unstated general S-unit theorem: Milne ADT I Corollary4.15 p62 gives finiteness and I Theorem5.1 p67 gives the truncated Euler characteristic |H^0||H^2|/|H^1|=1 for a totally real field and the trivial F_2-module. Its footnote explicitly uses ordinary H^0, not Tate H^0. Since dim H^0=1, dim H^1=r_n+s_n. Kummer identifies restriction of the actual unit classes with their signs, so the preceding unit calculation proves surjectivity H^1->F_2^r_n.

Define C^+=Cone(RGamma(G_(K_n,S_n),F_2)->direct_sum_(v real) RGamma(K_(n,v),F_2))[-1], using ordinary cochains including degree0. The H^0 map is the diagonal injection. Its cokernel has dimension r_n-1; the H^1 restriction kernel has dimension s_n. The long exact sequence therefore gives dim H^1(C^+)=r_n+s_n-1. Surjectivity in H^1 gives H^2(C^+)=ker(H^2_global->H^2_real), of dimension s_n-1. Surjectivity in H^2 and Milne ADT I Theorem4.10(c), pp56–57, which is an isomorphism to real cohomology for every degree>=3, give all other H^i(C^+)=0. Negative degrees also vanish for these ordinary nonnegative complexes.

This C^+ is not the compact-support complex of ADT II2.3: that construction uses complete Tate resolutions at the real places and also the finite places. We use only the stated ordinary positive-degree comparison and keep the ordinary real H^0 term. Nothing here interchanges n or a coefficient limit; all assertions above are at each finite n.
