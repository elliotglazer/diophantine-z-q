# Compact Selmer limits and central torsion cancellation

## Scope and inputs

Fix the actual elliptic curve E/Q, T=T_2(E), D=E[2^infinity], and the fixed finite support S containing 2, infinity and every bad prime. Assume the caller's full rational two-torsion, integral real eigensplitting, and computed real Kummer complex. Retain the elliptic Weil pairing, finite local Tate duality and finite Poitou–Tate with the real Tate terms. The calculation below uses ordinary real H^0 in the Selmer cone; positive real degrees agree with Tate cohomology. The same selected class z and its explicit reciprocity formula remain separate caller inputs.

## Coefficients, local conditions and limits

Put M_n=E[2^n]=T/2^nT. In the compact system M_(n+1) maps to M_n by multiplication by 2; in the discrete system M_n maps to M_(n+1) by inclusion. These maps are adjoint under the finite Weil pairings. The compact Kummer map on E(k)/2^n is reduction from n+1 to n. The discrete Kummer transition is multiplication by 2 on these quotients. Thus their direct limit is E(k) tensor Q_2/Z_2. These transitions commute with localization and cup products; they are not an unspecified choice of identifications between finite groups.

For a finite place v, let B_v=E(Q_v)^wedge_2. Local analytic structure makes B_v a finitely generated Z_2-module, of rank one at v=2 and rank zero otherwise. The derived reduction of B_v[-1] has H^0=B_v[2^n]=E(Q_v)[2^n] and H^1=B_v/2^n=E(Q_v)/2^n. Its Kummer morphism induces the full degree-zero local identification and the usual injective degree-one Kummer map. This can also be obtained by reducing the compatible Kummer exact sequences; retaining the torsion of B_v is essential.

At the real place use exactly the caller's two-term complex U_R, with differential J-1 and full ordinary invariant summand, not E(R)^wedge_2[-1] alone. Its derived reduction has the full E(R)[2^n] in degree zero and the computed Kummer image in degree one. Consequently the global Selmer cone reduced modulo 2^n has H^1 equal to Sel_(2^n)(E/Q), by the long exact sequence of the cone. At each place the finite Kummer subgroup is its own annihilator under finite local duality. At finite places this is the compatible Kummer/local Tate-duality diagram; at the real place it is the separately established minus-line calculation.

All finite-coefficient global groups for this fixed S are finite, as are the local groups and the kernels, images and quotients used here. For any fixed n, the images from later indices form a descending chain in a finite group, so they stabilize. Every such inverse system is therefore Mittag–Leffler and has zero first derived inverse limit. The exact sequence for continuous cohomology identifies H^i(G,T) with the inverse limit of H^i(G,M_n); the direct-limit identity identifies H^i(G,D) with the direct limit of H^i(G,M_n). The same argument applies to the finite local-condition cones. Filtered direct limits are exact. These facts justify each limit of kernels, images and finite exact sequences used below, including images that need not have surjective transition maps.

Restrict finite Poitou–Tate to the orthogonal Kummer subgroups. Its degree-one pairing sends a local class to its pairing with localization of a dual global class. Annihilating the dual Selmer group is exactly belonging to the sum of the global image and the local Kummer subgroup. Thus the restricted finite sequence has the local quotient, dual finite Selmer group, global degree two, local degree two, and dual global degree zero as its consecutive terms. Passing to the compact system on cohomology and the adjoint discrete system on Selmer gives the corresponding exact sequence. Pontryagin duality changes the discrete direct limit into the compact inverse limit. No inverse limit over the support or over a family of curves occurs.

The compact local quotient is the Tate module lim H^1(Q_v,E)[2^n], by the Kummer sequence and exactness just proved. Under finite local Tate duality its Pontryagin dual is the direct-limit Kummer subgroup E(Q_v) tensor Q_2/Z_2. Indeed the dual of the subgroup H^1(Q_v,E)[2^n] is E(Q_v)/2^n, with transition multiplication by 2. Local analytic structure therefore makes this compact quotient free of rank one at 2 and zero at every other finite place. Denote the quotient at 2 by L_2. At the real place the compact Kummer image is all of H^1(R,T), whereas its discrete orthogonal Kummer group is zero. There is consequently no real degree-one quotient, but H^2(R,T) remains. Write J_2 for the actual image of global H^1 in L_2. This image is closed, since the global group is a finitely generated compact Z_2-module. The resulting sequence is 0 -> L_2/J_2 -> Sel_(2^infinity)(E/Q)^vee -> H^2_glob -> (direct sum over v in S_f of H^2(Q_v,T)) direct sum H^2(R,T) -> H^0(Q,D)^vee -> 0. Its maps are localization and dual restriction. The finite high-degree real-localization isomorphism and the vanishing derived limits terminate this sequence. This is not a claim that the absolute Galois group of Q has 2-cohomological dimension two.

## Corank zero and the ordinary Selmer cone

Assume now that the discrete Selmer group has corank zero. It is cofinitely generated, hence finite. The global Kummer sequence and Mordell–Weil finite generation give rank E(Q)=0 and identify the discrete Selmer group with Sha(E/Q)[2^infinity]. Put s=length Sha[2^infinity]. The local H^2 groups are finite by local duality with the finite groups E(Q_v)[2^infinity], and the real H^2 group is finite. The preceding exact sequence therefore makes H^2_glob and L_2/J_2 finite.

The inverse limit of finite Selmer groups is E(Q)^wedge_2: the inverse limit of Sha[2^n] under multiplication by 2 is zero for finite Sha, and the finite Kummer exact sequences have vanishing derived limits. Thus H^1(C_Sel)=E(Q)^wedge_2. The local quotient cones have degree-one group L_2 at 2, zero degree-one group elsewhere, and degree-two groups equal to the actual local H^2 groups. Their long exact sequence, together with the preceding Poitou–Tate tail, gives 0 -> L_2/J_2 -> H^2(C_Sel) -> H^2_glob -> (direct sum finite local H^2) direct sum H^2(R,T) -> H^0(Q,D)^vee -> 0. In particular H^2(C_Sel) is finite. This derives the cone sequence from its actual maps; it does not identify two finite groups merely because their orders agree.

The two-term free resolution of Z/2^n gives 0 -> H^1(C_Sel)/2^n -> H^1(C_Sel tensor^L Z/2^n) -> H^2(C_Sel)[2^n] -> 0. The first two terms are E(Q)/2^n and Sel_(2^n), with their actual Kummer map. Comparison with the usual Kummer exact sequence identifies H^2(C_Sel)[2^n] with Sha[2^n]. For sufficiently large n both finite groups are exhausted, so length H^2(C_Sel)=s. There is no added real degree-zero torsion term.

Write g=length E(Q)[2^infinity], g_v=length E(Q_v)[2^infinity], r_j=length H^j(R,T) for j=1,2, and i=length L_2/J_2. Alternating lengths in the cone sequence give length H^2_glob = s-i+sum_v g_v+r_2-g. Local duality identifies the finite-place H^2 lengths with g_v and the last length with g. This is precisely the caller's E.6.

## The positive cone and its integral determinant

The map H^2_glob -> H^2(R,T) is onto. Indeed restriction H^0(Q,D) -> H^0(Q_2,D) is injective, so its dual is onto. Given any real component, choose a component at 2 with the opposite image in H^0(Q,D)^vee. Exactness of the preceding sequence lifts the resulting tuple to H^2_glob. This argument keeps the real term and uses the actual dual restriction map.

Let A=H^1_glob, K=ker(A -> H^1(R,T)), P=H^1(C^+). The ordinary-cone sequence identifies the kernel of A -> L_2 with the image of the finite H^1(C_Sel). Since L_2/J_2 is finite, J_2 has rank one; hence A has rank one. Use the positive-cone sequences 0 -> T^+ --a-> P -> K -> 0 and 0 -> coker(A -> H^1(R,T)) -> H^2(C^+) -> H^2_glob -> H^2(R,T) -> 0. The torsion of A has length g: the coefficient sequence T -> T tensor Q_2 -> D identifies it with H^0(Q,D), since rational invariants vanish. Let k and p_0 be the torsion lengths of K and P, m=length(A_free/K_free), and a_s the index of the image of T^+ in its saturation in P_free. Exactness on free parts and torsion gives length image(A -> H^1(R,T))=g-k+m and k=p_0+a_s. Hence the cokernel has length r_1-g+k-m. No assertion that a is primitive is used.

Retain the same-class identity of the caller, 2^(-c_E) a wedge b = 2 a wedge z_tilde, where z_tilde is a rational lift of z in A tensor Q_2. In a saturated basis of P_free, the determinant of this wedge has valuation a_s+1+v_free(z)-m. The inverse determinant of the positive complex contributes p_0-length H^2(C^+); this sign and these terms follow by putting a finite free presentation over Z_2 in Smith normal form. From the second positive-cone sequence and E.6, length H^2(C^+)=r_1-g+k-m+s-i+sum_v g_v-g. The total valuation is therefore 1+v_free(z)+i-s-sum_v g_v+2g-r_1, since k=p_0+a_s. All saturation and torsion terms have been retained until this cancellation.

For z nonzero, let e_2 be the valuation of exp^* on a generator of the free local quotient L_2. Then v_2(exp^* z)=v_free(z)+i+e_2, using the actual global image lattice J_2. Substitution gives v_2(u)=v_2(exp^* z)-s-sum_v g_v+2g+1-r_1-e_2, the caller's E.7. At z=0 this finite-valuation calculation is not asserted; zero detection is the separate rational determinant argument.

The subsequent E.8 application uses the separately supplied explicit reciprocity formula for this same z, its conductor trace and character basis, the active-prime inertia calculation, the bounded remaining local factors, and the forward-rank theorem where invoked. Substituting that formula into E.7 cancels each unused good-prime point-count valuation against the equal g_v. This paragraph supplies only that substitution and cancellation; it does not prove those arithmetic inputs or any new uniform bound.
