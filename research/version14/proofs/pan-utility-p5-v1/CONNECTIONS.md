## Framed versus versal, PAN-12

Pan's group at a place above five is `G_Q5`, after his standing complete-splitting reduction. Work over the ring of integers O of his finite coefficient field, with residue field k. The residual representation in this case is `eta ⊕ eta omega`. Its characters differ: omega has order four on inertia at five. A commuting matrix is therefore diagonal, so `dim_k H^0(G,Ad)=2`. The scalar twist eta has no effect on Ad. In trace-zero matrices the invariant dimension is one. Hence

`dim B^1(G,Ad)=4-2=2`, equivalently `dim B^1(G,ad^0)=3-1=2`.

KW09b author §2.2, Proposition 2.1, pp. 7–8, states that the map from a chosen versal ring to the framed ring is formally smooth of relative dimension `d^2-dim H^0(G,Ad)`. It assumes the profinite group's p-finiteness condition and a residual representation, not residual irreducibility. The paragraph immediately after the proof extends this assertion to a relatively representable deformation condition. Fixed determinant is such a condition: impose the equations `det rho(g)=chi(g)` on the versal family. Conjugation preserves these equations. The inverse image in the framed problem is the same fixed-determinant condition, so the square of functors is cartesian. Formal smoothness and relative tangent dimension persist under that base change. Pan's fixed-determinant versal hull may be identified with this hull, noncanonically. Thus the actual map has relative dimension two, and the framed ring is noncanonically a power-series ring in two variables over that hull.

Here is also an explicit local proof of the framing assertion, conditional only on the existence of the indicated hull and framed representing ring. It avoids treating the name of the source proposition as a connecting argument. Use fixed-determinant functors throughout. Let R be a hull, Q the framed representing ring, and choose a representative of the hull's versal deformation. It gives a map Q→R. Applying versality to the universal framed deformation gives ξ:R→Q. The composite R→Q→R induces the identity on the tangent space, because the tangent maps are a section and the quotient `Z^1(G,ad^0)→H^1(G,ad^0)`. A continuous endomorphism of a complete Noetherian local O-algebra with the same residue field which is surjective on its relative cotangent space is surjective by successive approximation in the maximal-ideal filtration. A surjective endomorphism of a Noetherian ring is injective. Adjust Q→R by the inverse automorphism to obtain a section of ξ.

For a small extension A'→A with kernel I killed by the maximal ideal, take a hull point over A' and a framed point over A agreeing over the hull. The section gives a framed lift of the hull point. Over A it is strictly conjugate to the specified framed point. Lift that conjugating matrix to A' and conjugate. The new framed lift has the right reduction, while its hull point differs from the requested point by an element of `I⊗_k H^1(G,ad^0)`. Lift this difference to `I⊗_k Z^1(G,ad^0)` and subtract that infinitesimal cocycle. Since I²=0, this changes the representation by exactly the desired tangent difference, preserves determinant, and does not change its reduction. This proves the lifting criterion. Its relative tangent space is B¹, of dimension two calculated above. No smoothness of the unframed ring is asserted.

Pan's separate Hu–Tan input presents that hull as `O[[c0,c1,d0,d1,b]]/(c0*d1-c1*d0)`. Adding two variables gives his seven-variable hypersurface. This argument does not prove that Hu–Tan presentation, normality of the hypersurface, or the nonsplit Paškūnas branch. The local p-finiteness and existence of the two deformation rings remain the standard deformation-theory premises already used by Pan. The calculation is valid at five. At two the two residual characters coalesce and this centralizer calculation cannot be reused; at three they are distinct, but Pan's separate nonsplit branch has its own p>3 condition.

## Euler characteristic and the two different modules, PAN-13 and PAN-14

Kisin author §9.6–9.7, pp. 54–55, allows a number field F, a finite set S containing all archimedean and p-adic places, and any continuous finite-dimensional E-representation V of G_(F,S), where E/Q_p is finite. It states vanishing above degree two and

`h0-h1+h2 = sum_(v infinite) dim_E V^(G_v) - [F:Q] dim_E V`.

Pan writes S for finite primes. Replace it by S together with all archimedean places in this application; this leaves his allowed Galois group unchanged. The finite set must contain all p-adic places, as in his global setup. For these uses F is totally real and p=5. No crystalline or finite-flat hypothesis appears in this Euler formula.

A published alternative is Milne, Arithmetic Duality Theorems, second edition 2006, I.4.10(c), I.4.15 and I.5.1, printed pp. 57, 62 and 67. The author's version page authenticates the electronic file as the file sent to the publisher apart from the copyright page. His p. 67 footnote explicitly distinguishes ordinary H⁰ at real places from Tate H⁰. This distinction matters here.

The following derivation supplies the finite-to-p-adic connection for p=5. Choose a G-stable Z_5-lattice L in the underlying Q_5-vector space of V, and put M_n=L/5^nL. Such a lattice exists because the continuous image of the compact group G is compact. Milne I.4.15 makes every H^i(G,M_n) finite; I.4.10(c) makes them zero for i≥3, since cohomology of a group of order two on a 5-primary module vanishes in positive degree. The inverse systems of these finite groups satisfy the Mittag-Leffler condition. Thus continuous cohomology of L is their inverse limit, without a derived-limit term.

The groups H^i(G,L) are compact pro-5 Z_5-modules. The exact sequence for multiplication by five injects H^i(G,L)/5 into the finite group H^i(G,L/5L). It follows that H^i(G,L) is finitely generated: lift finitely many generators of this quotient, let N be their closed Z_5-span, and observe that the compact pro-5 quotient C=H^i/N satisfies C=5C. Every finite quotient of C is then 5-divisible and a finite 5-group, hence zero, so C=0. The span of finitely many lifts is already compact and closed.

The exact sequences

`0 → H^i(G,L)/5^n → H^i(G,M_n) → H^(i+1)(G,L)[5^n] → 0`

give `log_5 |H^i(G,M_n)| = n rank_(Z_5) H^i(G,L)+O(1)`, with a bound independent of n, since all torsion subgroups of these finitely generated modules are finite. Continuous cochains with values in V are the union of the complexes with values in 5^(-r)L: compactness of each domain G^j bounds every continuous cochain. Filtered unions are exact, so H^i(G,V)=H^i(G,L)[1/5]. At a real place averaging by `(1+c)/2` is integral on L; consequently `(L/5^nL)^(G_v)=L^(G_v)/5^n`, exactly.

Apply Milne I.5.1 to M_n. Its denominator is `|M_n|^[F:Q]` for totally real F, and its numerator is the product of ordinary real-place invariant orders. Take log_5, divide by n, and let n tend to infinity with the chosen F, S, E, V and L fixed. This gives the displayed Euler formula over Q_5; division by [E:Q_5] gives it over E. It also gives H^i(G,V)=0 for i≥3. No uniform bound as V varies is needed or claimed. This is a proposed published-source substitute for just Kisin Lemma 9.7 at five, not for his Proposition 9.5.

For Pan 7.1.3 take V=ad⁰ rho_E. Oddness diagonalizes complex conjugation with eigenvalues +1 and -1. Its centralizer in trace-zero matrices is the one-dimensional diagonal trace-zero space. Global invariant endomorphisms are scalar: an endomorphism commuting with complex conjugation is diagonal in that E-rational eigenbasis; if its two entries differ, its eigenspaces are G-stable, contradicting irreducibility. Its trace-zero scalar part is zero in characteristic zero. Therefore h0=0, dim V=3 and the real-place sum is [F:Q], giving h1-h2=2[F:Q]. This supplies the numerical input to Pan's deformation-ring presentation. It does not supply that presentation or its passage from pseudorepresentations.

For Pan 7.4.15 take V=E(psi2/psi1). The ratio is totally odd, hence its real invariants and global invariants vanish. Its dimension is one, so h1-h2=[F:Q], and h1≥[F:Q]. This gives only the lower bound. Pan's asserted equality and local restriction isomorphism still use his separate Lichtenbaum, Poitou–Tate, restriction/corestriction and local-cohomology arguments. This packet does not prove those arguments or their extension to the enlarged set S.

## Characteristic-zero completion, PAN-15

Kisin Proposition 9.5 is not an unconditional isomorphism. In his notation the map is `R^u_(V_E)→Rhat⊗_(E1)E`, where E1 is the residue field of Rhat. It is formally smooth under scalar endomorphisms of V_E, and an isomorphism when the residual versal deformation ring is universal. His setup uses W(k)-algebras and unrestricted determinant. Pan uses O-algebras and fixed determinant chi. These differences require the following translation.

First check universality on the actual objects. Pan 7.4.16 chooses a nonzero extension class B, giving `0→psi2→rho_B→psi1→0`. The two characters differ because their ratio is totally odd. Any endomorphism preserves the unique psi2 subrepresentation. On the subrepresentation and quotient it acts by scalars a and d; compatibility with the nonzero extension class gives `(a-d)B=0`, so a=d. The possible remaining map from quotient to subrepresentation is zero because their characters differ. Hence End_G(rho_B)=E. The chosen stable lattice in 7.4.18 has nonsplit residual extension with distinct residual characters, as witnessed by their values +1 and -1 at complex conjugation in characteristic five. The same argument gives residual endomorphism ring k. Pan's standard representability input therefore gives universal, not merely versal, deformation rings. A scalar or split residual representation would require a different conclusion.

Second, the fixed determinant is an equality of functions on each representation and is unchanged under conjugation or base change. Impose it by the closed ideal generated by `det rho_univ(g)-chi(g)` for g∈G. In any Noetherian representing ring that ideal is finitely generated and closed. An unrestricted isomorphism identifying the actual universal representation up to strict equivalence identifies these determinant ideals. Quotienting, then completing at the specified characteristic-zero point, gives the fixed-determinant isomorphism. No flatness of this quotient is required.

Third, one can run the compact-lattice proof of Kisin 9.5 directly over O, rather than silently replacing O by W(k). Here is that argument for the universal case. Let A be a local Artinian E-algebra with residue E and nilpotent maximal ideal m, with its finite-dimensional E-vector-space topology. For a continuous deformation of rho_B to A, choose a basis lifting the chosen basis of the stable lattice. There is a sequence of finite O-subalgebras A_n of A with residue field k, whose union is O+m. To construct them choose a full O-lattice J in m closed under multiplication, set A_0=O+J, and put `A_n=O+sum_(j≥1) pi^(-nj)J^j`. The sum is finite because m is nilpotent. These are increasing, closed under multiplication, finite O-modules, and open in O+m. Every element of m lies in their union. Inverting pi in any A_n gives A.

The matrices of the deformation and their inverses lie in O+m, because their reductions in E are the chosen integral representation and its inverse. Compactness of G puts all their entries in one A_n. Reduction to k is exactly the chosen residual representation, so residual universality gives a continuous O-map R_b→A_n. It sends elements outside p_B to units in A and sends p_B into the nilpotent ideal m. Therefore it extends uniquely to `(R_b)_(p_B)` and to its p_B-adic completion. The resulting representation over A is the given characteristic-zero deformation. Its determinant remains chi throughout.

For uniqueness, two maps from the completion to A inducing the same characteristic-zero strict-equivalence class have restrictions R_b→A with bounded images: use topological finite generation of R_b and the nilpotence of m to contain all images in some A_n. A conjugating matrix reducing to the identity in GL_2(E), and its inverse, lie in a sufficiently large GL_2(A_n) and reduce to the identity in GL_2(k). Thus residual universality makes the two maps into A_n equal. Their extensions to localization and completion are equal. This proves that the completion represents the same fixed-determinant functor as R_B, and proves the natural isomorphism by Yoneda.

The coefficient embedding O→R_b ensures that the residue field at the given characteristic-zero point contains E=Frac(O); its image is already a subfield of E, so it equals E. Thus no unrecorded E1-to-E extension remains in this application. Also p is invertible at p_B, so completing R_b at p_B agrees with completing R_b[1/p] at that prime. The topology on the Artinian test rings is the finite-dimensional E-topology, not the discrete topology.

This proof retains Pan's chosen nonsplit stable lattice and the representability of the residual and characteristic-zero functors. It does not prove the existence of that particular lattice or any global modularity theorem. The construction applies to the actual B-family one B at a time, with no asserted uniform denominator bound across B. Its immediate downstream uses are 7.4.20 and the later transfers between components of R_B and R_b. Their dimension and connectedness inputs remain separate.
