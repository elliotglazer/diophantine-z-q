# Integral real period lattice and the Kummer line

Let E=E_l^h be the actual quadratic twist, with l nonzero and h nonzero. The base curve y^2=x(x-l)(x+3l) has full rational two-torsion. The twist can be written y^2=x(x-hl)(x+3hl), so its three nonidentity points of order two remain rational and distinct. In particular complex conjugation acts trivially on E[2], including for negative h. No assertion about other real elliptic curves is used.

Choose a nonzero invariant differential defined over R. The complex period map P -> integral_O^P omega identifies E(C), as a complex Lie group, with C/Lambda, where Lambda is the full rank-two period lattice (Silverman VI.5.1.1 and VI.5.2). Conjugating a path conjugates its integral, because omega is defined over R. Thus Lambda is conjugation-stable and this identification is equivariant for complex conjugation J, acting on C by z -> conjugate(z). The same torsion identification is E[2^n]=(2^(-n)Lambda)/Lambda.

Under E[2]=Lambda/2Lambda, trivial conjugation means (J-1)Lambda is contained in2Lambda. Therefore both (1+J)/2 and (1-J)/2 preserve Lambda. They are complementary idempotents, giving the integral direct sum Lambda=Lambda^+ direct sum Lambda^-. Their real spans are respectively R and iR, since Lambda spans C over R; hence both summands have rank one. Choose a real generator omega_+ and a purely imaginary generator omega_-. They form an actual Z-basis of Lambda. This proves the rectangular lattice integrally, not merely a decomposition of its rational span. Passing to inverse limits identifies T_2(E)=Lambda tensor Z_2 with the same direct sum and J=diag(1,-1).

Write a point of C/Lambda as x omega_+ + y omega_-, with x,y real modulo integers. It is fixed by conjugation exactly when2y is an integer. Hence E(R) consists of two circles, y=0 and y=1/2. The first is the identity component. Multiplication by2^n is surjective on that circle and kills the component group. For every n>=1, E(R)/2^nE(R) is therefore Z/2, generated by P=[omega_-/2].

For this generator take Q=[omega_-/2^(n+1)], so2^nQ=P. Its Kummer cocycle sends J to JQ-Q=[-omega_-/2^n]. In the basis of E[2^n] induced by omega_+,omega_-, this is the vector(0,-1). The cyclic order-two cochain complex gives H^1(R,E[2^n])=ker(1+J)/im(J-1). Its plus component is (Z/2^n)[2], and its minus component is (Z/2^n)/2. The cocycle is the nonzero generator of the minus component. Thus the real finite Kummer image is exactly the minus line, not the plus line.

Put T^+=Z_2 omega_+, T^-=Z_2 omega_-. The ordinary cochain complex for conjugation begins T --(J-1)--> T --(J+1)--> T. Its truncation in degrees0 and1 is U_R=[T^+ direct sum T^- --(J-1)--> T^-]. All its terms are free over Z_2. Reduction modulo2^n therefore has H^0=T^+/2^n direct sum (T^-/2^n)[2], exactly E(R)[2^n], and H^1=(T^-/2^n)/2, mapping to the minus-line Kummer subgroup just computed. Ordinary degree-zero invariants have been retained. Replacing U_R by E(R)^wedge_2[-1] would lose the T^+/2^n summand.

The finite elliptic Weil pairing vanishes on pairs from the cyclic minus subgroup: each pair consists of multiples of one vector and the pairing is alternating. This makes the minus line isotropic for the cup pairing. The separately supplied finite real Tate duality is perfect, and H^1(R,E[2^n]) has order4. Its isotropic subgroup of order2 is therefore its own annihilator. This last step uses the accepted finite-duality input; it does not prove that theorem.

The conclusion supplies precisely the real lattice, real Kummer image and ordinary local complex used at main14247–14265. The compact/discrete global limit, corank-zero assumption and subsequent determinant cancellation remain in their separate accepted arguments.
