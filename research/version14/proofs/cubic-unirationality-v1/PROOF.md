# The published characteristic-zero cubic input

Author proposal for the current degree obstruction; independent review is pending.
This is a source-to-consumer connection using a published theorem. It is not a
new proof of the entire classical unirationality theorem.

## 1. Available published assertion

Let k have characteristic zero, and let X in P^n_k, n >= 3, be a geometrically
integral cubic hypersurface which is not a cone. If X has a k-rational point,
then X is k-unirational. The source is Colliot-Thelene--Salberger, Arithmetic on
some singular cubic hypersurfaces, Proc. London Math. Soc. (3)58 (1989),519--549,
Section1, Proposition1.3, printed521. The ground-field condition is stated at
the start of Section1 on520; the standing geometric-integrality/noncone
conditions are explicitly restated on521. The ambient dimension n>=3 is the
cubic-hypersurface range in the introduction and preceding Proposition1.1.
Our dimension restriction is retained throughout; this is not a statement
about plane cubic curves.

The same source's Proposition1.2 handles a singular k-point by projection.
Proposition1.3 explicitly divides its proof into two cases. If X is singular
in codimension1, its argument identifies a linear maximal singular locus,
defined over k, and concludes rationality. If X is regular in codimension1,
it invokes Colliot-Thelene--Sansuc--Swinnerton-Dyer, Intersections of two
quadrics and Chatelet surfaces I, J. reine angew. Math.373 (1987),37--107,
Remark2.3.1 on printed56. That remark explicitly applies the preceding
characteristic-zero hyperplane-pencil proof to cubic hypersurfaces in P^n,
n>=3. Thus no smoothness hypothesis is inserted into the source application.

The 1987 proof and its references are a retained published dependency chain,
recorded in PREDECESSORS.json. In particular its reference29 was then a
Coray--Tsfasman preprint, later published in PLMS57 (1988),25--87. We do not
claim a preprint/final statement comparison for that work. We invoke the
explicit final1989 Proposition1.3, not an unpublished assertion as a newly
verified independent premise. A recursive verification of every classical
ancestor is outside this bounded source-substitution claim.

## 2. Exact object constructed by the manuscript

The current argument homogenizes its irreducible degree-three polynomial to
H on a rational vector space E. It first excludes geometric reducibility by
using the three conjugate hyperplane factors and two rational zeros with
inputs0 and1. It therefore reaches the geometrically integral case.

It defines W={v in E: D_v H=0 identically}. Characteristic zero implies that
these are precisely the directions along which H is translation invariant.
Choose a rational complement E' of W. In coordinates E=W direct-sum E', H
is a polynomial only in E'. Consequently it descends to Hbar on E/W. If
Hbar factored over the algebraic closure, the same factorization would factor
H. Thus Hbar is still geometrically integral and has degree3.

The linear map v -> D_v H has rational coefficients. Its kernel commutes
with field extension. By maximality of W, the induced cubic has no nonzero
vertex direction even over the algebraic closure. This is the noncone
condition of the published proposition. Concretely a projective cone with
vertex [v] can be expressed in coordinates with the defining homogeneous
polynomial independent of the vertex coordinate; it then has D_v Hbar=0.
Conversely such independence displays the cone. For the forward implication,
the hypersurface ideal is generated by its irreducible cubic; translating
along the vertex preserves it, and the resulting degree2 directional
derivative must vanish identically, rather than merely modulo that cubic.

The existing argument also proves W is killed by the homogenizing and input
linear forms X0 and X1: otherwise translating a rational zero along a vertex
direction gives a nonconstant fractional linear function of a rational
parameter, whose cofinite rational image cannot lie in Z. Applying this at
inputs0 and1 gives v0=v1=0 for every v in W. Hence X0 and X1 descend.
A rational linear section of E -> E/W preserves both forms and lifts every
point of Hbar=0 to H=0. The input projection on X0=1 is unchanged.

## 3. Applying the source and obtaining the contradiction

The relevant branch has dim Xbar>=2, so its ambient projective dimension is
n>=3. It is over Q, a characteristic-zero field. It is geometrically integral,
nonconical over the algebraic closure, and has a rational point on X0=1.
These are exactly the hypotheses of the published proposition. Its rational
point need not be declared smooth: the source includes singular points.
We obtain a dominant rational map P^m_Q --> Xbar for some m.

The function f=X1/X0 on Xbar is nonconstant, since its rational values on that
chart include every integer under the contradiction hypothesis. Dominance
injects Q(Xbar) into Q(P^m); therefore the composite f remains nonconstant.
The nonempty domain where the map and f are defined contains a nonempty
rational affine principal open subset. The manuscript's preceding real-ball
lemma shows that a nonconstant rational function on this domain cannot take
only integer values at its rational points. This is the required contradiction.
No density of Xbar(Q) in its real points is asserted or needed.

The dimension-one and dimension-zero cases remain separate and unchanged.
In particular this application does not cover a cone over a plane cubic,
a plane cubic itself, finite ground fields, or positive characteristic.
No quantitative constant, polynomial degree, height estimate or input range
changes. The only theorem imported here is a characteristic-zero theorem
already sufficient for the actual use over Q.
