# Exact source and correction checks

Silverman is the final second edition, Springer 2009, not an author preprint. Its pinned local copy is the same file used by the root draft. Printed page numbers, rather than an assumed constant PDF offset, identify the statements. III.6.1(a) is printed p.81, III.6.2(a),(d),(e) p.83, and VII.5.5 pp.197 and 199. The statement and the odd-residue proof of VII.5.5, the dual identities and the multiplication-degree statement were read; the selected pages were also rendered and inspected.

The official April 2, 2015 errata was read at PDF pp.7 and 21, with the related Appendix entry on p.32. Page 7 corrects the Picard pullback domain on book p.80, the omitted name of the isogeny in III.6.1, the characteristic-zero versus Frobenius case split, the reference on p.82, and the minus sign in the divisor calculation on p.84. None changes the dual identities or degree formula used here. We retain the corrected direction E2 to E1 for the dual. Page 21 corrects the neighboring example on p.197 and replaces the field K by K' on p.198. It does not alter VII.5.5. The Appendix correction replaces char(K) by char(k); our proof uses residue characteristic q explicitly and does not use the characteristic-two Appendix argument.

The official October 13, 2022 errata covers the second printing and says that corrections incorporated in that printing are omitted; it therefore does not replace the 2015 list. The entries for III.6.1(a) and pp.83–84 were checked. In particular, the dual is defined over the original field by uniqueness and Galois invariance, which is incorporated explicitly in Section 3. Corrections to the III.6.2(c) divisor proof are not claims that the multiplication-degree identity fails in positive characteristic. The statement of III.6.2(d) is used at the special fiber. The nearby pp.197–199 entries concern VII.5.4 and the Neron–Ogg–Shafarevich proof; the latter is not used to obtain potential good reduction here. No blanket absence of other corrections is claimed.

The BLR source is the university-hosted scan of the published Springer 1990 book. Definition 1.2/1 is read with its Dedekind-base convention; Proposition 1.2/8 and its proof give the full smooth-source mapping property, including uniqueness. Proposition 1.4/2 supplies the group structure on the common smooth proper models. Its printed hypotheses and the preceding setup were read visually. The published Weil extension theorem used within BLR's proof is retained as a transitive foundation, not presented as independently reproved here. Silverman's section-extension overview is not substituted for the full BLR statement.

The EGA sources are the original published French volumes downloaded from Numdam. EGA IV4 17.11.2(c') uses the cotangent map after residue-field extension; unlike its optional tangent-space version (d), it does not require k(x)=k(y). EGA IV4 17.6.1–2 gives the local quasi-finiteness of etale maps. EGA III1 4.4.11 applies because the target elliptic scheme is locally noetherian, and 4.4.2 was also read. EGA IV4 18.2.3 and 18.2.7 give finite local freeness, so degree constancy is derived from connectedness in the proof. EGA IV4 18.5.14–15 gives completeness-to-henselianity and the finite-etale residue equivalence. The displayed criteria and the relevant surrounding proofs were read; pages 84, 134 and III p.138 were inspected visually. The original printed cross-references to earlier EGA errata are preserved, including the local quasi-finiteness formulation in 17.6.2.

Bounded public searches for corrections to the selected EGA and BLR interfaces did not locate a relevant notice. This is a search record, not proof that no correction exists. No mathematical conclusion relies on third-party translated EGA text, Stack Exchange, search-result snippets or an unversioned online foundations theorem. The new scheme-theoretic inputs are supplied by the pinned published EGA statements.

The source audit does not compare final Cornut–Jetchev or Jetchev–Kane editions. It does not establish CM integrality, the characteristic-zero ideal action, inert supersingularity, reciprocity, or the enhanced supersingular class-set and Frobenius input.
