# Good reduction and specialization of labelled CM ideal isogenies

Author: `/root/astra_v14_assertions_arithmetic`, completing the separate root draft pinned in SOURCES.json. This is a new author successor awaiting independent review. The original draft is unchanged. The installed manuscript and portable CJ002 proposal remain separate targets; this file changes neither.

## 1. Domain and conclusion

Let O be the maximal order of an imaginary quadratic field F. Let q be an odd prime inert in F, and let N be positive with q not dividing N. In the conductor-one application, N is also prime to disc(F) and every prime of N splits in F. Fix the reduction place and the labelled CM action iota:O -> End(A). Consider a finite diagram of CM elliptic curves in the given ideal-class orbit, their labelled O-actions, their selected O-stable cyclic level subgroups, and ideal isogenies for nonzero integral ideals a of O prime to qN. Set d=|O/a|. The characteristic-zero ideal-action input supplies A[a], its quotient B=A/A[a], the O-equivariant isogeny f of degree d, and its level subgroup f(C). That ideal-action construction is retained, not proved here.

Assume the separately supplied integrality of every j-invariant in this finite diagram. We prove that, after one finite extension of the local field of definition, the curves have common good elliptic models, their labelled actions and isogenies extend, and reduction is injective on O. The special isogeny has degree d and kernel precisely the simultaneous a-kernel of the specialized action. It realizes the ideal quotient, carries the selected level subgroup to its specialized image, and preserves the labelled level eigensummands and the q-residue orientation. On the reduced curves it induces an isomorphism of q-divisible groups.

The field extension may depend on the finite diagram. No finite field containing all torsion of all orders or all possible ideal isogenies is asserted. No supersingularity, CM reciprocity, class-set realization, Frobenius formula or reduction-surjectivity theorem is proved by this supplement.

## 2. Common good models

Start with a finite extension of Q_q over which all curves, the finitely many arrows and level subgroups are defined. Include a finite Z-generating set of each labelled O-action. Since O is generated by two elements as a Z-module, this includes every endomorphism in the action. Each of these finitely many algebraic data is defined over a finite extension.

Silverman VII.5.5, second edition, printed pp.197 and 199, gives potentially good reduction from integral j. Its definition requires a finite extension. Take the compositum of the finitely many good-reduction extensions. Base change preserves the smooth proper models, so they all exist over the ring of integers R of one finite extension K/Q_q. The ring R is a complete noetherian DVR. Its residue characteristic is q, including the allowed case q=3. BLR 1.4/2, printed p.19, equips each smooth proper model with the extending abelian-scheme structure. Thus the models A and B below are elliptic schemes over Spec(R).

For this use Silverman's proof at odd residue characteristic is explicit: after a finite extension put the curve in Legendre form. The integral-j equation has leading coefficient a unit, so its root lambda is integral; reduction of that equation excludes lambda=0,1. Its discriminant is then a unit. The proof only excludes residue characteristic 2. The 2015 errata corrections on printed pp.197–198 concern the neighboring example and the extension field in VII.5.4; the later errata's Neron–Ogg–Shafarevich correction is not an input to this Legendre proof. SOURCE-CHECKS.md records the exact checks.

## 3. Extending maps and retaining the action

BLR Definition 1.2/1 and Proposition 1.2/8, printed pp.12 and 15, say that an abelian scheme over this Dedekind base has the Neron mapping property for every smooth source. Apply this with source A and target B. Any generic-fiber homomorphism f_K extends uniquely to an R-morphism f:A -> B.

It is a group homomorphism. The two maps A x_R A -> B expressing addition compatibility have the same generic restriction; A x_R A is smooth, so uniqueness gives equality. The zero-section and inverse compatibilities follow in the same way. Applying uniqueness again to composites shows that extension respects composition, addition and identities. In particular the labelled O-actions extend as ring actions, and every O-equivariance relation of the diagram survives. The conclusion uses the full smooth-source mapping property, not merely extension of sections. BLR's proof uses its published Weil extension theorem 4.4/1; that transitive foundational input remains identified.

For a nonzero alpha in O, its generic endomorphism is nonconstant. Silverman III.6.1 and III.6.2(a) supply its dual with dual(alpha) alpha=[m], where m=deg(alpha)>0. The dual is defined over K: uniqueness makes it invariant under every K-Galois automorphism, as the official 2022 errata also explains. Extend both maps and the identity by BLR. If alpha specialized to zero, [m] would be zero on the special elliptic curve. This contradicts III.6.2(d), which gives deg[m]=m^2 in every characteristic. In particular m need not be prime to q for this injectivity argument. Since specialization respects subtraction, distinct elements of O stay distinct.

## 4. Finite etale torsion and specialization

Let n be a positive integer prime to q. On Lie(A/R), the differential of [n] is multiplication by n. This follows directly by differentiating the group addition at the identity: its two restrictions are the identity, hence its tangent map is addition. Iteration gives n times the identity; inversion gives the negative case if needed. Translation carries the identity calculation to every geometric point.

The schemes A and A are smooth and locally of finite presentation over R. The induced map on relative cotangent spaces is an isomorphism, since n is an R-unit. EGA IV4, Corollary 17.11.2(c'), printed p.84, therefore makes [n] etale. This cotangent formulation does not impose an equality of residue fields at arbitrary points. The map is proper: its graph is closed because the target is R-separated, and the projection A x_R A -> A is proper by base change. An etale morphism is locally quasi-finite; here it is quasi-compact, so quasi-finite. EGA III1, Corollary 4.4.11, printed p.138, makes this proper quasi-finite map finite. Its target is locally noetherian, as required. Pulling back the zero section proves that A[n] is finite etale over R.

EGA IV4, Proposition 18.5.14 gives henselianity of R; Proposition 18.5.15 identifies finite etale algebras over a henselian local ring with those over its residue field. Over the strict henselization R^sh, finite etale schemes are therefore constant finite schemes: the residue field is separably closed and a finite etale algebra over that field is a product of copies of it. Fix a compatible geometric generic point and geometric special point. Each component Spec(R^sh) has one point over each, giving the specialization bijection on torsion. Full faithfulness makes this identification commute with every map between the finite etale schemes, in particular all labelled endomorphisms. This is a geometric-point comparison after fixing the place, not an assertion about rational torsion over the original K.

For any finite collection of endomorphisms acting on A[n], their simultaneous kernel is a union of components after this base change, and taking the special fiber selects exactly the same components. Thus simultaneous kernels commute with the comparison. No statement of this kind is being made for q-primary torsion, which need not be etale.

## 5. The actual ideal quotient

Let a and f_K:A_K -> B_K be as in Section 1, with degree d prime to qN. Extend f_K and its dual by Section 3. The two identities

    f^dual f=[d] on A,       f f^dual=[d] on B

hold over R. On the rank-one tangent modules their product is multiplication by the unit d, so the differential of f is an isomorphism. Translation and the same EGA argument make f finite etale. Its kernel is finite etale over R and is contained in A[d].

EGA IV4, Proposition 18.2.3 and Remark 18.2.7(i), printed pp.112–113, make f finite locally free. Its rank is locally constant on B. The target B is connected: its generic fiber is connected, and any nonempty open-and-closed part of B has open image in Spec(R), by smoothness, and hence meets the generic fiber. Thus the rank is the same d on every fiber. The special isogeny is consequently nonconstant, finite etale and of degree d; it is surjective because its finite locally free rank is positive everywhere.

The finite abelian group O/a has order d, so d belongs to a. Choose a finite Z-generating set alpha_1,...,alpha_r for a. Every simultaneous a-kernel is thus already contained in the d-torsion. All alpha_i extend by Section 3. Inside the constant finite group A[d] over R^sh, let S be their simultaneous kernel. The characteristic-zero ideal-action input identifies S_K with ker(f_K). Both S and ker(f) are unions of components of A[d], so equality on their geometric generic fibers implies equality of those component subsets. Their special fibers are equal. Therefore

    ker(f_bar) = A_bar[a]

as finite subgroup schemes, with the original labelled action. This is stronger than equality of numerical cardinalities.

For the quotient assertion no new general construction of quotients of abelian schemes is needed. The map f_bar is a finite etale surjective homomorphism. The map A_bar x ker(f_bar) -> A_bar x_(B_bar) A_bar sending (x,s) to (x,x+s) is an isomorphism, with inverse (x,y) -> (x,y-x). Thus f_bar is the torsor quotient by its kernel in the fppf sheaf sense, and B_bar represents that quotient. It is precisely the ideal quotient required here. O-equivariance follows from the extended relations, and base change carries the image f(C) to the image of C_bar.

## 6. Level and q-orientation

Because gcd(d,N)=1, choose integers u,v with ud+vN=1. The map [u]f^dual is the inverse of f on N-torsion on both sides. This proves an isomorphism of the finite etale N-torsion schemes, not just an injection of point sets. It is O-equivariant and commutes with specialization. It therefore sends the selected labelled O-stable cyclic subgroup C to the selected image and preserves each labelled eigensummand for the split primes dividing N. Repeated prime powers in N cause no change to this argument.

On the special curves, multiplication by d is an automorphism of the q-divisible group. More explicitly, choose the compatible residues of d^(-1) in Z_q/q^r. At level q^r the inverse to f_bar is multiplication by this residue followed by f_bar^dual. The two dual identities prove both inverse equations; compatibility of the residues proves compatibility with the transition maps. Thus the induced map on the q-divisible groups is an isomorphism, without incorrectly treating their torsion as etale.

The tangent differential of f_bar is nonzero. The O-action on the one-dimensional tangent line gives the residue embedding O/qO -> k_bar: it is a unital ring homomorphism, it kills q, and O/qO is a field because q is inert. Differentiating O-equivariance and cancelling the nonzero differential shows that the two scalar actions agree for every labelled alpha in O. This preserves the same residue embedding, not its conjugate. These are exactly the compatibility inputs requested by the separate enhanced Tate/Dieudonne class-set argument. This supplement does not identify that class set or its Frobenius action.

## 7. Scope and related uses

The result supplies common models and the prime-to-q specialization functor in retained inputs 2–3 of the conductor-one proposal. It applies one finite diagram at a time and remains compatible when diagrams are enlarged, by uniqueness of extension. The selected N-level and tangent orientations are both checked because the downstream argument uses both. Applying the common-model step to every member of the finite class orbit does not prove a bijection of reduction fibers.

CM j-integrality, the characteristic-zero ideal action and its reciprocity convention, the forward inert-prime supersingularity theorem, the enhanced supersingular class-set realization, its local Tate/Dieudonne descriptions and Frobenius, and the rational embedding/local-counting input remain separate. The unit restriction and other global hypotheses in the conductor-one target are not removed. Source publication checks here concern the exact Silverman, BLR and EGA interfaces, not final-version equivalence of Cornut–Jetchev or Jetchev–Kane. The accompanying contracts and source pins are author records for independent review.
