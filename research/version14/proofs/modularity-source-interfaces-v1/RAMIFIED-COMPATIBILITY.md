# Ramified and p-place compatibility in the actual H stages

Author: /root/astra_v14_assertions_arithmetic. New bounded source/application proposal; independent review pending. This is not an extension of the already reviewed U04 attachment conclusion by inference. It invokes a distinct published theorem for the stronger statement.

## Published statement and actual objects

Christopher Skinner, A note on the p-adic Galois representations attached to Hilbert modular forms, Documenta Mathematica 14 (2009), 241-258, DOI10.4171/DM/272, final p.242, equation(1), gives the Frobenius-semisimple Weil-Deligne comparison at every finite place away from p. Theorem1 on the same page gives potential semistability, Hodge-Tate type and the same comparison at places over p. The standing hypotheses on p.241 are a totally real F, a cuspidal pi with weights k_i>=2, common w, and k_i congruent to w modulo2. There is no p>2 hypothesis and no requirement for a finite discrete-series place. Page243 expressly separates the earlier Saito cases from the remaining parallel-weight-two case. The printed theorem on p.242 was inspected visually.

At every H intermediate field, the modular form has parallel weight two and finite-order central character in unitary normalization. Thus k_i=2 and w=0 satisfy these hypotheses. This is the same normalization used in the U04 attachment packet. In this note the H representation being compared is only the already attached modular comparison representation rho_f or rho_g, not the target rho whose modularity is to be proved. Put r_pi=rho_pi^vee in the H Tate convention: on arithmetic Frobenius its polynomial is the source polynomial for rho_pi on geometric Frobenius. The attached modular comparison representation has these same Hecke polynomials at almost all places. Its absolute irreducibility follows from the established residual nonsolvability. The retained characteristic-zero character criterion therefore identifies rho_f or rho_g with r_pi. Residual equality with the target rho is not a characteristic-zero identification. The target has its separate local properties from the KW system and H hypotheses; none is inferred here by assuming its modularity.

## What the comparison supplies

Functoriality of the Weil-Deligne construction under duality transports the source's full pair (Weil action,N), not just its trace. Frobenius semisimplification keeps inertia and N. Duality preserves triviality of inertia, N=0, and N nonzero. Skinner displays the two graded D_HT indices (w-k_i)/2 and (w+k_i-2)/2; at k_i=2 they differ by one. Duality reverses both indices and preserves that gap. We do not identify these raw graded indices with the covariant convention used by the BT input. Instead use the actual H determinant det(r_pi)=psi*chi, with psi finite and chi the cyclotomic character. In the covariant convention chi has weight +1 and a finite character has weight0. At each local embedding the two weights of r_pi are integers differing by one and their sum is1, so they are exactly0 and1. This determinant calculation is the extra input fixing the sign and common shift. It does not follow from an unramified WD trace alone.

Consequently, a spherical pi_v gives trivial inertial type and N=0. At v away from p this makes r_pi unramified. At v over p the following explicit Fontaine descent gives the needed stronger property. Choose a finite Galois L/K over which r_pi is semistable. Its D_st over L has dimension equal to the representation dimension over the maximal unramified field L_0, and carries the semilinear Gal(L/K) descent action. Trivial inertia in D_pst says precisely that the inertia subgroup acts trivially on this module. The action therefore factors through Gal(L_0/K_0); finite semilinear Galois descent gives an invariant K_0-module of the same rank. These invariants are D_st,K(r_pi), by the definitions inside B_st. Hence r_pi is semistable over K. Fontaine final Expose III,4.2.5-6 pp146-147 gives this module descent, and5.1.4-7 pp156-158 gives the period-module definitions and dimension criterion. By5.1.6, D_cris is the kernel of N on D_st; if N=0, it has full dimension and r_pi is crystalline. Conversely, a nonzero N rules out crystallinity. This uses potential semistability from Theorem1 in addition to the WD equality. The already reviewed H-D2 integral comparison then turns the actual crystalline weight0,1 lattice into the Tate module of a p-divisible group over the actual local valuation ring. More explicitly, forget coefficients on the given O_E lattice, apply Kim2012 Theorems2.3(2) and4.1, and upgrade the integral comparison from the uniformizer-root tower by crystalline full faithfulness as checked in h-crystalline-weight-review-05. No replacement by a more convenient lattice or descent of that group through a ramified field extension is asserted. This is the needed BT implication; it is not supplied by U04's almost-everywhere statement alone.

For an unramified Steinberg twist the parameter has trivial finite inertia and N nonzero. The same comparison yields semistability with N nonzero at p and the corresponding nontrivial monodromy away from p. The ordinary structure and exact unramified character gamma in Kisin's chosen extension condition remain the separate local statements used in Lemma3.3.1 and Proposition3.2.9; this note does not infer gamma from a trace or forget the fixed determinant.

For a ramified principal series, the finite inertial characters have open kernels and N=0. For a cuspidal GL2 type the irreducible Weil parameter also has N=0 and finite inertia. A finite local extension can kill that finite inertia. Restrictions then have N=0 and trivial inertia, and the same Fontaine criterion gives crystallinity at p. At non-p places it gives unramifiedness. The previously reviewed H-D4 field theorem realizes the simultaneous local fields. BT and trivial residual action, once achieved, survive further restriction.

This explains precisely the p-place BT and non-p finite-inertia consequences needed in the staged H argument. It does not by itself prove that an automorphic base change has the restricted local Langlands parameter. That local base-change compatibility remains an independent automorphic theorem input. Nor does it replace the ordinary/cuspidal type-change lemmas by a generic potential-semistability assertion. In particular, the assertion that the chosen comparison form has the required ordinary/nonordinary status is still supplied by those lemmas and the retained BT geometry.

## Actual consumers and adverse cases

Final Kisin3.3.1, pp.630-631, explicitly uses trivial p-type to get a p-divisible group and special p-type to get semistable weights0,1. Its Q2 and reducible-residual hypotheses are preserved by the early splitting stage. Lemma3.3.2, p.631, changes specified trivial/Steinberg coefficient types to cuspidal types; it does not state arbitrary prescribed-component existence. Lemma3.3.4, p.632, produces ramified principal series outside Sigma, so the subsequent finite-inertia-killing extension is required. The revised order leading to3.2.9 applies the determinant normalization before matching Steinberg characters.

If Sigma is empty and the intermediate field has even degree, Saito's finite-discrete-series hypothesis cannot be invented. The full published Skinner Theorem1 supplies the p-place comparison in this case too. When Sigma is nonempty, the separately accepted F01 proof already uses Saito/Blasius for its exact purity and unique-component argument; nothing here reopens it. The old p=5 newness packet is not applied wholesale at p=2.

This is a new immediate source application of the published statement, not a new proof of Skinner's theorem. Its proof references (including Kisin finite-slope/deformation results, symmetric-square and interpolation inputs) remain published-theorem ancestry, not silently checked by this note. No identified correction to a different Breuil-Mezard statement is transported to this theorem without an actual dependency comparison. The local base-change theorem and ordinary-type arguments retain their own precise scope.

## Coefficient and monodromy conventions retained

Fix the one complex-to-p-adic coefficient embedding iota used by U04 for the chosen eigensystem. The source comparison, its dual, and the Hecke comparison use that same embedding. For finitely many base changes the finite coefficient fields can be replaced by their compositum. The Fontaine modules may be checked after forgetting E coefficients or after extending scalars; the dimension criterion and inertia-triviality statement are unchanged. No embedding is selected independently at different places to force the conclusion.

The standard Frobenius-semisimplification of a WD pair changes its Frobenius semisimple part; it leaves its inertial action and N in the same isomorphism class. On a dual the monodromy is minus the transpose, so its vanishing is unchanged. Fontaine5.1.7(ii),p158, explicitly identifies the dual period module. A normalization scaling N by a nonzero number changes neither N=0 nor N nonzero;5.2.1-3,pp158-159, records that scaling. These facts justify the uses above while making no claim to recover a stronger unsemisimplified Frobenius operator. At non-p places the ordinary WD construction identifies a representation with finite-inertia part and exponential monodromy; therefore trivial finite inertia together with N=0 gives actual unramifiedness. Trivial finite inertia alone does not.
