# An ordered modular entry in the exceptional characteristic-three case

Author-frozen proposal, pending independent review. This proof supplies only the modular entry required before Skinner--Wiles (2001), Theorem 5.1, in the exceptional branch of the proof of Theorem 5.2, printed page 204. It does not use the conclusion of either modularity-lifting theorem.

## 1. Exact input and output

Let k be a finite field of characteristic 3 and let r:G_Q -> GL_2(k) be continuous, absolutely irreducible and odd. Put K=Q(sqrt(-3)), and suppose r restricted to G_K is reducible. Assume that r restricted to G_Q3 is reducible and its two distinct characters have inertia restrictions 1 and omega, where omega is the mod-3 cyclotomic character. Specify one of these characters, chi2, as the required quotient. The local reducibility is supplied by the actual ordinary caller. The inertia assertion also follows directly here: the two local constituents of an induced representation differ by delta, while each mod-3 local character kills wild inertia and the tame relation Frob*tau*Frob^(-1)=tau^3 forces its inertia order to divide two. Thus their inertia characters are exactly 1 and omega. Local reducibility is not inferred from global dihedrality alone.

The output is a cuspidal modular 3-adic representation R_g lifting r, with determinant a finite-order character times cyclotomic^4, and a G_Q3-stable line whose quotient has finite inertia and reduces to the specified chi2. This is an ordered chi2-good modular lift in the terminology of SW page 203. No assertion that its weight equals the target's weight is needed by that definition. Finite coefficient extensions are allowed throughout.

## 2. Prime-to-three induction and the order of the local characters

Clifford theory in index two gives r = Ind_K^Q(theta_bar), with theta_bar a finite character and theta_bar distinct from its conjugate. Here is the elementary reason in this case. A G_K-stable line has a distinct conjugate under any element outside G_K; otherwise it would be G_Q-stable. The two lines span r and the action in their basis is monomial. The diagonal characters have values in a finite extension of k, hence order prime to 3; the image is contained in their diagonal group extended by the permutation of the two lines. Its order is prime to 3. In particular every local restriction is semisimple.

Let delta be the quadratic character of K/Q. It has delta restricted to inertia at 3 equal to omega. If chi2 is ramified, replace r and its specified quotient by r tensor delta and chi2*delta; otherwise make no replacement. In both cases the specified quotient is now unramified. At the end undo this finite global twist. Write the normalized local representation as eta_bar direct-sum eta_bar*delta, where eta_bar is the specified unramified character. This follows because the two inducing summands have the same restriction to the local index-two subgroup, and the two extensions differ by delta; equivalently the local induced representation is reducible with these extensions. The stated local inertia hypothesis makes eta_bar unramified.

Lift theta_bar by Teichmuller values to a finite-order character theta, and form the odd complex Artin representation R=Ind_K^Q(theta), using compatible embeddings of its cyclotomic coefficient field into C and Qbar_3. Its reduction is the normalized r. On the local index-two subgroup theta is unramified and is the restriction of the Teichmuller lift eta of eta_bar. Consequently R restricted to G_Q3 is eta direct-sum eta*delta. Its inertia invariants have dimension one. Its Artin conductor N has v_3(N)=1, and its determinant epsilon also has conductor exponent one at 3. Arithmetic Frobenius acts on the invariant line by the unit eta(Frob_3).

## 3. A weight-one form and its operator at 3

The final Deligne--Serre paper, Theorem 4.10 and the dihedral example following Section 4.14, supplies the primitive weight-one cusp form f attached to R. Its level is the Artin conductor N and its nebentype is epsilon. The equality of Euler factors at all primes, including bad primes, gives a_3(f)=eta(Frob_3). Thus f is a U_3 eigenform with a 3-adic-unit eigenvalue. The coefficients of f are algebraic integers: the local inverse Euler factors have integral coefficients, since their roots are eigenvalues of finite-order matrices, and multiplication of the Euler series preserves integrality. Normalize a_1(f)=1.

These statements use the dihedral Artin-to-theta-form theorem, not an assumed modularity-lifting result for r. The finite-order character theta and its twists give nontrivial Hecke characters of the imaginary quadratic field; the dihedral example in Deligne--Serre provides the holomorphy input to Theorem 4.10. The reducible Artin case is excluded by theta != theta^c.

## 4. Weight raising and simultaneous integral eigenvalue lifting

Let E_4=1+240*sum_{n>=1} sigma_3(n)q^n. Then h=f E_4 is a weight-five cusp form of level N and character epsilon, with integral q-expansion and h congruent to f modulo the coefficient uniformizer above 3. Its first coefficient is 1.

For a prime l not dividing N, the q-expansion formula for T_l in weight k is a_{ln}+epsilon(l)l^(k-1)a_{n/l}. Since 3 divides N, such l is a 3-adic unit, and l^4=1 modulo 3. Therefore the mod-3 Hecke formula for h in weight five agrees with that for f in weight one. For l dividing N the U_l formula is a_n -> a_{ln}, independent of weight. Hence the nonzero reduction of h is a simultaneous eigenvector for every T_l with l not dividing N and every U_l with l dividing N, with the same residual eigenvalues as f. In particular the U_3 eigenvalue is a unit.

Choose a number field E containing the character values and the coefficients of f, together with the already chosen embeddings into C and Qbar_3. Let O=(O_E)_lambda be its localized number-field DVR, not its completion. Deligne--Serre Section 2.6 and Proposition 2.7 give the rational structure and the full integral q-expansion lattice in weight five on Gamma_1(N). Intersect the fixed epsilon eigenspace with the forms whose q-coefficients lie in O, and call the intersection M. It contains a full lattice: the epsilon projector exists over E, and a single nonzero denominator clears its action on a finite basis of the lattice from Proposition 2.7. This uses no division by the order of the diamond group inside O. Finitely many coefficient functionals inject the E-vector space; they embed M into O^d for a finite d. Thus M is finitely generated and torsion free over the DVR O, hence free. It contains h. The fixed epsilon condition makes every diamond translate an O-unit multiple, so integrality at the chosen q-expansion is consistent with the all-diamond condition in Proposition 2.7. Formula 1.6.1 proves stability under all good T_l, and 1.6.2 proves stability under every bad U_l, including U_3. The pairwise commutation follows directly from these coefficient formulas at distinct primes; no T_3 is included because 3 divides N.

Deligne--Serre Lemma 6.11 applies to this finite free M and the entire commuting family just specified. It gives a finite extension Eprime/E, a DVR Oprime over O, and a nonzero common eigenvector in M tensor Eprime whose eigenvalues reduce to those of h. Choose a complex embedding of Eprime extending that of E and the 3-adic embedding determined by Oprime. The rational structure just used realizes the vector as a classical complex cusp form g. This lifts eigenvalues, not the particular vector h, and does not yet assert that g is normalized. Its U_3 eigenvalue is a 3-adic unit.

To avoid any oldform ambiguity, pass to the primitive cusp constituent g0 of g. The decomposition in Deligne--Serre Section 1.9 has primitive levels N0 dividing N and degeneracy indices d dividing N/N0, with epsilon defined modulo N0. Since epsilon has conductor exponent one at 3 and N also has exponent one, v_3(N0)=1 and all such d are prime to 3. On each relevant primitive constituent U_3 acts by a_3(g0), and it commutes with these prime-to-3 degeneracy maps. Choose a nonzero constituent of the common eigenpacket. Then a_3(g0) is the same unit eigenvalue. Its good-prime eigenvalues have the desired residual values. This step does not require an assertion that the original lifted eigenvector had first coefficient nonzero.

## 5. The prescribed quotient

The classical ordinary local theorem gives the Galois representation of the weight-five primitive form g0 an unramified one-dimensional quotient at 3. A directly available final published statement is Ghate--Vatsal (2004), printed page 2144, equation (1.1) and the paragraph preceding it, which attributes the general theorem to Wiles (1988). Only that recalled ordinary theorem is used, not Ghate--Vatsal's later main theorem with its extra residual hypothesis.

The arithmetic good-prime characteristic polynomials of this representation reduce to those of normalized r, by the simultaneous Hecke congruence. Chebotarev and Brauer--Nesbitt identify its semisimplified reduction with r. Since r is absolutely irreducible, a stable lattice has this irreducible reduction. Intersect the ordinary line with the lattice; this intersection is saturated, so the quotient lattice is free of rank one and its reduction is a quotient of the local r. That quotient is unramified. The local r is a direct sum of eta_bar and eta_bar*delta, with distinct inertia characters 1 and omega. Its unique unramified constituent is eta_bar. Thus the quotient is the specified normalized chi2. No equality of unordered constituents is substituted for this argument.

Undo the quadratic twist from Section 2 if it was used, as follows. Put tau=sum_{a mod3} delta(a)exp(2*pi*i*a/3), which is nonzero, and g_delta(z)=tau^(-1)sum_{a mod3}delta(a)g0(z+a/3). The quadratic Gauss identity gives the q-coefficients delta(n)a_n(g0), so a_1(g_delta)=1. Rational translation preserves holomorphy and cuspidality. For gamma=(A,B;C,D) in Gamma_0(9N0), the conjugate T_(a/3)*gamma*T_(-a/3) is integral, belongs to Gamma_0(N0), and has lower-right entry D modulo N0: C is divisible by9N0 and D-A is divisible by3 since AD=1 modulo3. Thus every translated summand transforms with epsilon(D). It follows that g_delta is a cusp form of level9N0 and nebentype epsilon. For l not dividing3N0, the coefficient formula and delta(l)^2=1 give T_l g_delta=delta(l)a_l(g0)g_delta. A primitive constituent therefore has arithmetic good-prime polynomials equal to those of rho_g0 tensor delta. The classical attachment recalled in final GV2143-2144 and the characteristic-polynomial uniqueness lemma DS3.2 identify its representation with that tensor product. No ordinary U3 assertion is made for g_delta; the required local filtration is obtained by tensoring the already established filtration of rho_g0. The resulting quotient has finite inertia and reduces to the original specified chi2. The determinant remains epsilon times cyclotomic^4, up to a finite-order factor; twisting by delta squares to one. Since the weight is five and epsilon(-1)=-1, the representation is odd. This proves exactly the required ordered modular entry, conditional only on the explicitly named classical theta-form, integral-lattice, Galois-attachment and ordinary-local inputs.
