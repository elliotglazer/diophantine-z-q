## Induction, local normalization and the theta input

The index-two argument is valid in characteristic three. A stable line for G_K and its distinct conjugate span the representation; the monomial diagonal subgroup has order prime to three, and its permutation quotient has order at most two. Thus the entire finite residual image has order prime to three. The local restriction is consequently semisimple. Local reducibility remains an input, supplied by the ordinary caller; it is not inferred from global dihedrality.

For a reducible local restriction, induction from the nontrivial local quadratic subgroup, or the equivalent quadratic self-twist, identifies the two characters as eta_bar and eta_bar times delta. A one-dimensional mod-three local character kills wild inertia, and the tame Frobenius relation forces its inertial order to divide two. Since delta is the ramified quadratic cyclotomic character, the two inertial characters are 1 and omega. Twisting by delta when required makes the specified quotient unramified. This preserves the specified character as ordered data, rather than choosing an arbitrary constituent.

Teichmuller lifting of the inducing character preserves its inequality with its conjugate and its residual identification. Complex conjugation exchanges the inducing lines and has determinant minus one. The local inducing character is the restriction of the unramified Teichmuller character eta, so the complex lift restricts to eta plus eta times delta. This is tame at three, with one-dimensional inertia invariants. Its Artin conductor and determinant conductor both have exponent one at three.

The published dihedral example satisfies the holomorphy hypothesis of Theorem 4.10 and gives the primitive cuspform at the Artin conductor.
