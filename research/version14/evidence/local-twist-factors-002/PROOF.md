# Fixed local generators and omitted Euler factors

Fix the original elliptic curve E/Q, its rational de Rham convention, and the finite set of primes dividing 2N. At two, the local differential generator means an invariant differential of a minimal integral Weierstrass equation; if the coordinate is dual to this differential, use the dual generator. Multiplication by a 2-adic unit is harmless, but arbitrary independently chosen nonunit rescalings are not allowed. This makes explicit the local-generator normalization in current main13668–13676.

The published inputs from Silverman, The Arithmetic of Elliptic Curves, second edition (2009), are Table3.1 on p45 (coordinate and invariant-differential transformation), PropositionVII.1.3(b,c), pp186–187 (minimal differential unique up to a unit), and the local Euler polynomial definitions in AppendixC.16, p449. The equivalence between good reduction and an unramified prime-to-residue-characteristic Tate module is VII.7.1(a<=>c), p201. We also use the minimal-model reduction criteria in VII.5.1, p196, which have no residue-characteristic restriction. These are separate inputs from any global nonvanishing theorem.

Every odd 2-adic unit belongs to one of four squareclasses represented by 1,3,5,7. To see the only needed assertion, a unit congruent to1 modulo8 is a square: starting with an odd solution modulo2^n, n>=3, adding either0 or2^(n-1) changes its square by either0 or2^n modulo2^(n+1). Choose the addition to obtain the next congruence. The successive roots converge 2-adically. Thus for odd squarefree signed h we can write h=h_0 u^2 with h_0 in this fixed four-element set and u a 2-adic unit.

Write a fixed short equation E:y^2=x^3+A x+B over Q_2 and its quadratic twists E^h:y^2=x^3+h^2 A x+h^3 B. The isomorphism E^(h_0) -> E^h given by (x,y)->(u^2 x,u^3 y) pulls dx/(2y) back to u^(-1) dx/(2y). This is also the factor in Table3.1. There are only four comparisons from these displayed differentials to minimal invariant differentials, one for each h_0. Isomorphic curves have the same minimal discriminant valuation; by VII.1.3 their minimal differentials correspond up to a unit. Consequently the valuation of the comparison for general h equals that of its representative h_0. The dual-coordinate version inverts the factor and has the same finiteness property. Any fixed original rational period or lattice comparison adds only a fixed valuation. This proves boundedness of the local-generator contribution; it does not posit boundedness for arbitrary rescalings.

For each fixed prime p dividing2N there are finitely many quadratic local squareclasses (and the current family in fact fixes its local types there). At an odd p, a unit with square nonzero residue has a square root by successive lifting using the invertible derivative2x; thus its squareclass is determined by its residue squareclass and the parity of its valuation. Together with the explicit dyadic calculation above this proves the required finiteness. Hence only finitely many local twists and local Euler polynomials occur. In the notation of C.16, at s=1 their reciprocal factors are 1-a_p/p+1/p=#E(F_p)/p for good reduction, 1-1/p for split multiplicative reduction, 1+1/p for nonsplit multiplicative reduction, and1 for additive reduction. Every one is nonzero: in the good case the identity point already gives #E(F_p)>=1. Equivalently C.16 gives the common expression #E_ns(F_p)/p. No global L-value nonvanishing is used to prove this local assertion.

At an active good odd prime q dividing h, the original V_2(E) is unramified by VII.7.1. Since q divides the squarefree h, the polynomial X^2-h is Eisenstein at q and the quadratic character of h is ramified there, so some inertia element acts as minus the identity on V_2(E) tensor chi_h. Its inertia-invariant subspace is therefore zero. In particular the twist has bad reduction by VII.7.1. Its j-invariant equals that of E and is integral, since E has good reduction. If the twist had multiplicative reduction, VII.5.1(b) would give v_q(c4)=0 and v_q(Delta)>0 in a minimal model, hence v_q(j)=3v_q(c4)-v_q(Delta)<0, a contradiction. It therefore has additive reduction by the exhaustive criteria VII.5.1, and C.16 gives its local factor as1. This uses only prime-to-q Tate coefficients and the published minimal-model criteria, including at q=3; it does not require identifying two definitions of Euler factors.

At an unused good prime q dividing the common conductor support but not h, the twist representation is unramified and the curve is good by VII.7.1. The previously supplied Kato norm polynomial at t=0 is 1-a_q(E)chi_h(q)/q+1/q. The identity a_q(E^h)=a_q(E)chi_h(q) also follows directly by counting solutions of h*y^2=f(x) over F_q after completing the square in a good equation (q is odd). Hence this equals #E^h(F_q)/q by C.16. Thus the unused-prime contribution is exactly the displayed point-count factor, while the active-prime factor is1. The Kato norm relation itself is retained as a separate input.

The result is only the fixed local comparison and the stated local factors. It does not prove central reciprocity, the fixed original period comparison, or the full K3 identity independently of their previously supplied inputs.
