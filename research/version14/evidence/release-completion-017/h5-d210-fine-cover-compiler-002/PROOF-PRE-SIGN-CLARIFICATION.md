## 2. Nonempty finite etale full-marking cover

Because 3 is invertible in R, A[3] is finite etale of rank 3^4. Here we use the multiplication-isogeny and prime-to-characteristic etaleness statements of final Milne1986, Theorem8.2 and Remark8.4. Fix a geometric point of Xscr. On V_3(A), the unital action of B_3 is faithful and makes this four-dimensional Q_3-space a rank-one left B_3-module: B_3 is a division algebra of Q_3-dimension four. The integral Tate lattice T_3(A) is stable under its maximal order O_3.

Every full left O_3-stable lattice L in B_3 is O_3 x for an x in B_3^*. Indeed its nonzero elements have a least valuation w(x), where w=ord_3(nrd)/2. Such a least value exists because L is compact and is bounded away from infinity in the negative direction, and w takes values in (1/2)Z. For y in L, w(y x^(-1))>=0, so y is in O_3 x. The reverse inclusion follows from stability. Thus T_3(A)/3 is the regular left O/3O-module. The standard inverse-limit projection identifies this quotient with A[3]. This argument is at the ramified quaternion prime 3; it does not replace O_3 by M_2(Z_3).

Both finite etale group schemes become constant on an etale cover of the base. Their group isomorphisms form a finite etale scheme there, and imposing O-linearity amounts to finitely many equalities on a Z-basis of O. Those equalities select open and closed subsets. Descent therefore gives a finite etale Isom_O scheme on every atlas of Xscr, compatibly with base change. The preceding fiber calculation proves that it is everywhere nonempty. Its degree is the order of Aut_O(O/3O), the unit group of the opposite ring.

The radical m of O_3 has residue field F_9 and m^2=3O_3 up to a unit. Consequently O/3O has 81 elements, its radical has 9, and its unit group has 72. Thus Yscr --> Xscr is a representable finite etale surjection of degree 72. In particular Yscr is a proper smooth Deligne–Mumford stack of relative dimension one over R, carrying the pullback of A. The degree 72 is over the STACK; it is not the degree over its coarse curve.

## 3. Trivial inertia on every base

At any geometric point the stabilizer in Xscr is finite, because Xscr is proper Deligne–Mumford. Its action on T_3(A) is faithful by the prime-to-characteristic Tate faithfulness statement in final Milne1986, Lemma12.2. A stabilizer of a fully marked object acts identically modulo 3.

The subgroup 1+3M_4(Z_3) has no nonidentity finite-order element. A nontrivial finite cyclic subgroup would contain an element of prime order l. Write this element as 1+3^a M, a>=1, with M nonzero modulo 3. If l is not 3, expansion of its l-th power modulo 3^(a+1) gives the nonzero term l3^a M. If l=3, division of its cube minus 1 by 3^(a+1) gives

    M + 3^a M^2 + 3^(2a-1) M^3,

which is nonzero modulo 3. Both cases contradict its order. Every geometric stabilizer of Yscr is therefore trivial.

This also proves arbitrary-base rigidity, not just rigidity on algebraically closed fields. Inertia over an atlas is finite and unramified. Its identity section is open, because it is a section of an unramified morphism, and closed, because the morphism is separated. The complement has empty geometric fibers and is empty. Hence the entire inertia is the identity section. Stacks Lemma94.13.2, whose proof constructs the etale equivalence relation of this stack in setoids, identifies Yscr with an algebraic space Y. The universal abelian scheme descends to this same Y; no choice of a coarse universal family is being made.

## 4. Why this algebraic space is projective

We spell out this step because a proper algebraic space need not be a scheme. Every geometric fiber of Y/R is a proper smooth algebraic-space curve. By Stacks Tag0ADD its local rings of dimension at most one have schematic neighborhoods, so the fiber is a scheme. Over characteristic zero, the finite marking map to the selected genus-five coarse curve is nonconstant on every component. Riemann–Hurwitz gives genus at least 5 for each connected component, even without assuming that this finite map is unramified.

Use Stacks Theorem76.36.5 and Lemma76.36.9, the Stein factorization and finite-etale component statement for proper algebraic spaces. Since Y/R is smooth, flat and has geometrically reduced fibers, its component factor R' over R is finite etale; Y/R' has geometrically connected fibers. Smooth proper genus is locally constant by Stacks Lemma109.16.5 (Tag0E83), applied over this finite-etale component base; Y over that base is again smooth, proper and geometrically connected. Thus every geometric fiber component over R has genus at least 2: every component of the finite etale R' meets the generic fiber. These are applications of the algebraic-space Stein statement and the smooth-curve genus statement, not an assumption that Y was already a scheme.

The relative differential line omega=Omega^1_(Y/R) restricts to the canonical line of each smooth fiber component. It has positive degree 2g-2 there and is ample on the entire disjoint fiber. Stacks Tag0D3A now applies to the proper algebraic space Y over the Noetherian scheme Spec R and this line bundle. It makes omega relatively ample in a neighborhood of every base point. Tag0D32 then says that Y is a scheme and omega is relatively ample in the usual sense. Finally Stacks Lemma37.50.1 (Tag0B45), implication(6) to(1), gives projectivity: the affine base has its own ample invertible sheaf, as required there. This proves the requested scheme assertion without assuming an unjustified general 'proper algebraic space implies scheme' principle.

## 5. The characteristic-zero PEL datum and all level multipliers

Here and only here we use the rational PEL description. Let V be the regular left B-module, with its alternating form psi coming from the selected positive polarization of the standard O-lattice. The relation is

    psi(bx,y)=psi(x,b*y).

The space of such alternating forms is one-dimensional over Q. This can be checked after a splitting field: V becomes W tensor V0, with W and V0 both two-dimensional; the involution on End(W) is adjoint to a symmetric form, and compatible alternating forms are that symmetric form tensor an alternating form on V0. The latter space has dimension one. The associated similitude group is the right-multiplication copy of B^op,*; after splitting, it is GSp(V0), so this is Milne's simple type C, not the disconnected orthogonal type BD. Final Milne2005 Example8.6 and Proposition8.7 state precisely this classification and connectedness.

The positive/negative complex structures form both half-planes. This assertion is checked directly here, because Milne's official erratum corrects the general uniqueness assertion8.14 and the incomplete condition(**) of8.17. Over R the centralizer of the left M_2(R)-action is another M_2(R), and its full similitude group is GL_2(R). An O-compatible complex structure is J with J^2=-1 in this centralizer. Write the real regular module as W tensor W0, where the left algebra is End(W), both W and W0 have dimension2, and the right centralizer is End(W0). Here J acts on W0, not on the entire four-dimensional V. For any nonzero v in W0, the pair(v,Jv) is a basis. If g has those columns, then J=g J0 g^(-1). The element 1 tensor g lies in the actual right-centralizer G(R), so it commutes with the fixed left B-action. For z=a+bi the Hodge map acts by 1 tensor(a+bJ); thus the full map, not only its value at i, is conjugate to the fixed h0. The chosen rational isomorphism a is left unchanged; its transported Hodge map is shown to belong to X. Thus it belongs to the chosen full G(R)-conjugacy class X, including both half-planes. We do not assert this implication for a general unitary datum.

This gives the entire complex PEL variety, with no additional rational-form components. Its reflex field is Q; in this rank-two type-C case the cocharacter conjugacy class is the usual (1,0) class for the quaternionic inner form of GL_2 and is rational. At each finite prime an O-linear isomorphism of regular Tate lattices is automatically a similitude of their compatible forms, since the space of forms just computed is one-dimensional. At 3 its multiplier is a unit because both integral forms are perfect; it is NOT required to equal 1 modulo 3. Reducing an integral O_3-isomorphism gives precisely an O/3O-isomorphism, and every such isomorphism lifts, since units of O_3 surject onto units of O_3/3O_3. In particular we retain every possible multiplier modulo 3, and therefore every geometric component. Restricting to one Weil-pairing value would change this functor and is not part of the construction.

## 6. Integral objects versus rational PEL quadruples

For a marked object over C, choose integral O-linear Tate isomorphisms at all finite primes, matching eta at 3. At split primes their existence follows from the matrix-unit decomposition of an O_l-stable lattice; at division primes it follows from the valuation argument of Section2. Their total U(3)-orbit is independent of the choices. Together with the rational polarization class and B-action this is Milne's quadruple (A,s,i,eta_f U(3)).

Conversely such a quadruple specifies the full lattice eta_f(Ohat) in the rational Tate space. A lattice commensurable with the Tate lattice is realized by an isogenous abelian variety: after multiplying by an integer, this is the quotient by the corresponding finite torsion subgroup. The O-stability gives the integral O-action. This construction is independent up to unique integral isomorphism of the integer used. The residual marking at 3 is part of the same lattice orbit.

No new polarization level is introduced here. The compatible rational polarization class has a unique positive representative perfect on that lattice: locally, its dual-lattice discrepancy is a scalar, and a single positive rational scalar cancels its finitely many valuations. Any two such scalars have valuation zero at every prime and ratio 1. This is the selected integral polarization in the maximal moduli problem. The normalization restores the polarization of the given lattice; it neither rescales eta_f nor fixes its unit similitude multiplier. Equivalently final Buzzard printed592 gives the unique principal polarization from this integral O-action and fixed positive involution over Z[1/210]. That paragraph precedes, and does not assume, the prime-to-discriminant level hypotheses of its Section2. Its ancestry through Boutot-Carayol and the stated descent result is retained; no new proof of those inputs is claimed.

These two constructions are inverse. A rational quasi-isogeny carrying the integral Tate lattice to the integral Tate lattice is an actual isomorphism: a nontrivial finite kernel or cokernel would be detected on some prime-power torsion lattice. Thus the equivalence does not replace the fine moduli problem by an isogeny quotient with fewer objects. For the algebraic comparison, on each arithmetic component of Sh_U(3)(G,X) use its natural integral lattice local system and Hodge structures. Its arithmetic stabilizer preserves this lattice and its marking modulo3. The marking is a flat section of the finite etale isomorphism sheaf; such a section over a complex algebraic variety is algebraic, since its graph is a connected-component union in a finite etale cover. Thus the fine functor gives an algebraic comparison map from each component to Y_C. The point classification from7.4 and the integral-lattice equivalence make it bijective on geometric points.
