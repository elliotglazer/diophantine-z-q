## Common models and endomorphisms

Let q be the odd inert prime of the conductor-one construction. Every CM curve in the finite ideal-class orbit has integral j by the separately retained CM-integrality input. Silverman VII.5.5 gives good reduction after a finite extension of its local field of definition. Take one finite extension containing the definitions of all these curves, the finitely many isogenies under discussion and their duals, and their good models. Good reduction is preserved under further extension by base change of the smooth proper model. This gives a common complete discrete valuation ring R and abelian schemes over R. If one wants arbitrary additional ideal representatives, apply the same argument to each finite diagram; no single finite field containing every torsion point is asserted.

Let A and B denote any two such abelian schemes and let f_K:A_K -> B_K be a homomorphism. Apply BLR 1.2/8 and Definition 1.2/1 to the smooth R-scheme A and the Neron model B. There is a unique R-morphism f extending f_K. It is a group homomorphism: the two maps from A x_R A expressing compatibility with addition agree on the generic fiber and hence agree by the same uniqueness property. The identity and inverse assertions follow in the same way. Composition also commutes with extension by uniqueness. These statements are valid before taking a strict henselization.

For a nonzero endomorphism alpha of an elliptic curve, use its dual isogeny and the identity alpha^dual alpha=[deg alpha]. Extending this identity and then taking a fiber shows that the reduction of alpha cannot be zero: multiplication by any nonzero integer is a nonconstant morphism of an elliptic curve, even when the residue characteristic divides that integer. Thus specialization of the CM order is injective. The dual-isogeny and multiplication-degree facts are separate elementary elliptic-curve inputs; their exact Silverman III.6 locators still need to be added to the source-use record.

## Prime-to-q kernels and quotients

Let n be invertible in R. On an elliptic scheme A/R the differential of [n] on its rank-one tangent bundle is multiplication by n, and translation gives the same invertibility at every point. The relative Jacobian criterion between smooth R-schemes implies that [n] is etale. It is proper, since its source is proper and its target is separated over R, and etale morphisms are locally quasi-finite. The proper quasi-finite finiteness theorem then makes it finite etale. Thus A[n] is finite etale over R. The Jacobian criterion and proper quasi-finite finiteness are explicit general scheme-theoretic inputs, not consequences of the Neron mapping property.

After strict henselization, each finite etale R-scheme is a disjoint union of copies of Spec(R). This is the finite-etale equivalence for a henselian local ring and its residue field. Consequently specialization of A[n] is an isomorphism on geometric points and respects all endomorphisms. Equalizers of maps between these constant finite schemes are unions of components. In particular simultaneous kernels of the generators of a prime-to-q ideal of the CM order specialize to the simultaneous kernels of those same generators. The finite-etale equivalence is a retained elementary source input, whose exact source pin remains to be added.

For clarity, quotient compatibility here can be obtained without a general theorem constructing quotients of abelian schemes. The characteristic-zero ideal quotient B_K=A_K/A_K[a] is another CM elliptic curve, so B has already been given good reduction. Let f_K:A_K -> B_K be this ideal isogeny, of degree d=Norm(a), and extend it and its dual as above. Since d is invertible in R, the identity f^dual f=[d] makes the differential of f a unit. The same properness and Jacobian argument makes f finite etale. Its kernel is a finite etale subgroup of A[d]. Its generic fiber is A_K[a]; equality with the simultaneous-kernel subgroup holds over a strict henselization because both are unions of the same components of A[d]. Hence its special fiber is precisely Abar[a]. The special isogeny has the same degree d and therefore realizes the ideal quotient of Abar, with the specialized CM action. This proves the needed quotient compatibility for this actual finite diagram.

When a is prime to N as well, the restriction to A[N] is an isomorphism: its kernel is contained in the d-torsion, and gcd(d,N)=1. Thus the chosen cyclic level subgroup specializes compatibly with the ideal isogeny. At q the prime-to-q isogeny induces an isomorphism on the q-divisible groups. The tangent differential is nonzero, so it intertwines the same residue embedding of the CM order. These last observations preserve the orientations used by the separate conductor-one argument; they do not compute the supersingular adelic realization.
