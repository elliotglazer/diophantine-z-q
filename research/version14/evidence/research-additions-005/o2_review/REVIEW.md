I also read the final Hu--Tan page 1384, including its page image. Its type criterion applies to infinite-dimensional smooth absolutely irreducible representations and detects the inertia representation. It does not itself state the stronger full-parameter conclusion. Section 3 proves the additional conclusion needed here.

For an absolutely irreducible two-dimensional Weil--Deligne parameter, a nonzero monodromy operator would have a stable line as its kernel. Thus monodromy is zero. A scalar inertia action would leave a Frobenius eigenline stable over the algebraic closure. Finite inertia is semisimple in characteristic zero, so the remaining possibilities are an irreducible two-dimensional inertia representation or two distinct characters exchanged by Frobenius. This exhausts the stated domain; irreducibility only over a nonsplitting coefficient field would not suffice and is not the frozen hypothesis.

The exclusion of a character type is valid. Every smooth character of GL2(Z5) factors through determinant: elementary unipotents generate SL2(Z5), and commutators with diag(2,1), or its reverse, generate these unipotents. A putative one-dimensional sigma would then occur in a suitably twisted irreducible unramified principal series. The HT criterion would give scalar inertia, a contradiction. Hence a smooth irreducible containing sigma is infinite-dimensional, and the published criterion applies.

For a second parameter with the same inertia, its monodromy commutes with inertia and is zero in both cases. In the irreducible-inertia case the two Frobenius operators differ by a scalar. In the two-character case both operators exchange the same two lines. The displayed c and d give diag(d,1)(cF)diag(d,1)^(-1)=F'. This conjugation centralizes inertia. The resulting difference is precisely an unramified character. A positive power of Frobenius is a nonzero scalar in both cases, so Frobenius is semisimple in characteristic zero. The passage to Frobenius semisimplification does not discard an extra Jordan parameter. These arguments prove the full parameter comparison, rather than assuming a stronger Henniart statement. Henniart remains the proof ancestor of the published inertia criterion.

## Unitarity and filtration transfer

I read Colmez VI.6.9's fixed-module setup and VI.6.42 in the pinned final text. The two parameters are aligned up to an unramified character nu before that theorem is applied. Choose a common finite Galois field of crystallinity. Both actual representations have the same Hodge weights, hence the same total Hodge number. The unramified Weil twist changes the determinant Newton valuation by a nonzero fixed multiple of 2*v5(nu(Frob_geo)). Necessary admissibility for the two actual representations equates each Newton total with its Hodge total. The valuation is therefore zero. This argument needs only the necessary direction for representations that already exist, not a converse construction theorem.

An unramified character with unit value on Frobenius extends continuously to G_Q5. After removing this twist, the two objects have the same full Weil--Deligne module and weights. A common cyclotomic twist puts their filtration weights in the exact normalized domain of VI.6.9; with the Colmez/Paškūnas label conversion above, this is the same operation on both objects. VI.6.42 now compares the two admissible filtrations, and character-twist compatibility undoes the normalization and nu. The result is the stated formula for the arbitrary target r in this supercuspidal domain.

In particular the proof does not infer unitarity of a Weil parameter merely from unitarity of its Galois character. The cyclotomic character would disprove that shortcut. This explicit admissibility argument is the current supplier for the unitarity step; older compressed wording is not substituted for it. Nor does the argument use the modularity-dependent Remark VI.6.51(ii).
