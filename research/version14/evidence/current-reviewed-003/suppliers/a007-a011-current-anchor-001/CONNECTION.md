## C1. The local square-root obstruction

All groups of roots of unity in the square sequence are constant discrete Galois modules. Only its kernel mu_2 coincides with the natural Galois module of second roots of unity. Hilbert 90 and the Kummer sequence identify H^2(G_K,mu_2) with Br(K)[2]. This does not identify constant mu_infinity with cyclotomic coefficients.

For K=Q_p, local reciprocity identifies continuous finite-order characters of G_K with finite-order characters of the profinite completion of K^*. Milne ADT, second edition, I.1.6(b), printed19, and I.2 introduction, printed26, give the local invariant and reciprocity conventions. A finite-order character chi has a finite-order character square root if and only if chi(-1)=1. Indeed, on p^Z choose a square root of chi(p), of finite order, and extend to its profinite completion. For odd p, the unit group is the product of cyclic mu_(p-1) and 1+pZ_p; the latter is Z_p by logarithm. Its finite character group is p-primary, so multiplication by2 is invertible. In a cyclic group of even order, a character is a square exactly when it is trivial on its unique element of order2. For p=2, use Q_2^*=2^Z times {1,-1} times (1+4Z_2). Logarithm identifies the last factor with Z_2; every finite character of it has a square root by increasing its two-power order. The sign factor has a square root precisely when its character is trivial. These are the usual Q_p unit decompositions; the proof uses them only for Q_p, not an arbitrary dyadic extension.

Thus the boundary of chi in the constant square sequence vanishes exactly when chi(-1)=1. Since Br(Q_p)[2] has two elements, this vanishing test determines the local class, not merely one of its consequences. Reversing local reciprocity inverts chi and does not change its value at -1 or the order-two invariant.

## C3. Character invariance and ramification removal

For h in G_L and g in G_Q, normalization and inflation give epsilon(g,h)=epsilon(h,g)=1. Consequently beta(gh)=beta(g)beta(h)=beta(hg). Applying the same identities to h'=ghg^-1 in G_L proves beta(h')=beta(h). Also delta(beta) is trivial on G_L times G_L, so beta restricted to G_L is a conjugation-invariant character. Its kernel therefore defines a finite Galois extension L'/Q with [L':L]<=2m.

At an odd p outside T, inertia is contained in G_L and chi is unramified. The inertia restriction of beta is therefore a character of order at most2. A continuous finite-image cochain factors through a finite quotient as a function: take a finite clopen partition, then one open normal subgroup refining it. Hence only finitely many such inertia restrictions are nontrivial. For odd p the wild inertia is pro-p and the tame inertia has just one nontrivial continuous quadratic character. Let P be the product of the odd primes outside T with nontrivial restriction (P=1 if there are none). The global quadratic character attached to Q(sqrt(P)) has that nontrivial inertia character at each prime dividing P and is unramified at every other odd prime. Its discriminant divides4P, so any additional finite ramification is at2, already in T. Multiplying beta by this character kills exactly the required restrictions and preserves beta^2, delta(beta), mu_(2m)-valuedness and conjugacy invariance. Thus the resulting L' is unramified outside T and still has [L':Q]<=2m[L:Q]. No extension of the auxiliary modularity comparison field is involved.

## C4. Fricke stability for the full coefficient-conjugate span

## C6. Duality and conductor convention

Milne's original1986 chapter, Theorem15.1(a), printed129, gives the canonical integral identification H^1_et(A_f,Z_5)=Hom(T_5(A_f),Z_5), and Remark15.5, printed131, explicitly gives Galois equivariance over the base field. Rationalizing gives H^1_et(A_f,Q_5)=V_5(A_f)^vee with no additional Tate twist. Thus r_5 in the covariant Tate realization is equivalent to r_5^vee in cohomology. At good primes the arithmetic-Frobenius polynomial on Tate modules matches the geometric-Frobenius cohomological polynomial; these conventions are not silently interchanged on the same action. This is a different published locator from Theorem12.1(a) in Milne's later course notes.

Duality preserves the local Artin/Weil–Deligne conductor. For the finite inertial Weil representation, duals have the same invariant dimensions on every ramification subgroup, by characteristic-zero averaging, so the Artin and Swan terms agree. For a Weil–Deligne pair (r,N), the additional term is dim(V^I)-dim((ker N)^I). Inertia commutes with N, averaging commutes with N, and the restricted dual nilpotent has the same rank on the corresponding invariant subspaces. This term is therefore unchanged too. This proves only duality invariance; identification of the primitive level with these local conductors remains the separate Carayol/newform input already displayed later in the manuscript.
