## Finite invariant module and fibers

The support proof is valid for the stated Noetherian E-algebra A. Both implications about vanishing fibers are needed. The direct summand F of M makes a nonzero F fiber inject into the M fiber. Generation by translates of F gives the converse using a surjection from a direct sum of copies of F. For finite F, its support is closed and Nakayama identifies nonzero localization with nonzero residue fiber. Dense nonzero fibers therefore force every maximal fiber to be nonzero. Reducedness is unnecessary for this conclusion; it is relevant to the additional faithfulness formulation, which is not used.

The compact averaging description is valid in the stated smooth prime-to-p setting, after passing to a small enough compact open subgroup fixing finite generators. Admissibility must actually provide finite A-invariants. I read draft C.35–C.36 and the allowable-fiber calculation on pp78–79 and Remark 6.4.3 on p83. The abstract proof does not establish coadmissibility, finite generation, admissibility, the smooth-contragredient base-change identity, or dense allowable fibers for X(theta). Those are separate input obligations. In particular, the draft's duality is a smooth contragredient over the residue field, not arbitrary algebraic or continuous duality. The report explicitly requires this appropriate category.

The A[1/t] example correctly prevents an inference from faithfulness of an arbitrary module. Likewise a point in the Hecke spectrum alone gives neither nonzero Hom fiber nor the claimed map. The open EME-ACTUAL-X item is indispensable.

## Weak map and arbitrary tame multiplicity

The proof is valid with precisely its premises. Since rho is finite-dimensional absolutely irreducible, its image generates End_E(rho). The closed kernel of the continuous map to a Hausdorff target is therefore rho tensor B_0 by matrix units. B_0 is closed and G-stable. Topological irreducibility and nonzero map force B_0=0. This establishes injectivity without a multiplicity-one assumption.

The locally algebraic restriction lands in the locally algebraic target. Cancelling the rho factor by the same matrix units gives B^alg into S tensor U. Here the assumed tensor is the algebraic tensor: it is a direct sum of copies of the simple module S for any basis of U. Every submodule is semisimple with only that simple type. Neither of the special character/Steinberg factors in VI.5.7 is the algebraic supercuspidal type S. Thus the special alternative is excluded and B^alg is simple. A nonzero coordinate projection then identifies it with S. This does not require a finite-dimensional U or a tame conductor-fixed image.

The target decomposition remains an input. Published Theorem 7.4.2 decomposes the complete locally algebraic space, while Theorem 2.5.1 describes classical parabolic cohomology. Isolating a single cuspidal Hecke system and its Galois representation, excluding other contributions, and supplying classical local-global compatibility require their stated additional results. An unspecified eigenspace or completed tensor with arbitrary U is not enough. The author's report retains these premises explicitly. No unsupported passage to K_1(N) is used.
