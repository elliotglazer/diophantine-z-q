## Exact input and actual consumption

Hu–Tan's final 2015 paper, page 1385, attributes one global input to Emerton [9] and directs the reader to the introduction of Paškūnas, On the Breuil–Mézard conjecture. This input is the identification of the smooth factor in the locally algebraic vectors of the p-adic local Langlands representation in the supercuspidal case. It is not a request to use all of Emerton's global modularity theorem. Hu–Tan's page 1387 comparison saying that Emerton had already proved most cases is historical comparison; its Section 6 proves modularity through patched-module faithfulness.

The trace through the available primary texts is as follows. Page locators for the Duke paper below refer to its explicitly identified author version, not to uninspected final typesetting.

In the Paškūnas convention, the needed irreducible-case identity has the form Pi(V)^alg = pi_sm(WD(V)) tensor W(a,b), where W(a,b)=Sym^(b-a-1) E^2 tensor det^a in the convention of BM 4.14. The local reciprocity and Montreal functor normalizations must be transported together. In the supercuspidal case N=0 and the crystalline and semistable K-types agree. Thus the identity and Henniart's type characterization give Hom_K dimension one precisely for the prescribed Hodge and inertial type, and zero for a different type. Nonvanishing of locally algebraic vectors alone does not identify the inertial type and is insufficient.

This trace does not say that every component of Paškūnas or Hu–Tan depends on Emerton. In particular, the elementary principal-series computation and the Steinberg case should remain separate. It also does not replace Dospinescu's stronger statement about extensions by the simpler identity for irreducible objects.

## Emerton's exact scope and version

The pinned Emerton PDF is the March 23, 2011 draft. The author's current public papers page still places this title in its submitted-or-under-revision list and labels this date. A bounded title/publication search did not locate a final journal version. The 2006 published paper with the additional word “conjecture” in its title is a distinct work; it cannot be substituted merely because the titles resemble each other.

Theorem 3.3.22 on draft p28 states the full locally algebraic-vector identity. The proof points to Colmez for the crystabelline and semistable-twist cases and to Section 7.4 for the remaining case. I read Section 7.4, pp96–98, and visually checked pp28 and98. The argument first uses Colmez VI.6.42 to make the result depend only on WD and the Hodge weights. It then chooses an auxiliary modular residual representation, lifts a K-type into completed cohomology using 5.3.15, and obtains a classical eigenpoint with the desired local smooth type. Classical cohomology at its tame conductor and p-adic local-global compatibility identify the desired local factor.

Remark 7.4.2 explicitly says that the full local-global isomorphism is unnecessary: a nonzero equivariant map from the auxiliary global representation tensor its local Banach representation into the completed-cohomology eigenspace suffices. Irreducibility makes this an injection, Colmez supplies nonzero locally algebraic vectors, and the classical locally algebraic target is irreducible. This is the narrow global input to reconstruct. Remark 6.4.3 supplies that map through the X(theta) construction; 6.4.2 uses classical-point density and the coadmissible-module results. Remark 1.3.1 identifies a smaller source route through 3.1–3.4, 5.1–5.4, 6.3 through6.3.17, 6.4 through6.4.3 and the appendices, with7.4. It excludes the additional machinery devoted to the split local case. This list is a dependency guide, not a new acceptance of those sections.

A normalization issue must be resolved before literal transcription: p28 displays det^(b+1), while the completion of the proof on pp97–98 displays det^b with the same pi_p(V_E) notation. The discrepancy is visible on the PDF pages; no conclusion that the underlying compatibility theorem is false is drawn. The current report keeps the required identity in the actual Paškūnas/Hu–Tan convention and requires an explicit normalization check for any replacement.

## Published alternatives checked and remaining proposal

1. Paškūnas, IHES118(2013), final pp3 and175–176: Theorem12.7 gives a closely related classification of irreducible admissible unitary completions. Its proof and introductory footnote3 expressly retain Emerton7.4 in the supercuspidal case. This is a published statement, not an independent removal of the input.
2. Colmez–Dospinescu–Paškūnas, Cambridge Journal of Mathematics2(2014), author version Theorem1.2 and footnote5: the same classical identification is explicitly derived using Emerton. The authors explain that purely local arguments at that point identify a set of possible Weil parameters, without identifying it with the parameter attached to the given smooth supercuspidal representation. This is precisely the unresolved identification, not merely a Banach-category issue.
3. Colmez–Dospinescu–Niziol, Acta Arithmetica208(2023), author version Remark0.2(v): the authors expressly retain Emerton as the global ingredient identifying M with LL(M). I visually checked this paragraph. The publisher confirms publication, but I have not compared the author PDF against final typesetting. In either event, the inspected argument is not a removal of this dependency.
4. Caraiani–Emerton–Gee–Geraghty–Paškūnas–Shin, Compositio154(2018)503–548, is a plausible alternative reconstruction source. The inspected arXivv2 is identified as an accepted version by Imperial's repository. Its Section7, Theorem7.4 and Remark7.7 provide a new local-global compatibility route using its4.32 and6.18. The actual assumptions include p>3, residual oddness and absolute irreducibility, irreducibility over Q(zeta_p), local Assumption2.2, no vexing primes, and the specific minimal tame level. Assumption2.2 requires scalar endomorphisms and excludes twists of extensions of1 by omega in the displayed orientation. Remark7.1 warns that naive arbitrary-tame-level substitutions fail. The paper states that the needed modular-curve patching construction is essentially the earlier one and recalls its output; that transport is not independently verified here.

A proposed reconstruction using item4 must first produce an auxiliary global modular point for each needed local WD/type, with all those residual and minimal-level conditions, or prove the exact level/type extension needed. It must then identify the specialized projective-envelope representation with Colmez's Banach representation, match the classical locally algebraic eigenspace, obtain the nonzero map, and apply filtration independence without importing the compatibility statement being proved. Neither the actual r5 nor the auxiliary form in Emerton7.4 is automatically known to satisfy these extra conditions. Merely citing item4's abstract, or substituting its theorem at an arbitrary tame level, does not complete the argument.

Accordingly the proposed dependency node is HT-SUPERCUSPIDAL-LOCAL-ALGEBRAIC. Its required conclusion is the precise local smooth-factor/type identity, at least for weight gap one; its current source is Emerton3.3.22 with the bounded global route above; its source-version and independent-reconstruction status remain open. The concrete next proof target is the weak auxiliary-eigenpoint embedding of7.4.2 with compatible classical type. All other Hu–Tan, corrected Kisin/Gee–Kisin, Paškūnas and KW proof inputs retain their earlier status. The Pan scalar closed-image supplement supplies a different inference and has no edge closing this node.
