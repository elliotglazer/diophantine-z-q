## Ray character and all twenty weights

The norm on Z[sqrt(-2)] is Euclidean by the stated coordinate-rounding estimate, so the ideal class group is trivial and the only units are +/-1. The two residue fields at (5) and the chosen prime above 193 have orders 25 and 193. Their product of unit groups, modulo the diagonal global units, is the ray class group in question. The congruence 34^2=-2 mod193 and inertness at5 check. The local character exponents have matching parity, so the product is trivial on -1; reversing the Artin convention does not change that parity. The chosen coefficient field can contain all needed roots of unity, since both 24 and192 divide 5^16-1.

For n=0,1,2,3, k=n+1 gives distinct inertia characters omega_2^k and omega_2^(5k). Frobenius swaps their lines, so the induction over the unramified quadratic local extension is absolutely irreducible. The exact accepted Lemma 2.15 gives the weights (m,n) and (m+n,p-1-n), with m reduced modulo4. For n=4, taking n=0 gives the desired second weight (m,4). Thus the construction covers every one of the twenty irreducible Serre weights. These checks do not assume the target representation has this residual type.

Complex conjugation exchanges the induced lines and has determinant -1; the scalar cyclotomic twist does not alter oddness. L is disjoint from Q(zeta_5), whose unique quadratic subfield is real. At the selected prime above193, theta has inertia of order192 or96 while its conjugate is unramified. Restriction to LQ(zeta_5) does not change that inertia, because the cyclotomic extension is unramified there. The two restricted inducing characters stay distinct, giving the required absolute irreducibility over Q(zeta_5).

Only2 and193 can divide the tame conductor, and neither is -1 mod5. No vexing-prime case remains. The conductor exponent at193 is1 since the representation there is the sum of one nontrivial tame character and one unramified character. Its cyclotomic twist is unramified there. The conductor at2 need not be computed: taking the actual conductor N throughout suffices, and N is divisible by193. This is not a change to an arbitrarily enlarged level.

The torsion argument at Gamma_1(N) is adequate for the curve coverings used. A finite stabilizer has integral rational characteristic polynomial; its eigenvalues are roots of unity, and its trace belongs to {-2,-1,0,1,2}. The Gamma_1 congruence at193 forces trace2, and a finite-order unipotent matrix in characteristic zero is the identity. The same check holds for the conjugate arithmetic groups on the components. Thus no orbifold stabilizer invalidates the covering argument. No claim about an arbitrary adelic group's neatness is needed beyond this use.

## Cohomological lift at that same level

The needed inputs are comparison for finite local systems on the affine smooth complex modular curve, the covering spectral sequence, and the non-Eisenstein H^0 calculation. The curve has graph homotopy type, giving H^2=0 also for nonconstant finite local systems. For finite modules over R_s=O/pi^s, the R_s-linear dual is exact because R_s is self-injective. After a finite cover trivializing the coefficient system, H^0 is built from connected-component characters. Away-from-level Hecke operators act on those components; localization at an absolutely irreducible residual trace system annihilates these Eisenstein terms. This assertion includes the coefficient-system occurrences, not just constant coefficients.

With these inputs, the localized covering spectral sequence identifies Hom_K(M,H_s) with the stated first cohomology. The coefficient long exact sequence and vanishing H^0 and H^2 make this contravariant Hom functor exact on finite smooth K-modules. That exactness is sufficient for the proposed finite-module extensions; the stronger all-smooth-module injectivity formulation can also be obtained by the usual finite-submodule extension criterion for this locally finite smooth category.

One must distinguish a constituent from a submodule. Exactness through a composition series shows that a nonzero Hom from the chosen constituent implies a nonzero Hom from the entire reduction of the lattice; no inclusion of that constituent in the lattice is assumed. The coefficient sequences identify H_s with the pi-torsion-range submodule of H_(s+1) and make reductions surjective. Extending along pi*(Theta/pi^(s+1)) therefore gives a compatible lift. Multiplying its value by pi and using the inclusion H_s into H_(s+1) checks compatibility with reduction; this is not just a collection of unrelated lifts. The inverse-limit map has nonzero reduction and remains nonzero after inverting pi because the limit is pi-torsion-free.

CEGGPS 7.5 and 6.21(i), at the same N, supply the initial constituent: kappa is the supersingular pi in the locally irreducible case, and the nonzero quotient map in Lemma2.15 combined with Frobenius reciprocity embeds the irreducible weight into pi. A nonzero vector of the global residual factor supplies the K-map into cohomology. This uses the cited source conclusions conditionally; it does not constitute a new audit of their patching inputs.

## Eigenpoint, duality, and the local factor

For the smooth irreducible type sigma and the algebraic factor W, W tensor sigma is irreducible on K. On a sufficiently small open subgroup sigma is trivial and W is algebraically irreducible; its matrix algebra isolates the finite multiplicity space, on which sigma is irreducible. Thus the nonzero lifted map is injective. The type-Hom space is finite-dimensional at fixed level: after making sigma trivial, its algebraic-type component is computed by finite-level first cohomology with the corresponding finite-rank algebraic local system. This is the fixed-level classical comparison, not an assumption that every locally algebraic subspace of every admissible Banach representation is finite-dimensional.

A commuting Hecke algebra on this nonzero finite-dimensional space has an eigenvector after finite scalar extension. The published locally algebraic decomposition places it in classical cohomology, and non-Eisenstein localization excludes the boundary systems. The minimal-type and classical local-global inputs then identify its local WD representation up to an unramified twist. Those two external source connections remain explicitly unclosed in the packet.

Theorem7.4 gives the completed homology with the global Galois DUAL, exactly as printed. Specializing at the eigenpoint and taking the continuous coefficient-field dual therefore restores the global representation in completed cohomology. This operation imposes the eigenspace equations by annihilating the specialization ideal; it does not assert that arbitrary completed-cohomology base change is exact. The finite-dimensional global factor may be taken outside that dual.

An important retained prerequisite is the identification of the local projective-envelope fiber with the local Banach representation. Corollary6.19 by itself supplies only its modified Colmez image and nonvanishing, not that identification or absolute topological irreducibility. The actual chain uses the projective-envelope isomorphism of6.18 and the Paskunas Image fiber/classification input invoked in6.17. I raised this distinction with the author, who confirmed it is part of the retained Section6 source condition. This decision requires that interpretation of C07. No inference from equal Colmez images alone is accepted. An immutable source addendum is being prepared separately; its conclusions are not anticipated here.

Once that condition and the classical target are supplied, the smooth-factor argument agrees with the independently checked Gaussian conditional lemma. Matrix units for the global absolutely irreducible factor, and then a nonzero multiplicity coordinate, give the required isomorphism. Colmez VI.5.7 excludes the special alternative; VI.6.18 supplies nonzero locally algebraic vectors. There is no need for a chosen tame newvector to be fixed or for multiplicity one.

The type theorem supplies an unramified twist aligning the full irreducible WD representations, not just their inertia restrictions. With the same Hodge weights, the ratio of the two determinant characters is that unramified twist squared and is unitary. Hence the twist itself is unitary and extends to a continuous Galois character after any needed finite scalar extension. Only after making this alignment can VI.6.42 compare admissible filtrations of the same fixed WD module. Undoing the twist gives the claimed normalization. The coefficient extension may be retained for the type test; it does not require descending a chosen isomorphism.
