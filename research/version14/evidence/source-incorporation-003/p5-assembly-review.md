## Mathematical checks

The field, lattice and group remain the same throughout: finite k of characteristic five, A=k[[T]], K=k((T)), the actual nice-prime representation r, M=ad⁰(r)(1), N the common kernel of r and the full cyclotomic character, and Gamma=G/N. No ordinary local hypothesis or coefficient-rational inducing character is silently added. Pan also imposes conditions needed elsewhere, including even degree of the totally real field and local triviality of the lattice. Their omission from this smaller lemma is harmless because the lemma does not use them; the actual source domain implies its stated hypotheses.

Oddness gives a rational involution with two distinct eigenlines. Any invariant line after extending coefficients is one of these and descends, so the given irreducibility is absolute. The nondegenerate trace pairing on sl₂ turns a proper invariant plane into an invariant line. A nilpotent generator would give an invariant line for r; an invertible traceless generator gives the quadratic character and geometric induction. Thus the case split covers geometric induction, even when its inducing lines require a coefficient extension.

In the induced case, zero trace on the nontrivial coset and residual trace 1+bar-chi identify the inducing field as F(bar-chi). It is totally imaginary. Complete splitting of five in F excludes every nontrivial subfield of the totally ramified Q(zeta₅); the unique quadratic cyclotomic field after base change is totally real. This proves the required disjointness on the existing point. The rotation image is abelian pro-five, inversion by the other coset and invertibility of squaring give abelianization of order at most two, and disjointness then holds for the whole cyclotomic tower. The resulting central cyclotomic scalar proves vanishing directly on the original lattice by the displayed cocycle identity.

For the non-dihedral branch, the fixed uniform annihilator and continuous inverse-limit comparison are explicit retained inputs, not consequences of the element-selection step. Finite degree-zero terms remove the derived-limit obstruction. Inflation embeds the compact H¹ into the assumed finitely generated global module. Its finite generation and annihilation over the Noetherian DVR imply finite length. There is no incorrect inference from bounded exponent alone to finite length.

The distinct-eigenvalue argument uses five actual projective group elements. Scalar normalization need not preserve membership in the linear image, but it does preserve the projective classes used by the argument. Two different unipotent fixed lines give five distinct determinant-one traces, of which at most two have zero discriminant. The common-line case contradicts normality and absolute irreducibility. The scalar-image case uses the full procyclic group Z₅×, with orders four and powers of five coprime, and a closed eigenline stabilizer. It does not assume that every product of procyclic groups is procyclic.

In the dihedral branch the two adjoint constituents have different dimensions and are simple after splitting coefficients. Their two inducing characters in the off-diagonal constituent are distinct: equality would force theta⁴=1, whereas a nontrivial pro-five subgroup of a characteristic-five field's multiplicative group has no such torsion. A rotation and a reflection give the two required fixed lines. Surjectivity from the original cyclotomic kernel ensures that these are actual permissible group elements.

For a non-torsion global cohomology class, vanishing of restriction to N would put it in the torsion inflation submodule. Thus its restriction is nonzero. Since N acts trivially on M, this restriction is a homomorphism, and its K-span B is Gamma-stable by the cocycle conjugation identity. In the irreducible case B is all of M_K. In the dihedral case its scalar extension contains at least one of the two simple summands. The corresponding choice of sigma_0 makes its image nonzero in Q=M_K/(sigma_0−1)M_K, of dimension one. The adjoint action is semisimple for this element, and its invariant line maps isomorphically to Q.

If phi(sigma_0)+phi(tau) vanished in Q for every tau in N, tau=1 would give vanishing of phi(sigma_0), and then all of phi(N) would vanish in Q, a contradiction. For sigma=sigma_0*tau the representation image and cyclotomic value do not change. The cocycle equation identifies phi(sigma) modulo sigma_0−1 with the selected nonzero vector. The integral quotient is therefore torsion and finitely generated, hence finite length. A change of cocycle by a coboundary changes its evaluation only by an element of (sigma−1)M. This checks the identity of the exact class and quotient required in Pan 4.3.10.

The finite-level invariant bound uses a full-rank minor of a fixed combined action-difference matrix. Its adjugate annihilates the kernel by T^d; its length in a rank-three module is at most 3d. The evaluation maps on the fraction-field global cohomology have zero common kernel. Successively restricting to the current kernel lowers dimension by one, so exactly its dimension many maps give an isomorphism to the sum of one-dimensional coinvariants. Integral kernel and cokernel are finitely generated torsion modules. Identification of this dimension with the source's r is the new paragraph reserved for the independent Gaussian review.

Finite coefficient extension preserves these ranks and detects vanishing or torsion by faithful flatness. A finite A-basis identifies continuous cochains after extension with a finite direct sum, so the asserted cohomological base change is valid. The discriminant is computed in A before any extension, and no square root is used as an integral coordinate.
