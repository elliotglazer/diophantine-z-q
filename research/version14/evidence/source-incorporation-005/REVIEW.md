## Available objects and the required object

The needed output is a multiplicity-one equivariant reduction map on a **single fixed orientation component of labelled enhanced curves**, and its restriction to every fiber over an underlying enhanced supersingular pair. It is not an injection after forgetting the embedding and is not an assertion that both inert orientations arise by reducing one fixed CM type.

The actual field is F=Q(sqrt(D h_*)), with maximal order O. The consumer's filter supplies odd fundamental discriminant, units {1,-1}, splitting at every prime of 2N, and an odd inert q=ell_0 outside N disc(F). A cyclic subgroup of order N is selected by an O-eigensummand at every p^e exactly dividing N. Both supplements use that same enhancement; neither introduces a full level basis. They allow arbitrary e, including at p=2. The excluded extra-unit fields and the nonmaximal-order or conductor-changing cases are outside this review.

On the source side, fixed-type uniformization identifies the labelled curves with fractional O-ideals up to F* scaling. The chosen ideal-annihilator level subgroup is intrinsic and is carried by every O-linear isomorphism. The CM supplement does not infer this from equality of j alone: same-type characteristic-zero differential faithfulness upgrades the isomorphism to an O-linear one. Its finite set of H-models permits a common finite bad-prime set; ideal approximation then represents every class by ideals avoiding that set and Nq. Thus the good-prime reciprocity formula covers the complete class group, not a possibly smaller subgroup chosen for good reduction.

The CM supplement retains Silverman's printed Frobenius convention. Its auxiliary arithmetic-Frobenius definition from Milne notes is expressly qualified, not a new published-proof substitute. This does not leave an ambiguity in the actual quadratic sum: using the opposite global Artin convention inverts every ideal class, and chi(a^-1)=chi(a). It does not permit separate inversions or separate reduction places for different terms.

## Target torsor and remaining algebra inputs

Fixing an embedding of F into B and a reference enhanced lattice, the target classification is independent of reduction surjectivity. The rational conjugacy step uses the final Skolem–Noether statement for simple Q-algebras with central codomain B. F is a quadratic field, B a four-dimensional central simple algebra, and the maps are labelled unital embeddings. The centralizer dimension formula gives dimension two; since it contains F it equals F. Thus the remaining group is F*, not its normalizer. The final [chapter7](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-56694-4_7) supplies Main Theorem7.7.1 and Proposition7.7.8(b); its section7.7.12 also records the resulting embedding parametrization.

At a split quaternion place away from Nq, identify the two-dimensional Q_p space with the rank-one F_p module. An O_p-stable full lattice is a fractional ideal; over a quadratic local field its maximal order is a DVR, and for the split algebra it is a product of two DVRs. Its ideal is principal in either case. Ramification of F at primes dividing its discriminant therefore causes no extra orbit. At a level place, the two idempotents split each lattice. If both elementary quotient exponents were positive, its p-torsion would have dimension two, contrary to cyclicity. Hence only (e,0) and (0,e) occur. This is precisely the argument in the full proof, not a squarefree-level shortcut. It includes p=2 because the product-order idempotents are integral.

For q, the final [chapter13](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-56694-4_13), Proposition13.3.4 and Theorem13.3.11, supplies the valuation order, its unique maximal ideal, and the unramified-quadratic cyclic presentation. The normalization is w=v_q(nrd)/2. Conjugate the displayed unramified field to the actual embedded F_q using Skolem–Noether; uniqueness of the maximal order preserves its integral part. Every nonzero element is a unit times a power of a division uniformizer. Even powers are central powers of q and can be absorbed in F_q*. Units act trivially by conjugation on the commutative residue field; an odd power induces its nontrivial automorphism. Therefore exactly two unit-conjugacy classes of labelled embeddings occur, and fixing the residue embedding leaves one local F_q* orbit. This also verifies that x and -x have different q-orientations when q is odd and the residue of x is nonzero.

For the rational B and F embedding already used by the manuscript, the final [chapter14](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-56694-4_14), Main Theorem14.6.1 and Proposition14.6.7(v) implies (iii), applies over Q: {q,infinity} is an eligible two-place ramification set, and the imaginary quadratic F is a field at both places. These are published imports, not conclusions inferred from a reference curve which presupposes the desired reduction bijection.

Combining the local orbits gives Fhat* z_0 Rhat*. Its intersection stabilizer is Ohat*: elements of the ambient order lying in F are integral, hence lie in O by maximality, and the same holds for inverses. The quotient is the full Pic(O). This proves a free transitive target action. It assumes no count or lifting theorem from CJ or JK.

## Reduction, coordinate direction and fibers

The specialization supplement supplies a common good-model diagram at the one fixed place. Its extension uniqueness preserves O-actions and compositions. A nonzero O endomorphism cannot specialize to zero: composing it with its dual yields [deg], which remains a nonzero morphism. Maximality of O then gives optimality. Prime-to-q ideal kernels are finite etale, and their quotient and selected level subgroup specialize to the corresponding quotient on the special fiber. The differential of this prime-to-q isogeny is nonzero, so it intertwines the same tangent residue embedding. At q it is an isomorphism of q-divisible groups, not an assertion about a rank-two etale q-adic Tate module.

Ribet's reference module is Hom(E_0,E), a right R-module. Under a quasi-isogeny E to E_0 it is z Rhat; its left order is B intersect z Rhat z^-1. This is the consumer's R_z. A prime-to-Nq ideal quotient replaces each rationally identified CM lattice by a^-1 times that lattice, so it changes this coordinate by a^-1 z. Its q-component remains an isomorphism, and its N-component preserves the chosen enhancement. That is the same inverse-ideal action as the CM supplement. The use of a z_0 in the displayed sum is legitimate after the single global reindexing described there, because the class character is quadratic. No transporter may be inverted alone while keeping its old left-order formula.

Now both sets are nonempty torsors for the same action, anchored by the reduction of the actual reference CM point. The specialization equality makes the map equivariant, so it is bijective. Restricting this bijection over any underlying enhanced pair gives exactly its labelled embedding classes, with multiplicity one. This is the only surjectivity argument needed. No unique CM lift or surjectivity theorem from the old suppliers is hidden in it.

At p^e|N, the quotient and dual subgroup exchange the two eigensummands and commute with this reduction. At q, Frobenius supplies the other orientation geometrically. It does not put that orientation in the image of the same fixed type. Ribet's involution on coarse supersingular points makes them F_(q^2)-valued; no scalar-Frobenius model of every labelled curve is required. Once the separately supplied parametrization over F_q is applied, all values are in the domain of the same lambda_M. Arithmetic and geometric Frobenius agree there because their square is the identity.
