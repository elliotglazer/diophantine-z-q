# Later bounded selection for Section 13

Author proposal, observed run 01a1beda-3166-7970-ae1a-067e89f83280. This is a new explicit application argument, with prior exposure disclosed; the earlier initial-selection review is not asserted to accept this application. It uses the actual Section 13 hypotheses at proof.tex lines26–39 and88–108.

Fix standard s>=3 and d=s-2>=1, a nonempty internal finite interval J=[N], an internal parameter set H, bounded f and the actual globally represented unit chi(h,n). Assume for every h that Delta_h f has degree-at-most d correlation with chi(h,.), in the stated tensor/component convention. Equivalently some scalar component u_h(n) of the tensor involving Delta_h f and chi(h,n) has nonzero standard-size average against a scalar degree-at-most d nilsequence. The finite coordinate and conjugation choices are retained as part of the witness. This is the lower-test correlation premise of Section 13, not an inverse theorem premise.

For each h choose such a lower test only to establish existence. Apply the ordinary-test approximation in GTZ Lemma6.4 to its fixed compact model. Its proof first approximates a bounded Lipschitz limit test by an ordinary standard Lipschitz test, then uses global ordinary unit vertical functions. Approximate more closely than half the absolute correlation divided by max(1,||u_h||_infinity). A finite component sum with finite coefficient sum results, so at least one component of one ordinary globally equivariant unit-vector test on its polynomial orbit has positive standard-size correlation with u_h. The orbit may be a limit polynomial; the test is chosen from an ordinary global unit class. Conjugation, if needed by the correlation convention, preserves that class. Thus existence is established for every h before uniformity is claimed.

Make that witness class a genuine sigma-limit set as follows. Enumerate the countable rational filtered model types of degree at most d, equip each with one fixed smooth metric, and enumerate their integral vertical characters and finite target dimensions. For each ordinary integer M form the ordinary parameter set T_M with the first M model/character/dimension tags, test Lipschitz and sup bounds at most M, and a positive reciprocal correlation threshold with denominator at most M. Include all ordinary globally equivariant unit tests satisfying those bounds, not just a finite list of functions. Include all compatible polynomial Taylor coefficients, without bounding them. Include the finitely many scalar-coordinate and conjugation choices. Choose the sets increasing and put them inside one ordinary disjoint-union parameter set T. Every ordinary test just constructed, together with its limit Taylor coefficients, belongs to *T_M for some standard M. The sequence M -> T_M is one ordinary sequence, and its natural extension supplies precisely the internal exhaustion required by Definition A.10. This is stronger data than an arbitrary countable family of internal sets.

The relation R(h,t) asserts that the encoded test evaluated on the encoded polynomial orbit has correlation at least the reciprocal threshold encoded by t with the encoded component u_h, on J. Finite sums and evaluation are internal. The universal norm-one and vertical conditions already belong to the ordinary test parameter set and hence transfer to all limit points and all limit top-group elements. R is therefore an internal relation between H and *T; no external condition such as 'the correlation is positive standard' is inserted in R.

Every h has a witness in the standard-stage union by the preceding existence argument. Apply Lemma A.11 to obtain an internal witness map. More explicitly, its proof takes the least internal stage supporting a witness; this is an internal pointwise bounded integer-valued function, hence is bounded by one standard M0 by the finite-intersection argument below (rather than an unjustified diagonal choice). Internal choice in *T_M0 gives the map. Consequently the model list, dimensions, Lipschitz bounds and reciprocal threshold are all bounded by that standard stage. The polynomial orbit coefficients remain unrestricted, as required. No compactness in the noncompact orbit parameter is used.

For completeness, the bounded-stage argument has no hidden diagonal assumption. If the least-stage map were not bounded by any standard M, its internal sets E_M of parameters requiring stage greater than M would all be nonempty and decreasing. Choose representatives E_(M,i) decreasing in M by taking finite intersections, and let S_M={i:E_(M,i) is nonempty}. Each S_M is ultrafilter-large and the S_M decrease. At coordinate i choose k_i=max({k<=i:i belongs to S_k} union {0}); when k_i>0 choose a point in E_(k_i,i). For each fixed M, on the ultrafilter-large set S_M intersect {i>=M}, k_i>=M, so this chosen point is in E_(M,i). Its limit belongs to every E_M, contradicting pointwise standard-stage existence. This establishes the bounded stage used by the A.11 selection proof.

Pad the finitely many target dimensions with zero coordinates. If H has positive density, a finite pigeonhole fixes a scalar coordinate and any needed convention choices on a still positive-density internal subset. This is exactly the scalar correlation required for the plain-quadruple application, while the full unit vectors remain available. Outside the retained subset use a constant unit representative and keep the parameter indicator separate. Empty H gives no positive-density application and is not used; d=0 is not invoked here because s>=3.

Conclusion: Section 13 obtains the internal bounded strong represented family phi_h from its actual lower-test correlation premise. No symbol is prescribed, no arbitrary preexisting weak nilcharacter is upgraded, and no initial inverse theorem is invoked. The external ingredients are Lemma6.4's ordinary global-test construction, countability of model tags at p.1250, and A.5/A.10/A.11. The published E.6 proof and its E.4 splitting step are not needed for this route.
