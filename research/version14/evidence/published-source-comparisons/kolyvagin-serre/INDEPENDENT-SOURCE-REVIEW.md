For the local character I read Serre's definitions in Chapter III, section 1.1, the theorem in section 1.2 and Appendix A.7, and directly inspected the statement on printed page III-52. The 1989 book PDF is `971dbfdd4414d99ea21d7d0af63dbb790d7b1188e9aec37f422181a2b543dec4`. The theorem requires an abelian finite-dimensional Q5-representation of the local field, Hodge–Tate type, and semisimplicity on inertia. It does not assert this conclusion for an arbitrary Hodge–Tate representation.

The proposed application discharges those conditions. A one-dimensional E-valued character becomes a direct sum of its coefficient embeddings after scalar extension, so its underlying Q5-representation and inertia restriction are semisimple. It is abelian. Its de Rham property and the implication to Hodge–Tate remain explicit upstream p-adic Hodge inputs. The local base field is Q5, so Serre's source torus is Gm over Q5.

The associated algebraic representation commutes with E on an open subgroup of Q5 units. Such an open subset is Zariski dense in Gm, so it commutes with E everywhere and factors through the invertible E-linear endomorphisms, Res(E/Q5)Gm. The adjunction for restriction of scalars identifies this group homomorphism with an E-homomorphism Gm to Gm, hence an integral power. This uses morphisms of algebraic groups, not arbitrary regular maps. Serre evaluates the algebraic map at the inverse of the local unit; the cyclotomic character uses that same inverse. Thus an integral cyclotomic power agrees with the character on an open inertia subgroup.

After division, inertia at 5 has finite image. At another prime l, the local principal-unit subgroup is pro-l, whereas an open subgroup of E units is pro-5. Its image is therefore finite; the finite unit quotient adds only finite image. The existing finite-ramification hypothesis gives one finite conductor. Global class field theory over Q, including the real place when needed, then gives a finite ray-class character. This justifies the proposed paragraph's global step. The subsequent two-weight comparison is unchanged.
