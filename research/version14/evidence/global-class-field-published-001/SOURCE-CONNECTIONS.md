# Published global class-field inputs and their actual specializations

Author: /root/v14_arithmetic_review. Proposal only; independent review and source adoption pending. No canonical file is changed.

HILBERT SOURCE. Cox, Primes of the Form x^2+ny^2, Wiley 1989, Theorems5.18 and5.23, pp106,109, and Theorem8.10/equation8.9 p164: a number field has a finite maximal everywhere-unramified abelian extension; its arithmetic ideal Artin map identifies its Galois group with the ordinary ideal class group.

The Frobenius convention is Cox Lemma5.19 and Corollary5.21, pp106–107: Frobenius acts by the residue-field norm power; conjugating a place conjugates Frobenius, and trivial Frobenius is equivalent to complete splitting at an unramified prime.

RING SOURCE. Cox Proposition7.22 pp145–146 identifies the invertible ideal class group of an imaginary quadratic order O_f with I_K(f)/P_(K,Z)(f), where the principal subgroup is generated by elements congruent modulo fO_K to a rational integer prime to f. Theorems8.2,8.5,8.6 pp161–162 and the construction at p180 identify this quotient with the ring-class Galois group, unramified outside f. Lemma9.3 pp180–181 gives normality over Q and conjugation by complex conjugation as inversion. The exact sequence and residue-unit calculation (7.25)–(7.27), p147, identify the kernel over Cl(K) with (O_K/fO_K)^*/(Z/fZ)^* when O_K^*={+1,-1}. These are ideal-theoretic statements, not a claim that Cox states the entire local-idelic reciprocity theorem.

Only invertible order ideals occur. Cox Proposition7.4 p135 and the definitions at p136 make this restriction explicit; noninvertible ideals are not substituted.

CHEBOTAREV SOURCE. Cox Theorem8.17 p170: for a finite Galois extension L/K of number fields and a conjugacy class C in Gal(L/K), the unramified prime ideals with Frobenius class C have Dirichlet density |C|/[L:K]. The definitions and elementary density properties at pp169–170 permit deleting a finite set without changing that density. Combine with the place convention at pp106–107 to choose a representative within C.

There is no effective bound or assertion about an infinite Galois extension here. Compatibility of prescribed actions must already give an element of the finite Galois group.

IMAGINARY QUADRATIC SOURCE. Cox Theorem2.13 p29 and Theorem7.7 p137 identify an imaginary quadratic order class group with a finite class group of positive definite forms. The discussion at p103 states that the unit group of an imaginary quadratic maximal order is finite, with only the two familiar exceptional fields beyond {+1,-1}. No general-number-field unit rank or Minkowski bound is attributed to this source.

APPLICATION BRIDGES. The following are local deductions connecting these published statements to unchanged consumer assertions; they do not reprove class field theory.

For an invertible O_f ideal, local generators give an element of the finite ideles, well defined modulo K^* and completed order units. Conversely intersecting K with the corresponding product of local lattices gives the ideal. These inverse constructions are the retained closure-batch-009 section1 argument. Thus the ideal quotient in Cox describes the identical Pic(O_f) as the existing idelic notation, rather than a new class field. At p dividing f, O_(f,p)^*=Z_p^*(1+f O_(K,p)); factoring a unit a+fb by its rational unit a verifies the quotient used there. The arithmetic prime-ideal convention is unchanged.

For n a product of new primes, the kernel Pic(O_(fn)) -> Pic(O_f) is the quotient of completed O_f units by completed O_(fn) units and O_f^*. This follows directly from the two nested idelic quotients, not from splitting a class-group sequence. Under the actual O_K^*={+1,-1} premise, global units are already in the denominator. At each new inert ell, reduction identifies the local factor with F_(ell^2)^*/F_ell^*, cyclic of order ell+1. Distinct primes give a full product by the product of local unit groups. For a new split r the factor is (F_r^* x F_r^*)/diagonal(F_r^*) = F_r^* via u/v. This preserves the actual all-relation-lattice presentation and its chosen orientation; no division by a class number is used.

Cox gives unramifiedness outside the order conductor directly. A finite compositum of the resulting local unramified extensions is unramified. The separately supplied splitting of K at2N then identifies the base completion with Q_p. At a new inert ell prime to the old conductor, the K-prime (ell) is rational principal, hence trivial in the ring-class ideal quotient; trivial arithmetic Frobenius makes it split completely in the old ring-class field, retaining residue field F_(ell^2), not F_ell.

For the Hilbert input take conductor1 and Cox Theorem5.23. For either finite-Chebotarev caller set K=Q, take the already constructed compatible element sigma, and apply Theorem8.17 to its class. Corollary5.21(i), together with transitivity on places, changes the place to make Frobenius exactly sigma. Delete the finite exclusions. This changes neither precision order nor the prior compatibility problem.

For the imaginary quadratic S-unit input, the valuation map O_(K,S)^* -> Z^S has kernel O_K^* and finite-index image: for each prime in S, a positive power is principal because Cl(K) is finite. The image is a free group of rank |S|, so the exact sequence splits as abelian groups. The finite subgroup O_K^* of C^* is cyclic and contains -1, hence its quotient by squares has order2, including K=Q(i) or Q(sqrt(-3)). Consequently the squareclass dimension is1+|S|. This is the existing valuation argument with the narrower published imaginary-quadratic inputs; it does not provide a bound for the S-class-group two-rank.
