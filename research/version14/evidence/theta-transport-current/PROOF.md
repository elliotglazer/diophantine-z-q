# Coefficient conjugation and finite twists in the theta construction

Author: /root/v14_gi_selection_review. This is a proposed connecting proof, pending independent acceptance. It applies at manuscript 14669–14698; the original packet, support and proportionality premises remain separate.

1. Let L be divisible by 4, let g be an algebraic-coefficient cusp form of weight 3/2 and character psi at level L, in Ramsey's theta convention, and let tau be a field automorphism of Qbar. Use a coefficient field containing the character values and the chosen L-th root of unity. Ramsey Definition 3.1 identifies g/theta^3 with a section on X_1(L) whose divisor and cusp conditions are defined over the base. Field conjugation preserves those conditions and sends the diamond character to tau(psi). Only flat field extension is used; the weight-at-least-5 vanishing theorem is not used.

2. The chosen Tate cusp need not be rational. If tau(zeta_L)=zeta_L^a, conjugation gives the conjugate q-expansion at the conjugate marking. This marking differs from the fixed one by diamond a. The section is a diamond eigenvector, so these two expansions differ by a nonzero constant. Rescale by that constant to obtain precisely the coefficientwise conjugate expansion at the fixed marking. At this standard marking the theta series has rational coefficients. Thus this procedure gives the form g^tau with coefficients tau(a(n)), with character tau(psi). It preserves cuspidality at all cusps.

3. Ramsey Theorem 6.2 at k=3 gives the good T(p^2) coefficient formula a(p^2 n)+psi(p)(-n/p)a(n)+p psi(p^2)a(n/p^2), with the last coefficient zero unless its index is integral. All non-character factors are rational. Therefore conjugation sends good eigenvalues eta(p)a_p(E) to tau(eta(p))a_p(E), using the separately given rational elliptic-curve traces.

4. Let nu be primitive of conductor m. Define h(z)=G(bar(nu))^(-1) sum_{t mod m} bar(nu)(t)g(z+t/m). The primitive Gauss identity, including the zero value when (n,m)>1, gives h(z)=sum_n nu(n)a(n)q^n. The nonzero primitive Gauss sum and this finite Fourier identity are the elementary character-sum inputs. For m=1 this is simply h=g.

5. For gamma=(a,b;c,d) in Gamma_0(L m^2), choose t' congruent to d^2 t modulo m. Direct multiplication gives n(t/m) gamma = gamma_t n(t'/m), where gamma_t has entries a+tc/m, b+(td-at')/m-ctt'/m^2, c, d-ct'/m. These are integers because ad is congruent to 1 modulo m and c is divisible by m^2. The matrix has determinant one and belongs to Gamma_0(L). Write d'=d-ct'/m.

6. Ramsey's p.600 theta multiplier is epsilon_d^(-1)(c/d)(cz+d)^(1/2). Here d' is congruent to d modulo L and modulo 4. Also (c/d')=(c/d). To see the latter for c nonzero, write c=s r^2 with signed squarefree s. On odd integers prime to c the symbol equals the quadratic character (s/.) whose conductor divides 4|s|. This modulus divides c/m: for an odd prime occurring in s, v_p(c)-v_p(m)>=1 since v_p(c)>=2v_p(m); at 2, v_2(c)>=2+2v_2(m), which gives v_2(c)-v_2(m)>=2 if v_2(c) is even, and at least 3 if v_2(c) is odd. These are exactly the required bounds for 4|s|. The signed Kronecker/Jacobi convention includes negative c and odd negative denominators. Consequently the character values at d and d' agree. For c=0, gamma has diagonal entries ±1 and an integral translation; the identity follows directly from periodicity and the original -I transformation law.

7. The analytic square-root factors are identical, since c(z+t'/m)+d'=cz+d, so there is no new branch sign. Applying the transformation law and reindexing t'=d^2t multiplies the character-sum weights by nu(d)^2. Hence h has character psi nu^2 on Gamma_0(Lm^2).

8. Each rational translation maps a rational cusp to a rational cusp. After composing a cusp scaling matrix with that translation and a scaling matrix for its image cusp, the remaining upper-triangular real matrix has positive ratio of diagonal entries. It therefore rescales the imaginary variable by a positive rational number. The vanishing cusp expansion of g remains vanishing. Choose a common cusp width for the finite set of translations to conclude that their finite sum is cuspidal. This proves the needed twist modularity and shows that the enlarged level introduces only primes dividing Lm.

9. For the manuscript character nu=eta^((e-1)/2), one may use the inducing primitive character. The prescribed unit support of g at S_0 makes its coefficient product identical to the possibly imprimitive presentation: at excluded nonunits the coefficients of g already vanish, and at integers prime to S_0 the two character values agree. The conductor m is supported on S_0. Alternatively, only the primitive twist is needed for the comparison at D and D0, both prime to S_0.

10. At p outside Lm, direct substitution in the formula of paragraph 3 gives T(p^2)h=nu(p)^2 lambda_p h if T(p^2)g=lambda_p g. This holds also for indices divisible by p, with the same convention for nonintegral indices. Since psi/eta is quadratic and e is odd, psi nu^2=psi^e and nu(p)^2 eta(p)=eta(p)^e.

11. The final comparison uses the separately supplied full good-Hecke-eigenspace proportionality theorem at a common level supported on S_0. It is not proved by this note. The actual D,D0 must satisfy its squareclass conditions, D0 must be prime to S_0, and a(D0)=1. Under those hypotheses the nonzero reference denominator is nu(D0), and coefficientwise conjugation yields tau(a(D))=nu(D/D0)a(D). No rationality of an L-value quotient is assumed.
