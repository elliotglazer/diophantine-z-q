# Additive finite-field input for formal cocycle correction

Author: /root/v14_arithmetic_author. New local proof; pending independent review and explicit source extension.

## Claim and elementary proof

Let k have q elements and kprime have q^r elements, with r>=1, and let sigma(x)=x^q generate Gal(kprime/k). The additive trace T=1+sigma+...+sigma^(r-1) maps kprime to k. Its polynomial X+X^q+...+X^(q^(r-1)) is nonzero and has degree q^(r-1)<q^r (also when r=1), so it cannot vanish on all of kprime. Thus T is a nonzero k-linear map to the one-dimensional k-space k and is surjective.

The kernel of sigma-1 is exactly k: its elements solve X^q-X=0, whose q roots are the elements of k. Therefore im(sigma-1) has k-dimension r-1. The telescoping identity T(sigma-1)=sigma^r-1=0 and the dimension of ker(T) show im(sigma-1)=ker(T). This includes r=1, when both spaces are zero.

An additive cyclic cocycle is determined by its value x at sigma. The cocycle relation at sigma^r=1 says T(x)=0. Hence x=(sigma-1)y for some y in kprime. The cocycle and the coboundary of y agree at the generator, so agree on the whole cyclic group. Consequently H1(Gal(kprime/k),kprime)=0. No division by r is used, even when the characteristic divides r.

## Normal-basis and higher-cohomology refinement

Regard sigma as a k-linear operator on the r-dimensional space kprime. If a nonzero polynomial P of degree m<r annihilated sigma, the polynomial sum_i a_i X^(q^i), of degree at most q^m<q^r, would vanish on every element of kprime. This is impossible. Since sigma^r=1, its minimal polynomial is therefore t^r-1, of degree r.

Here is the elementary cyclic-vector argument, including the inseparable-polynomial case. Factor the minimal polynomial as a product of distinct irreducible powers p_j^e_j. Bezout identities give the direct-sum primary decomposition into kernels of p_j(sigma)^e_j, with polynomial projection maps. On the jth summand the minimal annihilating power is exactly e_j, so a vector v_j exists with p_j(sigma)^(e_j-1)v_j nonzero; its annihilator is p_j^e_j. The sum v of these vectors has annihilator the product, since a polynomial kills v exactly when it kills each primary component. Thus v,sigma(v),...,sigma^(r-1)(v) are linearly independent and form a k-basis. This proves kprime is the regular module k[G], without a separability assumption on t^r-1.

For completeness, the usual cyclic free resolution can be verified directly in Z[G]: the kernel of sigma-1 consists of constant-coefficient sums, hence is generated by N=1+sigma+...+sigma^(r-1), and the kernel of multiplication by N consists of coefficient-sum-zero elements, hence is the image of sigma-1. Together with the augmentation this gives the alternating free resolution. Applying Hom_Z[G](-,kprime), all positive odd cohomology is ker(T)/im(sigma-1)=0 and all positive even cohomology is ker(sigma-1)/im(T)=0 by the trace calculation above. This also directly verifies the vanishing in residue characteristic dividing r.
