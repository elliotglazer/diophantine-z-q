# GTZ-FINITE-FOURIER — bounded source statement

This is a project paraphrase, not the publication.

Source: Ben Green, Terence Tao and Tamar Ziegler, An inverse theorem for the Gowers U^{s+1}[N]-norm, Annals of Mathematics 176 (2012), 1231–1372. Final publisher PDF SHA256 cd320f5c1217df9bb41395e847ae2a924e70521021ca1b01c7abc20550123be1. Locator: Lemma E.5, pp.1352–1353.

**statement**: A degree-at-most d nilsequence can be uniformly approximated to any positive standard tolerance by a finite sum of bounded linear functionals of psi_j tensor chi_j, where psi_j is lower degree and chi_j is a degree-at-most d unit-vector nilcharacter.

**objects**: The stated filtered abelian domain and nilsequence, lower-support tests, unit-vector nilcharacters and finite linear functionals.

**hypotheses**: The nilsequence and degree hypotheses of E.5. The current applications use taut one-variable geometry with central top torus; the proof constructs ordinary global vertical tests.

**quantifiers**: Each such nilsequence and each positive standard approximation tolerance.

**parameter range**: Fixed standard finite degree d and fixed compact geometry for each application.

**uniformity**: Uniform approximation on the domain for the selected nilsequence. Uniform finite lists over a bounded class require the additional compact-net argument.

**constants**: Finite coefficients and dimensions may depend on the tolerance, geometry and test; they are not automatically uniform over an unbounded class.

**normalizations**: The chi_j are unit vectors; their components and functional coefficients remain in the finite sum.

**exceptional cases**: The statement is not a procedure upgrading an arbitrary weak nilcharacter to a strong one, and does not discard the lower-degree factors.

**limit order**: Fix the ordinary geometry/test and tolerance, construct the finite approximation, then make any separately justified uniform or internal selection.

**Application boundary**: The current LP02, rank and selection proofs supply finite compact nets, tolerance budgets, internal choices and strong represented-family bounds. Those are local application steps, not an unqualified uniformity clause in E.5.
