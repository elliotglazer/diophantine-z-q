# Published inputs for the finite real cyclotomic calculation

Cox, Primes of the Form x^2+ny^2, first edition (1989), pp101–102, Theorem5.9 and Propositions5.10–5.11: in a finite Galois extension efg is the degree, inertia has order e, and a prime at which an integral primitive element has separable minimal polynomial modulo that prime is unramified.

Cox, same edition, pp105–109, Theorems5.18 and5.23 and Corollary5.24: for any number field, the ordinary Hilbert class field is the maximal finite unramified abelian extension; its Galois group is the ordinary ideal class group. Unramified includes all infinite primes, so real primes split. This is a generic theorem profile, distinct from the previous imaginary-quadratic specialization.

Milne, Arithmetic Duality Theorems, second edition (2006), II Proposition2.1 and Remark2.2(a), pp163–165, and Proposition2.9 with its Kummer sequence, pp170–171: H^1(U,G_m)=Pic(U); H^2(U,G_m) embeds into the local Brauer groups at the omitted places as the kernel of the sum of invariants. With a finite place omitted this sum is onto. For finite locally constant coefficients of order invertible on the affine arithmetic curve U, etale cohomology agrees with ordinary G_S cohomology. The etale Kummer sequence gives the usual Picard and Brauer exact sequences. Local invariants are those of I1.1/Example1.6(b), pp17,19; at a real place the Brauer group has order2.

Milne, same edition, I Corollary4.15 p62 and Theorem5.1 p67: for finite S including archimedean places and a finite G_S-module M of invertible order, the cohomology groups are finite and |H^0||H^2|/|H^1| is the product over archimedean v of |H^0(G_v,M)|/|#M|_v. The H^0 here is ordinary, not Tate; the footnote explicitly gives value1 for trivial F_2 over a totally real field.

Milne, same edition, I Theorem4.10(c), pp56–57: for a finite G_S-module of order invertible in O_(K,S), restriction H^r(G_S,M)->product_(v real) H^r(K_v,M) is an isomorphism for every r>=3. This does not identify a fiber using ordinary real cochains with compact-support cohomology formed using complete Tate resolutions.
