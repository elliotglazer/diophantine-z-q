Size bounds for the actual ordinary-integer candidate

Scope

This note concerns ordinary-integer-definition.json, SHA256
a86790bbf1095d6b1cea9c66067ad8432dd35adc320254eecde21f9f98574714.
It bounds the syntactically specified polynomial, whether or not its proposed
ordinary-Z theorem is true. It is not a size claim about a subsequently
specialized ZFC-inconsistency polynomial or the older universal equation.
No monomial expansion or numerical evaluation of the fixed cutoff is used.

The checked assembly has one free variable t and 292,890,231,904 witnesses,
hence 292,890,231,905 total rational variables.

Proposed bound

Let T be the literal fixed guard cutoff already specified by this artifact:
T=(48*Aheight)^13, with Aheight and its dependencies in library.fixedConstants.
Then the claimed conservative bounds are

  total degree <= (T+1)^2048,
  absolute value of every integer coefficient <= 2^((T+1)^2048).

These are upper bounds, not exact values or decimal evaluations. The proof
below initially uses a maximum B and then proves B=T+1 for this artifact.
The independent audit of this new sizing argument is separate from the
earlier audit of the polynomial's conditional zero-set interpretation.

1. A uniform bound for the three finite constructors

Let B be the maximum of 2^20, T+1, and one plus the absolute value of every
fixedConstant, explicit scalar constant node, and evaluated fixed_integer
or psi-index expression in the artifact. All these are fixed integers.
Give a scalar polynomial its coefficient l1 norm. For pairs representing
a+b*sqrt(2), use ||a||_1+2||b||_1. This norm is submultiplicative and
subadditive under the exact quadratic-field operations in the source.

For the formal field variables x=x0+x1*sqrt(2) and y=y0+y1*sqrt(2),
their norms are 3, and the norm of A=-11243+7950*sqrt(2) is 27143<2^15.
In elliptic-division-polynomials.js the normalized f_n satisfy

  deg(f_n) <= (n+1)^3,
  ||f_n|| <= 2^(100*(n+1)^3), n>=0.

For n=0,1,2,3,4 this follows directly from the displayed base formulas.
The factor D=16*(x^3+A*x)^2 has degree 6 and norm <2^38. In the even
recurrence n=2m, m>=3, the larger possible sum of inductive cubic exponents
is (m+1)^3+(m+3)^3+2*m^3. Its difference from (2m+1)^3 is
4*m^3-24*m-27, which is at least 9 and increases for m>=3.
The other summand has a smaller sum. This absorbs the addition's factor 2.
In the odd recurrence n=2m+1, m>=2, the two sums are
(m+3)^3+3*(m+1)^3 and m^3+3*(m+2)^3. Their differences from (2m+2)^3
are respectively 4*m^3+6*m^2-12*m-22 and 4*m^3+6*m^2-12*m-16.
Both are at least 10. This absorbs both D and the factor 2 from subtraction.
The same inequalities absorb D's degree 6 for the degree induction.

Consequently psi_n, including its even factor 2y and the sign for negative n,
has degree <=2*(|n|+1)^3 and norm <=2^(101*(|n|+1)^3).
For |n|<B, these are at most B^4 and 2^(B^8), since B>=2^20.
The fixed_integer constructor trivially satisfies these same bounds.

For the guard coefficients, write the fixed P coordinates over denominator
32. Their numerator absolute values and the weighted sums are below 2^19.
Evaluating psi_m for 0<=m<=2T at P, using the preceding degree/norm bounds,
therefore gives a representation (a+b*sqrt(2))/c with
|a|,|b|,|c| <= 2^(160*(2T+1)^3). A common denominator is obtained by
homogenizing to the degree bound; reduction can only decrease these bounds.

For triples representing quadratic rationals of height at most H>=2, each
addition, subtraction, multiplication or defined division has a representing
triple of height <=3*H^4. This follows by common denominators and multiplying
a denominator by its conjugate. The fixed formulas

 x_n=x(P)-psi_(n+1)(P)*psi_(n-1)(P)/psi_n(P)^2,
 y_n=psi_(2n)(P)/(2*psi_n(P)^4)

use at most eight such operations after the psi evaluations, including the
scalar 2. The known nontorsion of P makes the required denominators nonzero.
Thus every reduced rational coordinate of iota(n), |n|<=T, has numerator
and denominator at most 2^K in absolute value, where

 K=2^30*(T+1)^3.

Indeed eight updates h -> 4h+log2(3), starting from
h<=1280*(T+1)^3, give h<2^30*(T+1)^3. The zero code and negative-index
sign changes also satisfy this bound.

Each guard factor uses fifteen such coordinates A_j/B_j and
D=product B_j. Both coefficients of D*X_j-(D/B_j)*A_j are bounded by
2^(15K). The norm of the sum of their fifteen squares is at most
15*2^(30K+2) <=2^(30K+6). There are at most (2T+1)^3<=8*(T+1)^3
factors. Hence the entire guard has

 degree <=16*(T+1)^3 <= B^4,
 log2(max(1,||guard||_1)) <=2^39*(T+1)^6 <= B^8.

The same two uniform bounds therefore hold for each finite constructor as
a polynomial in its indicated input coordinates: degree <= B^4 and
coefficient norm <=2^(B^8). Coefficients are integers throughout.

2. Composition bounds and the recorded syntax height

Variables have height zero. Ordinary scalar constants have height one.
An add or multiply node has height one plus the maximum input height.
A finite constructor has height one plus its maximum input height.
A template call has height one plus its callee's intrinsic height plus
the maximum height of its argument polynomials. Private witness variables
have height zero. These rules intentionally overestimate.

Inductively a polynomial of positive height h has degree <= B^(8h) and
log2(max(1,||P||_1)) <= B^(8h). Height-zero inputs are variables, with
degree one and norm one. For substitution use

 deg(P(Q)) <= deg(P)*max(1,max deg(Q_i)),
 ||P(Q)||_1 <= ||P||_1*max(1,max ||Q_i||_1)^deg(P).

The extra height at a call absorbs the sum of the two logarithmic bounds.
For a constructor with nonzero argument height k, its log bound is at most
B^8+B^4*B^(8k), which is <=B^(8(k+1)). With height-zero arguments the
substitution has norm-one arguments, so its bound is simply B^8.
Binary addition and multiplication satisfy the rules by subadditivity and
submultiplicativity, using B>=2.

Every model-recipe point argument is a coordinate variable, a literal
constant, a generator variable, or its negative. Height two bounds all of
them. Three extra levels therefore bound a substituted callee, including
the call rule. The three generator equations and five root differences
have height at most two. There are at most 2^20 constraints in each model
recipe, checked from the actual records. Squaring and summing adds two
levels: for at most B summands this absorbs their total norm, and even the
more conservative product norm bound would fit.

Composition recipes substitute only coordinate variables. Their square-sums
add two levels and their products add one level. The actual number of terms
is at most 2^20. Shared nodes do not change the polynomial or these bounds;
unused nodes being counted only makes the result larger.

The independent-of-expansion traversal in bound-ordinary-integer-polynomial.py
reads the actual hash-bound artifact, rejects unknown operations and invalid
edges, and applies these rules. It records all 23 template heights and all
seven recipe heights. The final height bound is 256. Therefore the two
bounds are B^2048 and 2^(B^2048).

3. Simplifying the fixed maximum to B=T+1

All claims here concern this artifact's literal constants, not arbitrary
extensions of the constructor language. Put F=324!, D=F*3^(256F^4), and
use its literal R,Q,E0,Aheight definitions. Certainly F>=256, so
D>2^(2^40). The literal W and the fixed program code are much smaller:
W has fewer than 1000 binary digits and every extra integer scalar has
fewer than 20,000,000 binary digits. These finite digit checks are enough;
there is no need to evaluate D. Thus W, P0, and all extra scalars are <D.

R>=D, Hmax, cstar, 10^12. The prime catalogue has exactly 22*phi(W)
entries, so Hproduct<=Hmax^(22W)<=R^(22W), and W<R. It follows that
Ns=P0*Hproduct<=R^(1+22W). Also

 ell=12*P0^2916*Hproduct^732 <= R^(2917+16104W).

Here E0=10*(D*G0)^2>=100, Q=R^(R*E0), and
T=(48*Aheight)^13 >= Q^221=R^(221*R*E0).
Since R>=W and R>=1, 2917+16104W<=19021R<22100R<=221R E0.
Thus ell,Hproduct,Ns<T. The other constants are bounded directly:
M<=cstar<=R<Q<T; F,D,G0<=R; E0<=Q; Bcm,Ccm,Aheight<=T.
All explicit scalar node constants are also below D by finite inspection.

The only psi indices in this saved library are n,n+1,n-1,2n for n=cstar
or one of the five positive extra scalars. These are <=2R+1<T.
The only fixed_integer node is ell. Hence every quantity in the definition
of B is at most T+1, and T itself is a fixedConstant. This proves B=T+1.

Practical interpretation

T is the already specified finite integer, involving the prime catalogue
and least formal multiple. It has not been evaluated numerically. Therefore
this note supplies explicit symbolic upper bounds, not decimal digits for
the actual degree or the largest coefficient. The bounds are deliberately
very loose and may be improved substantially without altering the polynomial.
