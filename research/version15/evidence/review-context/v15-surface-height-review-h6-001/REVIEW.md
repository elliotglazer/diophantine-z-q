Gaudron–Rémond, Nouveaux théorèmes d'isogénie, Mémoires SMF 176 (2023), publisher PDF, printed pages 7–8: the original stable-height convention is explicit. Its Xi definition and Theorem 1.9(1) give degree at most Xi(A0)^2 for an isogeny from A0 to A. Taking dimension 2 yields exactly (14^32 d' max(1,log d',hF(A0)))^16. Take both the theorem's base field and its extension field equal to the manuscript's L'. No extra splitting-field degree or endomorphism assumption is required.

Gaudron–Rémond, Théorème des périodes et degrés minimaux d'isogénies, Comment. Math. Helv. 89 (2014), publisher PDF, printed pages 352 and 400: Corollary 8.4 gives h(A)>=-dim(A)log(2pi)/2 in its h convention. Section 2.3 has hF=h-dim(A)log(pi)/2. Hence hF(A)>=-dim(A)log(pi sqrt2)>-3dim(A)/2, with no principal-polarization restriction. The same section gives the absolute isogeny height difference bound by half log degree. This verifies the lower bound and cost independently of access to Rémond's final version.

For the actual application, an L'-isogeny-factor assumption yields an L'-homomorphism A→J with finite kernel and image A0. Thus A0 is a surface defined over L', and the quotient J/A0 has dimension g−2. The subvariety inequality and lower bound imply hF(A0)<=hF(J)+3(g−2)/2<=H_J. Replacing hF(A0) by H_J in the increasing maximum is valid. Half the logarithm of the dimension-two degree bound gives the multiplier 8 in the displayed lemma. The case g=2 is included: the quotient is zero-dimensional and has height zero.

The stable subvariety inequality may be obtained after semistable extension without changing the heights; that does not require moving the subsequent isogeny theorem to the enlarged field. The varieties A0 and A already exist and are isogenous over L'. Thus d' is [L':Q], never the unrelated fibre-splitting field degree.
