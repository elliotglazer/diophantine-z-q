Equality modulo the original lattice is insufficient for equality modulo the new one. For example, vectors differing by 1 agree modulo Z but need not agree modulo 2Z. The correction must remove that discrepancy before the cover is consumed.

Lines 26822–26840 now do exactly this. For each of the finitely many weights, the difference between the actual horizontal Taylor vector and the prescribed sum of lifted frequencies is in the image of the old lattice. Selecting `gamma_(h,i)` in that lattice's intersection with `G_(i,1)` covers every required difference by the definition of the image. Internal selection is legitimate for these internal finite families. The right multiplier is an ordered product of integer binomial powers of lattice elements, so its values lie in the internal extension of the lattice. It leaves the original orbit and test values exactly unchanged. It is polynomial by the stated Taylor criterion and product rule. The horizontal Taylor map sends it to the negatives of the selected differences. No commutation of arbitrary group factors is required.

This removes the discrepancy in the real vector group at every weight. The possibly unbounded lattice multipliers do not require a new Lipschitz estimate: they alter neither the test nor the represented orbit.
