## 2. The contribution needs to be described precisely

### This corrects our proposed verification route—not a triangle claimed by the manuscript

The printed switch proof already uses **two separate exact triangles** and their common strict determinant factor. It does not assert the identity-on-strict triangle isomorphism that we subsequently considered. 

Therefore the appropriate claim is:

> We formalize an obstruction to one overly strong comparison strategy and isolate the additional compatibility required for the actual determinant argument.

It would be inaccurate to claim:

> We found and repaired an invalid triangle isomorphism in the paper.

The distinction matters mathematically, not just editorially.

### The current formal results extract consequences of a strong hypothesis

The `AxesKernel` assumption is
\[
\ker(d_F,d_S)=L(v,0)\oplus L(0,Jv).
\]

In this setting it is equivalent to the conjunction
\[
\ker d_F=Lv,\qquad
\ker d_S=LJv,\qquad
\operatorname{im}d_F\cap\operatorname{im}d_S=0.
\]

For the reverse implication, if \(d_F(x)+d_S(y)=0\), then \(d_F(x)=-d_S(y)\) lies in both images, hence vanishes; the two kernel descriptions finish the argument.

Thus the first three lemmas correctly unpack the hypothesis. **They do not establish the arithmetic self-annihilator statement that produces it.**

Likewise, the volume identity is standard determinant multiplicativity. Its formalization is useful, but these six elementary results alone would be a modest mathematical contribution. Their main value is making a sensitive proof interface explicit and preventing an invalid shortcut.

## 3. A stronger negative control: primitivity does not fix the problem

The committed checker shows that rescaling one functional by two changes the determinant valuation. I pushed this further.

**Even when both classes and both functionals are primitive and integral, the required determinant valuations can differ.**

Take \(R=\mathbb Z_2\), with fraction field \(L=\mathbb Q_2\), and let the common strict complex be
\[
\mathcal K=R^2[-2].
\]

Define two complexes in degrees one and two:
\[
C_F=[R^2\xrightarrow{D_F}R^2],
\qquad
C_S=[R^2\xrightarrow{D_S}R^2],
\]
where
\[
D_F=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&2\\
0&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
D_S=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0\\
0&4
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Set
\[
J=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
v=e_1,
\qquad
z_F=z_S=e_1,
\]
and choose the closed functionals
\[
\lambda_F=(0,1),
\qquad
\lambda_S=(1,0).
\]

Everything below holds:

- Both generic cohomology profiles are \((1,1)\).
- The combined boundary kernel is exactly \(L(e_1,0)\oplus L(0,e_1)\).
- \(Jv=v\), \(\det J=-1\), and both local quotient lattices modulo the class line are isomorphic.
- Both cycles and both functionals are primitive and integral.
- The strict complex is literally the same on both sides.

Nevertheless, in the cofactor convention used by your checker,
\[
z_F\lambda_F=u_F\operatorname{adj}(D_F),
\qquad
z_S\lambda_S=u_S\operatorname{adj}(D_S),
\]
we obtain
\[
\boxed{u_F=-\frac12,\qquad u_S=\frac14.}
\]

The determinant-line comparison induced by \(J\) therefore has
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Phi_{\det}(\tau_F)}{\tau_S}=2,
}
\]
which is not a 2-adic unit.

The reason is visible in the cohomology:
\[
H^2(C_F)\simeq R/(2)\oplus R,
\qquad
H^2(C_S)\simeq R\oplus R/(4).
\]

**Matching local class-line quotients does not control the indices of the two boundary images.**

This is not a counterexample to the arithmetic switch: it does not supply the manuscript’s compatible Poitou–Tate normalization. It is a stronger counterexample to the weaker inference from local \(J\), exact axes, integrality and primitivity alone.

That gives the next lemma a precise job.

## 4. A more substantial connecting theorem

I derived the following exact comparison. It is an **ordinary algebraic theorem in an explicit common-strict presentation**, not yet a Lean theorem or an arithmetic application.

### Setup

Let \(R\) be a DVR with fraction field \(L\). Write
\[
D_F=[D_0\mid A_F],
\qquad
D_S=[D_0\mid A_S],
\]
where \(D_0\) is an \(n\times(n-2)\) matrix and \(A_F,A_S\) each have two columns.

These are the differentials of two two-term complexes sharing
\[
\mathcal K=[R^{n-2}\xrightarrow{D_0}R^n].
\]

Retain the **actual** cycles
\[
z_F=(h_F,v),
\qquad
z_S=(h_S,Jv),
\]
where \(v\ne0\) and \(J\in\operatorname{GL}_2(R)\). Let \(\lambda_F,\lambda_S\) be nonzero closed functionals.

Choose a fractional complement \(x\in L^2\) with
\[
\eta=\det[v,x]\ne0,
\]
and put
\[
a=A_Fx,\qquad b=A_SJx.
\]

Assume that
\[
T=[D_0\mid a\mid b]
\]
is invertible over \(L\). This is the required independence of the two boundary directions modulo the strict image.

Define the **cross-boundary scalars**
\[
q_F=\lambda_F(b),
\qquad
q_S=\lambda_S(a).
\]

They evaluate each actual functional on the complementary boundary direction supplied by the other triangle.

### Exact result

Writing \(\theta=\det T\) and \(\delta=\det J\), the determinant coordinates satisfy
\[
\boxed{
u_F=-\frac{\eta q_F}{\theta},
\qquad
u_S=\frac{\delta\eta q_S}{\theta}.
}
\]

The determinant-line map supplied by the common strict factor and \(J\) contributes \(\delta\). Hence
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Phi_{\det}(\tau_F)}{\tau_S}
=
-\frac{q_F}{q_S}.
}
\tag{*}
\]

In particular,
\[
\boxed{
v_R(u_F)-v_R(u_S)
=
v_R(q_F)-v_R(q_S).
}
\tag{**}
\]

These formulas use a fixed explicit cofactor convention. Changing a consistent determinant orientation changes a sign, not the valuation conclusion.

### Why the proof works

Use the domain bases
\[
(e_1,\ldots,e_{n-2},z_F,(0,x)),
\qquad
(e_1,\ldots,e_{n-2},z_S,(0,Jx)),
\]
and the common target basis \(T\).

The final two-dimensional blocks of the differentials become
\[
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Closedness of the functionals makes their corresponding coordinates
\[
(0,q_F),\qquad(q_S,0).
\]

Their tensor coordinates are therefore \(-q_F\) and \(q_S\). Restoring the basis determinants yields the displayed formulas.

**The whole common denominator \(\theta\) cancels.** This retains, rather than discards, any nonunit strict contribution. The calculation also allows divisible \(v\), fractional complements and nonzero strict components \(h_F,h_S\). It never replaces the arithmetic class by a primitive generator.

This is an explicit two-term realization of the determinant-line comparison; it does not require an isomorphism between the two complexes. The general determinant formalism distinguishes precisely these objects. [stacks.math.columbia.edu](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0FJI)

### Why this is a meaningful improvement

The remaining question becomes:
\[
\boxed{\text{Why is }q_F/q_S\in R^\times\text{ for the actual arithmetic functionals?}}
\]

That is narrower and more testable than “verify determinant compatibility.”

It also weakens an unnecessarily strong requirement from our earlier discussion: **neither \(q_F\) nor \(q_S\) needs to be a unit individually.** A common nonunit factor cancels. What matters is their ratio.

For example, compatible boundary–cup identities of the form
\[
q_F=c\,\varepsilon\,\omega(v,x),
\qquad
q_S=c\,\varepsilon\,\omega(v,x)
\]
give the required unit comparison, even when the common normalization \(c\) is not a unit.

The outstanding arithmetic work is proving that the *same* normalization occurs, with the actual conjugate transport, orthogonal comparison, retained cochains and cone signs—not choosing functionals for which the answer works.

## 5. What should become the mathematics-bearing contribution

I would retain the current six lemmas, but make them supporting results rather than the endpoint.

The next scoped addition should establish:

| Result | Mathematical content |
|---|---|
| **Common-strict determinant-coordinate formula** | Prove \((*)\) from explicit matrices, cycles and closed functionals, including common torsion factors |
| **Unit-comparison criterion** | Deduce valuation preservation from the cross-boundary scalar ratio being a unit; allow common nonunit normalization |
| **Source-facing boundary–cup adapter** | State the exact lower-level arithmetic identities that imply that unit ratio, retaining the actual classes and functionals |

This fits your intended contribution: **connecting lemmas, not the full restricted converse**.

The critical design constraint is that the new theorem must not take the desired determinant equality as a hypothesis. It should derive it from the lower-level boundary–cup evaluations. That is the difference between a useful conditional theorem and a wrapper around the missing result.

The current contract already identifies these arithmetic obligations honestly. The improvement is to prove the algebraic implication between them and the determinant conclusion, rather than leaving that implication in prose.
