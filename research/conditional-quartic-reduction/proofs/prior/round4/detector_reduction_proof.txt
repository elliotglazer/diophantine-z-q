1. Replace primary inverse abscissas by their existing inverse roots
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Retain K=Q(rho), rho^2=2, D=(3-rho)^2, and the reciprocal primary chart

  f=g^2,  l^2=1-D*f^2,  x=1/f,  z=-g/l.

For U_1,...,U_4 replace the order-one detector test f(U_i) by g(U_i), at
the same order one. For H replace f(H) by g(H), at the same order forty.
Keep all five conjugated alternate tests f(V_i) unchanged. In particular
this candidate does not require a separately materialized inverse root ofV0.

At any consumed place, the normalized valuation has integral value group.
For a primary point, f=g^2, so

  v(f)>=1  iff  v(g)>=1.

Thus the four order-one substitutions impose precisely the same local
formal-membership requirements. If g=0, the point is O and both tests vanish;
the preceding identity-input soundness argument applies verbatim.

H is nonidentity. The new H test gives v(g(H))>=40. Because D is a unit at
every consumed prime and g(H) is positive-valuation, l(H) is a unit. Hence
v(z(H))=v(g(H))>=40 and v(f(H))>=80. This strengthens the former H test,
which gave v(f(H))>=40 and z-depth>=20. It is not claimed to be a rational
witness equivalence with the old H-test equation.

The detector factors become 1+q*g^2=1+q*f. Therefore their squared inputs
reuse existing f coordinates. Signs of the square-root chart are irrelevant
to every detector product. Each new factor is a rational function of the
underlying point, even though g itself is a chart coordinate. In particular
the completeness proof does not require a simultaneous continuous choice
of square-root sign at all places.

These substitutions remove ten raw coordinates. One of them was already
removed by the old final elimination, so the measured final saving is nine:
423->414. Replacing the five alternate order-one f tests by their existing
inverse roots was also tested. It gave the same counts because their f^2
coordinates remain required by the alternate logarithms. The smaller source
change, retaining all five alternate f tests, was selected.

4. The arbitrary-order detector lemma still applies
---------------------------------------------------
For each scaleA, retain t=A*lambda, delta=t-1, and

  e=(A*delta)^5/F(t),  R=1/(e*delta),
  q_m=e^(2m-1)/delta^3=q_1*e^(2m-2).

Indeed

  1+R*((D_j/delta^(m+1))/R^m)^2 = 1+q_m*D_j^2.

This exact identity holds for every positive integerm. The existing inverse
power chain already produces q12=q1*e^22 and q40=q1*e^78. It is unchanged.

local requirements at one common place above each odd contact prime.

5. Completeness estimates for the changed factors
------------------------------------------------
At a selected contact prime, R has valuation1 and delta is a unit. At the
successful place, v(D_j)>=m_j, so

  v(q_m*D_j^2)=1-2m_j+2v(D_j)>=1.

Every inverse factor has residue1. At either place the valuation of a
nonconstant term is odd, so its factor has valuation zero or negative and
its inverse has residue1 or0. The reciprocal trace therefore has residue1
or2, hence is a unit. The new pole input has valuation1 at its contact primes
and even valuation elsewhere, exactly as required in the inherited proof.

Real bounds for the new primary tests follow from

  |sigma(g)|^2=sigma(f)>=0.

The old fixed real bound on f supplies a fixed bound on g. The new squared
factor itself is1+q*f, with no branch ambiguity. For every fixedm the source
identity still gives q_m=O(lambda^-3). Thus W=1+O(lambda^-3), its reciprocal
trace tends to2, and the positive pole input tends to0.

6. A mixed block makes the reduced precision save variables
-----------------------------------------------------------
Separating all order12 factors into a new product would squander most of
the G2 saving. Pair the sole order12 squared testB with one order1 squared
testA. Precompute AB inK once and evaluate, for each detector,

  (1+q1*A)(1+q12*B)
    =1+q1*A+q12*B+(q1*q12)*(AB).

This uses one new scalar weight product per detector. The remaining eight
order1 tests are grouped4+4; the remaining six order40 tests are grouped3+3.
The total number of evaluated K-valued blocks stays five. The shared
coefficient cost stays the same: the mixed pair costs2 rational coordinates,
the order1 blocks20, and the order40 blocks12, for34 total, the old total.
The maximum q1 degree rises3->4 and the maximum q40 degree falls4->3, so their
power costs cancel. The sole extra cost is q1*q12 in each of three detectors:
three variables. G9->G2 removes six variables. Net saving: three.
