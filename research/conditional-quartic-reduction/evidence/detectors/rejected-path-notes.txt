ROUND-SEVEN DETECTOR SEARCH: SCOPE AND UNSELECTED PATHS

The accepted improvement is the contact-unit rescaling D -> delta D,
followed by direct q1 and E definitions. It saves three final rational
coordinates. Source/proof/checker bindings are in round7-review-bindings.json.

1. Six-node monomial chains under the old normalization

The local C++ searches enumerate exact integer exponent pairs under
A+B-C, corresponding to quadratic quotient gates. They include the old
stored q1 powers and E. The four-gate-low search restricts its first two
gates to doubled differences, with two subsequent general gates. The
six-gate-joint search restricts its first three gates to doubled differences,
then uses general gates and asks whether a supported high output is available.
These restricted searches exhausted their implemented class for low10/11/12
and high39 through51 without finding a candidate. They are not exhaustive
searches over all rational circuits, all schedules or all field products.

There is an exact obstruction for the specified old initial monomials:
L(a,b)=13a+3b takes values in[0,16]. Each A+B-C gate can at most double
the current interval width, so after six gates no value exceeds1024.
But L(q41)=1044, excluding a six-gate construction even for this one
output. For q45, the square and cube have L values2296 and3444, both
greater than the seven-gate bound2048. Thus two distinct such outputs
cannot both be produced before gate9. The old seven weight nodes plus
two high-power nodes meet that bound for this specified monomial model.
This does not apply after changing the weight normalization or introducing
nonmonomial functions, and it is not an optimality claim for the quartic.

2. Direct six-node searches under delta^-1 normalization

The same restricted searches were repeated with q1=e/delta. They found
no six-node candidate in the implemented class. The successful route
instead removes the separately stored e: q1=A*(A delta)^4/F and
E=q1 delta^2. Its seven higher-weight nodes remain, but the direct
q1 definition saves one surviving coordinate per detector. Full compilation
was essential because merely deleting delta^3 would double-count an old
final elimination.

3. Stronger normalization delta^2 D

This makes q1=E and seems attractive algebraically, but nonconstant field
factors then grow rather than decay at real infinity. The actual
preparation-zero pole converse requires b<17. No complete replacement
for its real argument was established for this stronger normalization.
It is not used. A separate rational scaling of the pole input might
restore that property, but would require a new cost and domain audit.

4. Product regrouping and precision allocation

I revisited the retained [4,4] order-one blocks, [3,3] high-order blocks
and one mixed pair. Simple uniform regroupings trade fewer shared
coefficient coordinates against more threefold product evaluations or
more weight powers; no measured improvement was established. Moving
unnecessarily strong tests to order12 is a possible mathematical option,
but was not needed for the accepted reduction and is not silently used
in its proof. All actual final orders remain1,12,45.

The selected source has no changes to alternate point depth, primary
Pi^44 divisibility, G12/G2 logarithms, point charts, pole predicate or
exceptional set. Its separate proof treats the revised real and
small-prime limits explicitly.
