# Heights and the finite search

The $x$-height on $C$ changes under doubling by a factor four up to the explicit constants
$\log392$ and $\log676$, coming from the duplication forms, their archimedean sizes and their
common divisor at $(s)$ and $(\delta)$.

## The canonical height

The canonical height is the limit of $4^{-m}h_x([2^m]T)$; it differs from $h_x$ by bounded
amounts and is a quadratic form.

## Small points

If $R$ were $[m]S+T$ with $|m|\ge3$, the generator $S$ would have $h_x(S)<\log9$, by
$h_x(2R)=\log41$ and one explicit integer comparison.

- [The height of $2R$](height-of-2r.md)
- [Two explicit integer comparisons](height-comparisons.md)
- [A multiple of a small point](small-height-bound.md)

A point of such small height has its abscissa in an explicit finite set: $14{,}008$ roots of
$7{,}004$ primitive quadratics and $111$ rationals. An exact square test leaves only the
two-torsion abscissas and the four translates of $R$.
