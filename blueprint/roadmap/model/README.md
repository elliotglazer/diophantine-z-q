# The elliptic model of integer indices

The polynomial never manipulates an integer index directly. It represents the index $n$ by
the five rational coordinates of the point $[n]P_0$ on an elliptic curve over
$\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$, and this chapter proves that the representation is faithful: the
canonical codes form exactly the infinite cyclic group $2E(K_0)=\mathbb ZP_0$ (input
$\mathsf G$ of the assembly lemma), the membership polynomial recognizes exactly those codes
among arbitrary rational inputs, and raw addition computes the group law on canonical codes.
Beyond the classical background (two-isogeny descent, reduction modulo primes, heights and
division polynomials), the one input beyond the manuscript's elementary arguments is the
Mordell-Weil theorem for $E/K_0$, which is proved from the descent index with Mathlib's height
theory, so it is not a hypothesis; every finite check is isolated as its own statement.

## Point codes

Let $K_0=\mathbb Q(s)$ with $s^2=2$, $d=75-53s$, $E:y^2=x^3-d^2x$ and
$P_0=\bigl(\frac{1641-1160s}{8},\frac{-123720+87483s}{32}\bigr)$. A point is coded by five
rationals: $(1,0,0,0,0)$ for the identity and $(0,x_0,x_1,y_0,y_1)$ for an affine point with
$x=x_0+x_1s$, $y=y_0+y_1s$; $\iota(n)$ is the code of $[n]P_0$. The integer index and the
coordinates play distinct roles throughout.

## The generator certificate

By rank one, $R=(1+s,2s)$ on $C:y^2=x^3-(3-s)^2x$ is $[m]S+T$ with $S$ a generator modulo
torsion and $m$ odd. If $|m|\ge3$, the generator has $x$-height below $\log9$, and the finite
search shows that every such nontorsion point is $\pm(R+T')$ with $T'$ two-torsion, so has the
same canonical height as $R$, contradicting $\widehat h(R)=m^2\widehat h(S)>0$. Hence
$2C(K_0)$ is generated by $2R$, and the change of variables sends $2R$ to $P_0$.

## Membership and raw addition

Membership in $2E(K_0)$, apart from the identity, is expressed by choosing a preimage with
nonzero ordinate and its tangent slope. It is an all-input statement, and with the generator
certificate it shows that accepted inputs are exactly the codes $\iota(n)$, each with a unique
index. The template itself is defined in the specification chapter
([the membership template](../syntax/template-member.md)).

- [Polynomial membership in the doubled group](membership.md)
- [Accepted inputs have unique indices](membership-indices.md)

Raw addition has the narrower contract
$\operatorname{Add}_{\rm raw}(\iota(a),\iota(b),R)\iff R=\iota(a+b)$; its five branches are the
cases of the chord-and-tangent law, and it is correct on all canonical codes. The template is
defined in the specification chapter ([the raw addition template](../syntax/template-raw-add.md)).

- [Raw addition on canonical codes](raw-addition-points.md)
- [Raw addition](raw-addition.md)

Later chapters supply canonical codes to raw addition: multiplication first imposes
membership of its three root inputs, and in the natural predicate the four untyped tuples are
typed by multiplication calls before their squares are added, after which Lagrange's
four-square theorem gives exactly $\iota(\mathbb N)$. That step uses multiplication
completeness (see [the multiplication template](../multiplication/multiplication-template.md));
the generator certificate alone does not supply the natural predicate.
