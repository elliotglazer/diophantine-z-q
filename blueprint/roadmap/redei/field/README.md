# The field $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-n})$ and its primes

As in the rest of the chapter, $n\ge1$ is an integer and $K=\mathbb Q(\omega)$ with
$\omega^2=-n$; $\mathcal O$ is its ring of integers, $\sigma$ its nontrivial automorphism and $N$
the norm of elements and of ideals. This section collects what the genus theory of the next
section needs about $K$:

- its conjugation and norms;
- the rational integers in $\mathcal O$;
- the splitting of a rational prime into split, inert and ramified primes;
- the set $T_n$ of primes of the discriminant and the prime ideals $\mathfrak p_p$ over them,
  with $(\omega)=\prod_{p\mid n}\mathfrak p_p$;
- the identity $I\cdot\sigma(I)=(N(I))$ for ideals $I$ of $\mathcal O$.

The hypotheses vary from fact to fact: the field, its primes and $I\cdot\sigma(I)=(N(I))$ need
only $n\ge1$; that the primes of $n$ ramify needs $n$ squarefree; the description of
$\mathfrak p_p$ for every $p\in T_n$ and of $(\omega)$ needs $n$ odd and squarefree. Ramification
is decided by norms of ideals and residue fields, so no integral basis of $\mathcal O$ is needed.

## Primes

A prime of $\mathcal O$ over $p$ is split, inert or ramified according to its norm and to whether
$\sigma$ fixes it. The ramified ones lie over the primes of $T_n$, and for odd squarefree $n$
every prime of $T_n$ ramifies.
