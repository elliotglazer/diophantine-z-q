# Hilbert 90 for ideals

Let $n\ge1$, $\mathcal O$ the ring of integers of $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-n})$ and $\sigma$ the
nontrivial automorphism. Let $I$ be a nonzero ideal of $\mathcal O$ and $x,y\in\mathcal O$ with
$x\ne0$ and
$$(x)\,\sigma(I)=(y)\,I.$$
Then there is $\nu\in\mathcal O$, $\nu\ne0$, with $\sigma\bigl((\nu)I\bigr)=(\nu)I$.

Along the way, for every automorphism $\tau$ of $K$ and $x,y\in\mathcal O$:
$\tau\bigl((x)\bigr)=(\tau(x))$; $\tau(x)\ne0$ if $x\ne0$; and $(x)\,\tau(I)=(y)\,I$ with $I\ne0$
implies $N(x)=N(y)$. Also $\sigma(\sigma(x))=x$.

**Proof sketch.** Taking ideal norms gives $|N(x)|\,N(\sigma I)=|N(y)|\,N(I)$. Since
$N(\sigma I)=N(I)\ne0$ and norms of elements are nonnegative, $N(x)=N(y)$, that is,
$x\,\sigma(x)=y\,\sigma(y)$.

- If $x+y=0$, then $(y)=(x)$, so $(x)\,\sigma I=(x)\,I$; cancelling $(x)$ gives the claim with
  $\nu=1$.
- Otherwise put $\nu=\sigma(x)(x+y)\ne0$. Then $\sigma(\nu)=x(\sigma(x)+\sigma(y))$, and
  $x\sigma(x)=y\sigma(y)$ gives $y\,\sigma(\nu)=x\nu$. So
  $$(x)\,\sigma\bigl((\nu)I\bigr)=(\sigma(\nu))(y)\,I=(y\,\sigma(\nu))\,I=(x\nu)\,I=(x)(\nu)I,$$
  and cancelling $(x)$ gives the claim.
