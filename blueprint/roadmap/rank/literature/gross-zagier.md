# Gross–Zagier: a simple zero gives a point of infinite order

The *Gross–Zagier statement* is the following assertion. Let $E$ be a Weierstrass equation over
$\mathbb Q$ with nonzero discriminant, that is, an elliptic curve over $\mathbb Q$ (the equation
need not be minimal), and let $F$ be an entire continuation of $L(E,s)$. If the order of
vanishing of $F$ at $s=1$ is exactly $1$, then $E(\mathbb Q)$ has a point of infinite order.

This is the positive-rank half of the theorem of Gross and Zagier (1986), which gives a Heegner
point of infinite order over an imaginary quadratic field $K$ when $L'(E/K,1)\ne0$. It is applied
with an auxiliary imaginary quadratic field $K$ that satisfies the Heegner hypothesis and has
$L(E^K,1)\ne0$ (Bump–Friedberg–Hoffstein 1990, through Waldspurger's formula, or
Friedberg–Hoffstein 1995), and with the modularity of $E$ (Breuil–Conrad–Diamond–Taylor 2001;
Shimura 1971 for curves with complex multiplication). Since the order of vanishing of
$L(E,s)$ at $s=1$ is odd, the root number of $E$ is $-1$, so the Heegner point lies in
$E(\mathbb Q)$ up to torsion and gives a point of infinite order there. Kolyvagin's conclusion that the rank is
exactly one is not used. The exact theorem numbers, and the level and discriminant hypotheses of these
results, have not been checked here. It is a hypothesis, not proved here.
