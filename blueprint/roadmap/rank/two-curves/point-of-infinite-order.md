# A point of infinite order on a certified curve

Let $d$ be a squarefree natural number with $d\equiv5\pmod8$, and let $a$ be a squarefree integer
dividing $d$ with $|a|\equiv7\pmod8$ such that $(d,a)$ carries a Selmer certificate. If
$C_d(\mathbb Q)$ has a point of infinite order, then there are $x,y\in\mathbb Q$ with
$y^2=x^3-d^2x$, $x\ne0$ and $x/a$ a square in $\mathbb Q$.

The conclusion is that of the certified rank hypothesis CR₃ at $(d,a)$: on a certified curve,
positive rank gives the point that CR₃ asks for.

**Proof sketch.** The lemma on the 2-Selmer group of a certified curve gives
$|\mathrm{Sel}_2(C_d)|=8$ and some $(a,b)\in\mathrm{Sel}_2(C_d)$. Since $d\ne0$, the group
$C_d(\mathbb Q)$ is finitely generated by Mordell–Weil for the congruent number curves. It has a
point of infinite order and $|\mathrm{Sel}_2(C_d)|=8$, so by RK9 every Selmer class comes from a
rational point; in particular the x-form of the 2-covering $D_{a,b}$ holds over $\mathbb Q$. Here
$b\ne0$, since $b$ is squarefree. The squarefree $a$ with $|a|\equiv7\pmod8$ is not a square, so
the first alternative of the x-form is excluded, and there is $x$ with $x/a$, $(x-d)/b$ and
$(x+d)/(ab)$ squares. As in steps 3–5 of the proof of CR₃ from its Selmer form, $x\ne0$, and
$y=abr_1r_2r_3$ gives the point.
