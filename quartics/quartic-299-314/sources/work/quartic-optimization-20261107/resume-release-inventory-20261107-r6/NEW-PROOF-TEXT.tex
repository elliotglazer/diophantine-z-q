\subsection{Proposed proof text: eliminating an alternate ordinate}
\label{outline:r6-expanded-elimination}
Work over $K=\mathbb Q(\rho)$, where $\rho^2=2$, and put
$D=11-6\rho$. The negative reciprocal coordinates of a point are
$f=1/x$ and $h=-y/x^2$; the pair $(0,0)$ denotes the identity.
Retain the exact square caches $Y=f^2$ and $B=F^2$.
Set $d=f-F$ and define the omitted ordinate by
\[
 k=sd-h.
\]
The reduced equations are
\[
 h^2=f-DfY,\qquad
 R=s^2-c+D(f+F)=0,\qquad
 T=2sh-cd-1+D(2Y+fF)=0,
\]
together with $(1-DB)z=k$, written after substitution so it is quadratic.
The identity defining $k$ is a reconstruction map, not an extra
quantified coordinate.

Using the two cache equations, direct expansion gives
\begin{align*}
 k^2-F+DF^3&=(h^2-f+Df^3)-dT+d^2R,\\
 s(h-k)-1+D(f^2+fF+F^2)&=T-dR.
\end{align*}
Thus the reduced system recovers the alternate curve and the complete
three-equation reciprocal slope system without division. Its previously
proved oriented secant/tangent interpretation supplies the actual
difference $U-V=(c,Dh-s(Df+c))$. The equal-reciprocal case is the tangent
case, not a freely chosen root abscissa. If one auxiliary point is the
identity, the same equations give the appropriate signed other point;
both cannot be the identity. These are polynomial consequences before
any interpretation of a ratio or logarithm.

\paragraph{The omitted raw ratio.}
Put $A=1-DB$ and $g=Az-k$. The exact identity is
\[
 A(kz-F)=kg+(k^2-F+DFB).
\]
This identity alone does not permit cancellation of $A$. First recover
the point curves and oriented roots as above. The retained nonzero-root,
parity and scalar-anchor argument excludes the all-empty contact-radical
case without using an alternate raw ratio. At a required contact prime,
the same-place low-test argument uses the now-established curve
valuations and the literal cache powers. It gives $v(F)>0$ for a
nonidentity alternate point. Since $D$ is a unit there, $A$ is a local
unit. At the identity, $F=0$ and $A=1$ directly. Hence $A$ is nonzero in
$K$, and the displayed identity now recovers $kz=F$.

This ordering proves redundancy only in the full retained system.
It makes no claim that the regular ratio implies the raw ratio on an
isolated reciprocal cubic at a finite two-torsion point. Projection
which deletes $k$, and reconstruction $k=sd-h$, are inverse maps on
the complete rational zero sets for the A, T and Z eliminations.

\subsection{Proposed proof text: the scalar-root auxiliary pair}
\label{outline:r6-expanded-S}
Keep the entire scalar root chart
\[
 a^2=c,\qquad b_S^2=c^2-D,
\]
and every scalar equation consuming the point $Q_S=(c,ab_S)$.
In particular $b_S$ is not deleted. Replace only the S auxiliary chart
by the reduced reciprocal equations above and add
\[
 ab_S=Dh-s(Df+c).
\]
This equation is quadratic and identifies the oriented group difference
$U_S-V_S$ with the actual point consumed by the scalar subsystem.
The root curve alone would permit the opposite ordinate and would not
supply this identification.

The retained root chart and height-product inverse imply $c\ne0$,
as in the preceding construction. All root subgroup, oddness and
nonvanishing conclusions therefore retain their original inputs.
The new auxiliary points themselves need not lie in $2E(K)$.
The domain extension must be applied rather than attempting to take
unjustified square roots of their reciprocal abscissae.

\paragraph{The all-four auxiliary-domain application.}
For every auxiliary point write $U_i=k_iR_0+T_i$ and
$H=mR_0+T_H$, where $T_i,T_H\in E[2](K)$ and $P=2R_0$.
The retained integer root is $Q_i=n_iP$. The valid group identities are
\[
 [2m]U_i=[2k_i]H,\qquad
 [2(m-2)]V_i=[2(k_i-2n_i)]V_0.
\]
Both torsion terms are killed. After the low tests establish formality,
their logarithms give the same full ratios with free indices
$u_i=k_i/2$ and $h_0=m/2$. These rational numbers are integral at the
consumed odd primes. The depth-one unit convention also applies at
the identity, with $A(O)=1$, and never divides $\log(O)$ by $z(O)$.
Because one of $h_0,h_0-1$ is a unit, the original cross-test transfers
integrality to both ratios. The unit-$L_i$ argument using
$F(0,0,0)=-1$ then recovers depth10 for H before the order40
approximation is used. The resulting congruences are to the same
integer root labels $n_i$, not to allegedly integral auxiliary labels.

The V2 rational approximant now also requires the global N/Q
nonvanishing certificate on $K$. The earlier positivity interval for
doubled points is not imposed on the enlarged S auxiliary cosets.
The unchanged certificates modulo71 and modulo7 provide the needed
global statement. The scalar point, its depth24 transfer, and all

\paragraph{Completeness.}
Take complete witnesses for the preceding system. In its S chart let
the old scaled coordinates be $V_{\rm old},I_{\rm old},Q_{\rm old},
K_{\rm old}$. The retained $c=a^2$ is nonzero. Put
\[
 f=V_{\rm old}^2/c,\quad h=-I_{\rm old}V_{\rm old}/c,\quad
 F=Q_{\rm old}^2/c,\quad k=-K_{\rm old}Q_{\rm old}/c.
\]
The old cache and ratio equations give all the new curve, cache and
regular ratio equations, including identity points. Choose the rational
chord slope when $f\ne F$, and the rational tangent slope otherwise.
The old actual equality $U_S-V_S=Q_S$ gives the new ordinate equation.
Retain every other witness. No new congruence, prime, real neighborhood,
anchor value or final H choice is selected. This proves the same input
projection, without asserting a bijection to all old auxiliary
square-root fibers.
