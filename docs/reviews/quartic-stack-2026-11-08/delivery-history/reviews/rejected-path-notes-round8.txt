ROUND-EIGHT DETECTOR SEARCH: SELECTED AND UNSELECTED PATHS

Selected and measured

Promote only the primary U_A inverse-root test from order 1 to order 12.
The existing completeness witnesses already have U_A=[n]H in formal depth 45,
while soundness needs only the previous weaker depth 1. This permits a uniform
(12,12) product pair and reuse of the q12^2 coordinate already required for
q45. Each of the three detectors deletes the old q1*q12 coordinate.
The isolated actual compilation gives 389 coordinates, 357 rows and 26,295
monomials; the extra-elimination pipeline found no additional elimination.
The complete proof, source checker and gate checker are supplied separately.
An independent scalar-lane review accepted the promotion with 157 assertions,
including conservation of all 362 unaffected source equations.

1. Promotions beyond the selected primary U_A

All constructed primary multiples [n_i]H have depth 45, so further primary
promotions are mathematically plausible on the completeness side. They do
not automatically produce another saving. The selected promotion exploits a
particular asymmetry: the existing input cross test already has order 12 and
q12^2 is already materialized. Moving another primary test into that bucket
changes the remaining product group sizes and generally requires another
middle-weight power. Only rough grouped-product cost comparisons were made;
no additional candidate was compiled and certified as a reduction. This is
not an optimality claim over product circuits or order allocations.

2. Lower high order 44

The arithmetic lane's new exponent 577/32 and anchor 2364 permit the current
coarse Lucas consumer at high order 44 as well as 45. That numerical possibility
is not a gate reduction. The selected exact chain naturally has
  q1^3*q45=(q12^2)^2.
In the coordinates q_m=q1^m*E^(m-1), an analogous final expression for q44
would divide q12^4 by q1^4*E, a monomial not already stored in the selected
higher-weight chain. No complete cheaper chain, including the low order 12,
q12-squared pair coefficient, product powers and required pole E, was found.
The delivered order stays 45, and no high 44 source or count is claimed.

Orders 37–43 fail the selected arithmetic lane's particular integer margin
at the fixed anchor 2364. This does not prove those orders impossible with a
different anchor or a sharper transfer estimate. A proposed gate gain would
have to include that tradeoff and all local precision/completeness checks.

3. Earlier restricted monomial searches

The round-seven notes already record restricted exact A+B-C exponent-vector
searches and their scope. Their negative results are not new exhaustive
results for round 8 and are not a proof of global circuit optimality. Those
searches must not be cited as excluding changes of normalization, alternate
initial coordinates, nonmonomial identities or every possible schedule.
No such broader negative claim is used in the selected package.

4. Stronger contact-unit normalization

The previous tempting replacement D by delta^2*D is still unselected. It can
make a weight algebraically simpler but invalidates the old decay argument
at real infinity: the pole-completeness construction needs the positive pole
input eventually below 17. A compensating rational-square rescaling of that
input would need a new complete domain, local-square, finite-prime and
real-bound proof, together with an actual source-cost comparison. No such
replacement was implemented or claimed this round.

5. Pure syntactic deletion or witness identification

Inlining an unstored weight square into a quadratic product can make a row
cubic and its squared contribution sextic. Any such deletion must be measured
in the actual lifted system, not counted from its surface notation. The
selected reduction reuses an existing stored square and retains every
protected input, anchor and scale coordinate.

The identity U_A=[n]H belongs to the completeness construction. Imposing or
assuming it on arbitrary soundness witnesses would change the problem and
cannot justify deletion of a detector test. The selected proof instead
applies the arbitrary-order one-place lemma to the unchanged full test list,
then uses the stronger local conclusion only where the older consumer needs
its weaker consequence. No test function or alternate-depth condition is
dropped.

The accepted reduction is local predicate transfer plus a checked existing-
witness completeness construction. The broader geometric, modularity,
height, group, quaternion, pole, prime-pattern and Lucas dependencies retain
their stated conditional status.
