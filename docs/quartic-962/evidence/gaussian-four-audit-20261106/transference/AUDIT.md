## Findings and repairs

1. The relative inverse hypothesis is the full cyclic `U^(2s)` norm of the majorant minus one. For target `U^s`, the expanded majorant cube has `4^s` vertices, in `2s+1` variables. The baseline's complexity and exponent are correct. The majorant is not extended by zero off the interval. Green–Tao's construction instead equals one there. I made the fixed scaling of the interval function and of the inverse threshold explicit.
2. Green–Tao Proposition 7.1 requires literal absolute-value domination by its measure. The baseline referred to a common majorant but left the rescaling of all telescope factors implicit. The replacement uses a single fixed factor `alpha_0` for every factor, applies the theorem, and divides by `alpha_0^t`. Its dependence is on the fixed system and chosen complexity, not on the cutoff or residue class. This is a clarification of an available argument, not a new pseudorandomness claim.
3. The admissible-residue mass proof needs a finite exceptional set for single nonprimitive forms too. For example, the form `2x` has exceptional prime 2 even though a singleton system has no pair minors. The replacement excludes a chosen nonzero coefficient of each form as well as a chosen nonzero pair minor. For a singleton form the remaining local factors are exactly one. The asserted bounded mass is unchanged.
4. Tao–Ziegler's arbitrary-function model is bounded by a fixed constant depending on the sieve exponent, not necessarily by one. The replacement explicitly divides the model minus its conditional density by a fixed bound before Theorem 9. The patterns agent's more detailed `repair-dense-model.tex` is compatible and should also be integrated at the later proof site.
5. The boundary argument is valid, but its suggested translation of an ambient interval does not spell out the prime-majorant coordinates. The replacement gives an explicit positive-coordinate construction even for negative `c_j`. It uses a period `n_* = ceil(BN/W)`, discards low positive outputs at `o_v(NT)` cost, and applies the localization argument separately to actual outputs on the enlarged period and artificial wrapped outputs on the original period. No unsupported translated prime weight is needed.
6. The Dodos–Kanellopoulos theorem was checked in arXiv v4. The baseline bibliography already says the final typeset statement was not compared. The replacement repeats that qualification at the application. It must not be changed to a claim of publisher-PDF verification.

## Checked implications

The pointwise inequality is `|Lambda'_C - mu Lambda'| <= Lambda'`, because both the ray-prime indicator and `mu` lie in `[0,1]`. Restricting below `L^(3/5)` has vanishing interval Gowers norm: its `2^s`-th power is bounded by a fixed multiple of `(log L)^(2^s) L^(-2/5)`. The zero-extension denominator is a fixed positive constant for the chosen ambient-size ratio. The same truncation has vanishing correlation with bounded nilsequences.

The fixed conductor extension of the ordinary divisor-sum majorant is legitimate. Eventually `V_z = E product_(p<=z) p` with fixed `E`. Unit residues prevent all divisor terms containing a prime dividing `E`; the normalization `phi(V_z)/V_z` is unchanged by the extra prime powers. The remaining local factors and correlation estimates are those of the ordinary construction. The centered-cube expansion therefore gives full-cyclic majorant uniformity along a sufficiently slow cutoff.

The two cutoff limits are separated correctly. At auxiliary stage `j`, first make the limiting majorant error smaller than the inverse tolerances for the first `j` tests, then choose the size threshold for the finitely many fixed-modulus nilsequence estimates. For the linear count, use physical thresholds so every required fixed multiple of `N/V_z` exceeds that stage's threshold. This never applies a fixed-modulus estimate uniformly over a growing family without diagonalization.

The normal-form construction is an integer bijection after adjoining variables. For each ordered pair choose a direction in the kernel of the other form but outside the kernel of the distinguished form. Such directions exist because the nonzero linear parts are pairwise nonproportional. The new full box multiplies the original sum by exactly its number of lattice points. Its inverse image of a convex region remains convex and lies in a fixed multiple of the original-scale box. For one form a single direction with nonzero value suffices. The resulting generalized von Neumann errors are uniform in bounded normalized constants and convex regions.

The mass identity follows from CRT, including prime powers in the conductor: unit admissibility is a condition modulo each prime, so the extra lifts cancel after normalization. At good primes inclusion–exclusion and pairwise independence give `1+O(p^-2)`. Finitely many bad factors are bounded independently of moving constant terms. Thus the growing number of refined residue vectors does not amplify a uniform normalized error.

For the exact local cube the affine forms have coefficient vectors proportional to `(1,q omega,-q omega)`, with distinct `omega`. Their first coordinate prevents proportionality unless the vertices coincide. Each wrap pattern is a convex region and their number is bounded in terms of the fixed cube order. At frozen `v`, the `q` set is finite and `J/n` has a positive lower bound depending on `v`. Hence `o_v(n^(2a+1))` becomes `o_v(1)` after division by `n J^(2a)`.

Ordinary-prime correlations, with the fixed-coefficient theorem of Bienvenu, give the stated centered ordinary cube. At primes dividing `W` the normalizations cancel exactly. At primes not dividing `Wq`, independent gradients give `1+O_a(p^-2)` even with moving wrap constants. For primes greater than `v` dividing `q`, the bound is `1+O_a(p^-1)`; `log q=O(v)` gives at most `O(v/log v)` such factors, hence the stated `O(1/log v)` total error. Prime powers contribute only `o_v(1)` by linear-fiber counting and the square-root count of proper prime powers.

The actual transformed polynomials are quadratic, with coefficients `O(W)` and pairwise distinct leading coefficients. Theorem 13 therefore has `d=d_0=2`. Its local step polynomials have degree exactly one and coefficients bounded by a fixed power of the original `A`. Enlarging the model parameter to `calA=A^B0` with `B0>C+2` covers these coefficients. The global tests at `q<=calA^a` and `J=floor(calA^(-2a)M1^2)` lie in the proved local-cube window. The model uses the smaller cutoff exponent `rho/B0`, so the already truncated prime function is dominated by its permitted prime weight. Fixed model scaling introduces no new dependence on `v`.

For localization, Theorem 15 applies to `nu_0 F`, `nu_0 F^2`, and `nu_0`. A constant-shift factor occurs at most once. In the square, the two independent polynomial variables ensure every cross-copy difference is nonconstant, while distinct leading coefficients ensure the same within each copy. Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz gives a uniform error for every base-variable interval; the one-dimensional divisor-sum mean on an interval of fixed relative length gives its length as the main term. Fixed domination constants transfer this to prime products. The diagonal preserves every test `epsilon=1/k` for fixed `k`; it does not require the moving width `T^2/N` to be smaller than the current stage reciprocal.
