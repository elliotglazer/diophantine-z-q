## Exact scope and history

In the September 3, 2021 author version of Pan's *The Fontaine-Mazur conjecture in the residually reducible case*, Lemma 3.5.9 on p. 31 constructs a continuous equivariant map with dense image between two admissible Banach representations over a finite extension E of Q_p. It cites Proposition 3.1.3 of Emerton's March 23, 2011 draft to conclude surjectivity. Emerton's own Remark 3.1.5, p. 15, identifies Schneider and Teitelbaum's published results as supplying the case with coefficient algebra O. Thus this is a located earlier published route, not a newly discovered false inference or a claim of a new theorem.

The needed scalar-coefficient assertion is narrower than Emerton's proposition for a general complete local coefficient algebra A. The argument below does not prove that more general assertion. It does not establish all of Pan's modularity theorem, compare Pan's author text with the final journal text, or replace Pan's other inputs.

The published source is P. Schneider and J. Teitelbaum, *Banach space representations and Iwasawa theory*, Israel Journal of Mathematics 127 (2002), 359–380, DOI 10.1007/BF02784538. A journal-layout university-hosted PDF was retrieved and pinned. We use its Theorem 1.2, Theorem 2.3, Proposition 3.1, Lemma 3.4 and Theorem 3.5, together with the compact-module splitting fact explicitly used in the proof of Proposition 1.3(i), p. 364. The latter proof cites SGA 3, Expose VII B, 0.3.7. Our use has exactly the same domain of compact torsion-free, linearly topologized O-modules.

## The assertion required

Let E be a finite extension of Q_p, O its valuation ring, and varpi a uniformizer. Let H be a compact p-adic Lie group. If V and W are admissible E-Banach representations of H and u: V -> W is a continuous E-linear H-equivariant map with dense image, then u is surjective. It is a quotient map for the Banach topologies. No restriction on p is needed.

Here admissibility is the one in the published source, equivalently finite generation of the continuous dual over E[[H]] by Lemma 3.4. The group algebra means E tensor_O O[[H]], not a ring of arbitrary unbounded formal series.

## Derivation with the actual topologies

Put R = O[[H]]. This ring is left noetherian. The duality and admissibility results give finitely generated, O-torsion-free R-modules M and N with their canonical compact topologies and natural topological identifications

    V = M^d,  W = N^d,
    M^d = Hom_O,cont(M,E),  N^d = Hom_O,cont(N,E).

The norms are the sup norms on M and N. The transpose of u is an R[1/varpi]-linear map N[1/varpi] -> M[1/varpi]. After enlarging M inside M[1/varpi] by the R-module generated by the images of finitely many generators of N, it induces an R-linear map f: N -> M. This enlargement is still finitely generated and torsion-free, has the same localization, and gives the same Banach representation up to the natural isomorphism of Theorems 2.3 and 3.5. Proposition 3.1(iii) makes f continuous for the canonical topologies. Under these identifications u is f^d. Changing the lattice does not replace the original map by a different map.

The transpose is injective: a continuous functional on W that vanishes on u(V) vanishes on its closure W. Hence f is injective. Let A = f(N), a compact submodule of the Hausdorff module M. The map N -> A is a homeomorphism, so a continuous functional on N may be treated as a continuous functional on A.

Define the saturation

    T = M intersect A[1/varpi]  inside M[1/varpi].

Since R is noetherian, T is a finitely generated R-submodule of M and is closed by Proposition 3.1(ii). For each member t_i of a finite generating set of T choose r_i with varpi^(r_i) t_i in A. With r the maximum of these integers, centrality of varpi gives

    varpi^r T subset A subset T.

The quotient Q = M/T is O-torsion-free. Indeed, if varpi m belongs to T then m belongs to A[1/varpi], hence to T; iteration deals with every nonzero scalar in O. It is also compact, Hausdorff and linearly topologized. The splitting fact used in the published proof of Proposition 1.3(i), applied to the surjection M -> Q, therefore supplies a continuous O-linear retraction rho: M -> T. Equivariance of this retraction is neither claimed nor needed.

For any lambda in A^d define

    lambda_T(t) = varpi^(-r) lambda(varpi^r t),
    Lambda(m) = lambda_T(rho(m)).

These are continuous O-linear maps to E. On A the first formula equals lambda by O-linearity, so Lambda restricts to lambda. Thus restriction M^d -> A^d, and consequently f^d: M^d -> N^d, is onto. This proves surjectivity of the original u.

The extension just constructed is E-linear in lambda and satisfies

    ||Lambda||_M <= |varpi|^(-r) ||lambda||_A.

It is therefore a bounded E-linear right inverse of f^d. This also proves the quotient-topology assertion without assuming that an algebraic categorical image automatically has the correct topology. The right inverse need not be H-equivariant.

## Connecting this assertion to Pan's actual map

In Pan's notation take V = Pi_v raised to the finite direct-sum multiplicity d(p)' and W = Pi(p), the closure of the specified locally algebraic subspace in S_psi(U^p)_E. This p in d(p) and Pi(p) denotes a prime ideal, not the rational prime. Pan first permits a finite coefficient extension so that E(p) = E. Therefore the scalar field is still a finite extension of Q_p, as required here.

The map is the one induced by the universal unitary completion of the locally algebraic representation. Its restriction to the original locally algebraic subspace is the specified inclusion into W, so its image is dense in W by W's definition. The source paragraph establishes that Pi_v is admissible; finite direct sums preserve this property. The target admissibility is the assertion in Lemma 3.5.9. Restrict both representations to H = GL_2(O_{F_v}), a compact open p-adic Lie subgroup. The standard admissibility condition for the noncompact group restricts to the compact-group condition used above. The lemma proves surjectivity of the same underlying linear map. Its original GL_2(F_v)-equivariance is retained; the auxiliary non-equivariant section does not alter it.

This consumes, rather than independently reproves, the upstream admissibility and universal-completion assertions. The precise conditional interface is: once Pan's two asserted admissibility properties and its constructed dense continuous map are established, the disputed citation may be replaced by the published duality argument above. This is not acceptance of the surrounding Berger–Breuil, Paskunas or Breuil–Emerton inputs.

The immediate consumer is the precomposition injection

    Hom_cont,G(Pi(p), Pi_B) -> Hom_cont,G(V, Pi_B)

displayed just after Lemma 3.5.9, and the subsequent comparison of the two central actions. Surjectivity suffices for that injection, and no Hecke or central equivariance of the constructed right inverse is used. A continuous map with merely dense image already makes precomposition injective, but the stronger quotient assertion claimed by Pan is proved here as well.

## Adverse case and scope boundaries

Density alone does not imply surjectivity. On c_0(N,E), the bounded injective map (x_n) -> (varpi^n x_n) has dense proper image: it contains the finitely supported sequences, but the sequence (varpi^n) is not in the image because its putative preimage is the constant sequence 1. With trivial group action this infinite-dimensional representation is not admissible, since admissibility for the trivial group requires finite-dimensional dual. The finite-generation and saturation argument above excludes exactly this lack of a uniform bound.

We do not use the source's Proposition 1.3(i) as though it already asserted surjectivity of every dual injection. Its general conclusion involves closure. The uniform exponent and saturated splitting are the additional ingredients that give actual surjectivity here.

## Nine application comparisons

1. Source hypotheses: E is a finite p-adic field, H compact p-adic Lie, and the two Banach representations are admissible. The last two admissibility assertions are inherited from the specified Pan passage, not newly certified by this packet.
2. Constructed objects: V, W, u and their chosen compact dual lattices are identified above. W remains the actual closure in completed automorphic forms.
3. Domain: restriction to a compact open H supplies the domain of the published theorem. The statement is only for scalar coefficient field E, not arbitrary A.
4. Maps: u is the original completion map; f is its contravariant transpose; the dual map is u under natural identifications. Retractions extend functionals and do not define a replacement equivariant map.
5. Consumed object: the resulting quotient is Pi(p), and the consumer is its actual continuous Hom space, not a completion or a dense subspace substituted for it.
6. Coefficients and topology: O is a discrete valuation ring, E is its fraction field, compact dual modules carry canonical topologies, and Banach duals use continuous functionals and sup norms. No tensor completion or inversion of a Hecke factor occurs.
7. Local conditions: all representation-theoretic work is at the fixed v. Choosing H does not change the scalar field, Banach topology, map, or global representation used by Pan.
8. Exceptional cases: zero spaces and zero multiplicity give the same result because density then forces W = 0. No restriction to odd p is used. Infinite-dimensional inadmissible maps are explicitly excluded.
9. Downstream: the quotient and subsequent Hom injection in Lemma 3.5.9's paragraph are supplied. Whole local-global compatibility and modularity remain dependent on their other inputs.
