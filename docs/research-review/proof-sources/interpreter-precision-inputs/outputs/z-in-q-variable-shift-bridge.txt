A conditional exact-value bridge using a variable elliptic shift
3 November 2026, research iteration 35

Result and status

Under the three explicit hypotheses M, H and PC below, there is a
positive existential definition over Q of the graph

                 Val(iota(n),t)  <=>  t=n,  n in Z.         (1)

Consequently these hypotheses imply that Z is Diophantine in Q.
This is a new conditional construction, not a proof that the hypotheses
hold. In particular, the earlier arithmetic-model proof, the geometric
and theta inputs to H, and the restricted converse behind PC remain
unvalidated. The objective is not marked complete.

The shift in this construction is a quantified integer code. Its value
need not be extracted as a rational coordinate. Its size enters a
computable precision requirement on integer indices. This permits a
height argument that the earlier fixed-width bridge did not have.

1. Hypotheses and fixed notation

Let K=Q(sqrt(2)), with conjugation sigma, and

       d=75-53*sqrt(2),       E: y^2=x^3-d^2*x,
       P=((1641-1160*sqrt(2))/8,(-123720+87483*sqrt(2))/32).

M. P has infinite order. The canonical codes iota(n) of [n]P form
   a Diophantine set D over Q. Addition and multiplication of the
   indices are Diophantine on D, with exactly their intended meanings.
   Field coordinates and group operations on these codes are exposed
   by their usual finite algebraic formulas.

The earlier proposed M uses D=2E(K), but only the stated properties
are used here. DPRM and M imply that every computably enumerable
relation on a fixed finite tuple of integer indices is Diophantine
on the corresponding codes. This assertion concerns indices only.
It does not assume (1) or identify an index with a rational coordinate.

Put

       C={0,-15,-20,-24,-30,-60},
       F(T)=product_(c in C)(T-c),
       S={rational primes <=324}.

For t in Q outside C let M_S(t) be the squarefree product of primes
p outside S for which v_p(t-c)>0 is odd for some c in C.

H. A fixed positive integer A is available such that

                    h(t) <= A*M_S(t)^24                 (2)

   for every t in Q outside C. Here h(a/b)=log max(|a|,b)
   for coprime integers a,b with b>0.

The explicit earlier choice H6(S) is an integer and would suffice.
No effective determination of its enormous expanded decimal value is
needed by the mathematical argument. It is a fixed constant.

Let Phi(B) be the eleven-witness predicate Q_pole=0 in
explicit-pole-predicate.txt. Its elementary, unconditional implication
is

 Phi(B), B!=0 => v_p(B) is not negative and odd
                    for p outside {2,3,5,7,13}.          (3)

PC. Phi(B) holds whenever B has square reduced denominator,
    0<B<1/2, and v_p(B)>=4 at p=2,3,5,7,13.

Only completeness uses PC. Statement (3) follows directly from the
two displayed elliptic equations of Q_pole: an odd negative valuation
of B forces one curve with unit twist parameter to have an x-coordinate
of odd valuation, contrary to its square equation.

All uses of M, H and PC below are conditional. None is certified by
the finite checker accompanying this note.

2. A uniform precision relation, conditional on M

For nonzero kappa and n outside C define the positive integer

                     B(n,kappa)=A*|kappa*F(n)|^24.        (4)

There is a Diophantine relation Close(N,Kappa,t), which includes
the guards N,Kappa in D, with both properties

 Close(iota(n),iota(kappa),t) => |t-n| < exp(-B(n,kappa)),  (5)
 Close(iota(n),iota(kappa),n) holds.                      (6)

Here Close also requires kappa!=0 and n outside C. This follows
from the shrinking-interval construction in z-in-q-mixed-order-audit.txt,
with the precision made a computable function of both indices.
For clarity, the complete construction is as follows.

Write z(Q)=-x(Q)/y(Q) and z(O)=0. For l>=1 let
z([l]P)=u_l+v_l*sqrt(2) and epsilon_l=|u_l|+|v_l|>0.
For a>=1 and j in Z let

             r(a,j)=Tr_K/Q(z([j]P)/z([a]P))/2.

These are exactly computable rationals. The relation on integers

 n outside C, kappa!=0, a>=1, l>=1, j=n*a,
 |r(a,j)-n|<epsilon_l,  4*epsilon_l < 4^(-B(n,kappa))       (7)

is decidable, hence has a DPRM definition. Interpret it through M.
Expose r and epsilon from the point coordinates and require
|t-r|<epsilon. These field-coordinate operations, absolute values
and strict inequalities are Diophantine over Q. For example s>0
means s*(w1^2+w2^2+w3^2+w4^2)=1.

The compact group E(R) x E(R), at the two embeddings of K, supplies
positive multiples of P approaching O simultaneously. Thus epsilon_l
can be arbitrarily small. For fixed n, the derivative of [n] at O is
n, so r(a,n*a) tends to n along such positive multiples a. Choose l
first and a afterwards to satisfy (7). Taking t=n proves (6).

Conversely |t-n|<2*epsilon_l<4^(-B)/2<exp(-B), proving (5).
Every computation in (7) has ordinary integer indices as its inputs;
the external rational t is used only in the final strict inequality.
Thus this construction is not circular. Close alone is still an open
condition in t and does not define equality.

3. Six field-valued tests and one pole predicate

Take valid codes N=iota(n) and Kappa=iota(kappa), with kappa!=0.
Using M, require C1=iota(kappa*n). Quantify two more codes
H=iota(h) and U=iota(s), and form by the group law

                   V0=H-Kappa,       V1=U-C1.

Require H!=O and V0!=O. Thus h and h-kappa are nonzero integers.
For Q in D put w(Q)=1/x(Q) for Q!=O and w(O)=0. These conventions
are algebraic finite branches: the nonzero multiples of P are not
two-torsion, so their x and y coordinates are nonzero.

For a rational input t outside C define the following elements of K:

       D1=w(H),                 D2=w(U),
       D3=sigma(w(V0)),          D4=sigma(w(V1)),
       D5=z(U)/z(H)-t,
       D6=sigma(z(V1)/z(V0))-t.                           (8)

All denominators are nonzero by the guards and the infinite order
of P. Let c0=2730^3. Quantify eta,q in Q and impose

       eta^2=1,
       R0=eta*c0^2*F(t),
       a0=1-R0*q^2 !=0,           R=R0*a0^2,
       Cj=Dj/F(t),               j=1,...,6,
       W=product_(j=1)^6 (1+R*Cj^2) !=0,
       Theta=Tr_K/Q(1/W) !=0,
       Bpole=R/Theta^2,
       Phi(Bpole).                                      (9)

Call the resulting predicate Test(N,Kappa,t). Equalities of elements
of K mean two rational coefficient equalities in the basis 1,sqrt(2).
Each nonzero guard is existential using an inverse. Therefore (9),
including its point-code conditions, is Diophantine under M.

4. Soundness of the local tests

Suppose Test(N,Kappa,t) holds. Let p outside S divide M_S(t).
There is a unique c in C with delta=v_p(t-c)>0 odd. The differences
between distinct contacts are p-adic units. Thus v_p(F(t))=delta,
v_p(R0)=delta, and K/Q is unramified at p.

Put b=v_p(a0). If q=0, then b=0. Otherwise delta+2*v_p(q)
is odd and nonzero, so

             b=min(0,delta+2*v_p(q)) <=0,
             alpha=v_p(R)=delta+2*b is odd.              (10)

At every place v above p, normalized by v(p)=1, the valuation of
each factor of W is exactly

                 min(0,alpha+2*(v(Dj)-delta)).           (11)

The two summands of that factor have unequal valuations because
alpha is odd and v(Dj) is integral. This includes Dj=0, when the
factor has valuation zero. Hence v(W)<=0 at every such place.

Let lambda be the maximum of these valuations of W. Conjugation
either exchanges the two places or preserves the inert place.
It follows in both cases that

                  v_p(Theta) >= -lambda >=0.             (12)

Statement (3) and the odd valuation of Bpole imply
alpha-2*v_p(Theta)>=1. In particular alpha>0 and
lambda >= -(alpha-1)/2 > -alpha/2.

Choose a place where v(W)=lambda. Each factor valuation in (11)
is nonpositive, so each is at least lambda. Solving (11) for v(Dj)
gives, with the same chosen place for all six tests,

       v(Dj) >= delta-3*alpha/4
              = delta/4-3*b/2 >0.                        (13)

Valuations are integral, hence all six v(Dj)>=1. Infinite valuations
of zero entries are harmless. D1,D2 place H,U in the formal group
at v; D3,D4 place V0,V1 in the formal group at the conjugate place.
For this integral good Weierstrass model, v(1/x)>0 implies
v(1/x)=2*v(z) with v(z)>=1, or the point is O.

The normalized formal logarithm satisfies

                       log(Q)=z(Q)*(1+e_Q),  v(e_Q)>=1   (14)

on the nonzero formal points here. The invariant differential has
integral coefficients; its degree r integral contributes a denominator
at most r. For r>=2, (r-1)*v(z)-v_p(r)>=1 when p>2, proving (14).
The formal logarithm respects integer multiplication.

Since [s]H=[h]U, nonvanishing of log(H) in (14) gives

                    log(U)/log(H)=s/h.

D5>=1 says z(U)/z(H) is integral and congruent to t modulo p.
Equation (14) therefore implies s/h=t modulo p. This also holds
when U=O. At the conjugate place the identical argument gives

                    (s-kappa*n)/(h-kappa)=t modulo p.

Multiplying these two congruences by their integer denominators and
subtracting proves

                    v_p(kappa*(n-t))>=1.                 (15)

The expression in (15) is rational, so the chosen unramified place
does not change its p-adic valuation. Since v_p(t-c)>=1 and kappa
is an integer, p divides the ordinary integer kappa*(n-c).
As M_S(t) is squarefree, we have proved

                     M_S(t) divides kappa*F(n).          (16)

No assertion that kappa is a p-adic unit was used. This is the
purpose of retaining its size in (4). When n is outside C and
kappa!=0 the integer on the right of (16) is nonzero.

5. Global completeness of Test at every integer outside C

Fix n in Z outside C. Choose eta so that

                     R0=eta*c0^2*F(n)>0.

This is a nonzero integer. Let T be its finite set of prime divisors.
It contains 2,3,5,7,13, and v_p(R0)>=6 at each of those five primes.

Choose a positive integer kappa such that [kappa]P is sufficiently
close to O in E(K_v) at every place above T. Such multiples exist:
each prescribed sufficiently small formal subgroup is open, and
the compact local group modulo it is finite. A common multiple
of the finitely many quotient orders puts [kappa]P in every one.

Take H=[2*kappa]P, U=[2*kappa*n]P. Then
V0=[kappa]P and V1=[kappa*n]P. In the formal parameter,
w(Q) tends to zero and z([n]Q)/z(Q) tends to n as Q tends to O.
These facts hold also at the finitely many bad and ramified places,
after taking a sufficiently small formal neighborhood.

Consequently kappa can be chosen so that all six Cj=Dj/F(n) in (9)
are integral at every place over T. H and V0 remain nonzero because
P has infinite order and kappa>0. The choices here depend on n,
as permitted by existential quantification.

Now choose q in Q integral at every p in T, and arbitrarily close
in R to a root of 1-R0*q^2, but not equal to that root. Explicitly,
use denominators L_j=R0*j+1 and numerators approximating
L_j/sqrt(R0) by integers; adjust by one if equality occurs. The
error tends to zero and each denominator is a unit at T. At p in T,
a0 is a unit and v_p(R)=v_p(R0)=r>0. Thus

                     W=1 modulo p^r,
                     Theta=2 modulo p^r.                (17)

The second statement follows by trace, including at the ramified
prime 2: trace maps the local integer algebra into Z_p. Therefore
v_p(Bpole)=r for odd p in T, and v_2(Bpole)=r-2>=4.
At the other four specified small primes this valuation is >=6.

For p outside T, v_p(R0)=0, hence

              v_p(Bpole)=2*v_p(a0)-2*v_p(Theta)

is even. Thus Bpole has square reduced denominator. Both embeddings
of K are real. As q approaches the chosen real root, R tends to
zero through positive values, each embedded W is positive and
tends to one, and Theta tends to two. In particular every guard in
(9) holds and 0<Bpole<1/2 for a sufficiently close choice of q.

PC now supplies Phi(Bpole). We have proved that for every integer
n outside C there is a nonzero kappa for which Test(iota(n),
iota(kappa),n) holds. The same kappa can be used in Close because
(6) holds for every nonzero kappa, regardless of its size.

6. Exact equality and the single-polynomial conclusion

Define Val(N,t) by requiring N in D and the finite disjunction of:

 a. N=iota(c) and t=c, for each c in C;
 b. there exists Kappa in D with
             Close(N,Kappa,t) and Test(N,Kappa,t).

For branch b, write N=iota(n), Kappa=iota(kappa). Its guards ensure
n outside C, kappa!=0 and t outside C. By (2) and (16),

                         h(t)<=B(n,kappa).                (18)

If t=a/b in lowest terms, b>0, and t!=n, then |a-n*b|>=1, so

               |t-n|>=1/b>=exp(-h(t))>=exp(-B(n,kappa)).  (19)

This contradicts Close. Hence branch b implies t=n. Conversely,
Section 5 and (6) prove branch b for every n outside C with t=n.
Branch a handles exactly the six excluded integer values. This
proves (1) under M, H and PC.

Every formula used is positive existential over Q after translating
the fixed integer relation (7) through M and DPRM. The code constants
iota(c) are fixed rational tuples. Replace K-valued operations by
two-coordinate rational equations, inequalities by square formulas,
and nonzero guards by inverses. Clear fixed coefficient denominators.
Conjunctions combine by sums of squares and finite disjunctions by
products, with separate witness blocks. Thus a single polynomial
over Z defines Val, and existentially quantifying N defines Z.

The proof establishes the existence and a finite compilation recipe
conditional on M, H and PC. No expanded polynomial or variable count
is claimed here; in particular the DPRM polynomial for (7) has not
been materialized.

7. Dependency and completion audit

The conditional bridge does not use a rational formula that extracts
an elliptic index. It uses additional rational witnesses for Phi.
Nor does it rely on finitely many approximation tests alone: the
global pole predicate supplies (16) at all relevant primes. The
old obstructions for those narrower constructions do not apply.

Dependencies of the new implication are:

 M: an exact Diophantine arithmetic model, still to be validated;
 H: the global six-contact estimate, with its geometry and theta
    comparison still to be validated;
 PC: the sufficient pole-completeness statement, whose restricted
     pointwise-converse dependency is still to be validated;
 elementary pole soundness, formal elliptic arithmetic, compactness,
 elementary rational approximation, DPRM, and rational height arithmetic.

The earlier proposed construction of M itself uses H and PC, plus
its generator, prime-pattern and compiler arguments. This is not a
circular use of the new bridge: none of those inputs is permitted
to assume Val or that the actual integers are Diophantine in Q.
