# How Version 17 uses its sources: an inventory

Done 2026-11-14 on `manuscripts/quartic_v17.tex` (commit `8799c581`; Version 18 began as an exact copy). All 154 bibliography entries were classified by how the manuscript relies on them, and Part I (lines 100–4600), which names sources in prose, was read separately. Seven reader agents did the reading; Claude Fable 5.2 assembled this file and read the Tao–Ziegler and (H4) passages directly. Line numbers refer to `quartic_v17.tex`.

**No external source was opened.** Every classification is from the manuscript's own wording. "Printed statement" means the manuscript cites a numbered result and shows no sign of going beyond it; it does not mean the hypotheses were checked against the source.

## 1. Where the proof rests on the project's own version of deep published work

**1a. The inverse theorem for Gowers norms (Green–Tao–Ziegler). Lines 28931–35200, about 6,000 lines.**
- The manuscript does not use the printed inverse theorem ("No use of Theorem 1.3 is made", 32535). It proves its own form, following the published architecture.
- Taken as printed: conventions, Appendix B, parts of Appendix E, Lemma 9.12.
- Replaced by local arguments: the degree-2 base case, Lemma 13.2, Proposition 7.3, Appendix D.4, D.5, F.1, Sections 9–13, Appendix C, the converse, and the extraction of a finite family.
- Called wrong in the source: Proposition 8.3 ("false", 30418), Appendix C ("defective", 29145), D.2 (with a counterexample, 30740), D.4, D.5, Definition 9.1, Lemma 12.1, Lemmas 13.3–13.4, and the hypothesis of Theorem 7.4 (34565). Eleven bibliography entries fall in category E for this reason.
- It relies on a six-page unpublished author erratum for the identification of problems; it says no correction is imported from it, and that one repair is "suggested, but not fully proved" there (30032). The bibliography gives no date or URL.
- The manuscript's own assembly is described as "conditional on the listed intermediate interfaces and base" (29483), and seven internal supplements in the bibliography are marked "awaiting independent acceptance".
- **Open question the manuscript does not address:** whether the printed Theorem 1.3 would suffice for the consumer (Green–Tao, Proposition 10.1, at 24573–24592). The stated need is a fixed finite family and linear nilsequences (28987). The manuscript never compares that with the printed statement.

**1b. Green–Tao, "Linear equations in primes".**
- The converse (their Proposition 11.2) is re-proved locally, 34991–35182, because the printed proof's step from (11.6) to (11.7) "widens the shift domain" (35171). The manuscript says this is not a counterexample and that it has not established whether a correction exists.
- The prime majorant with a fixed factor in the modulus is rebuilt in about 145 lines (24597–24741), arguing from inside the proof of their Theorem D.3. This is the same situation as Tao–Ziegler: a published result for one modulus, needed for another.

**1c. Tao–Ziegler, "Polynomial patterns in the primes"** (2018, 25159–25179). Used "through an adaptation of their proofs", justified in nine lines and a pointer to a review file. This is the one case where the step was asserted without an argument. Version 18 (PR 98) replaces it with a reduction to the printed theorems.

## 2. Statements stretched, or facts taken from inside proofs

These are argued in the text. Each needs someone to open the source.

- **Kato 2004 at p = 2** (7880, 18879–18968): Theorem 13.4(2) is applied at 2 through Kato 1999, Theorem 0.8, with a transfer between "the two published cohomology conventions" and an enlarged level. A "retained-smoothed version of Theorem 6.6" is used (18541).
- **Waldspurger** (19701–19751): hypothesis (H2) is dropped because "the final paragraph of that proof removes" it, and a linear relation is extended to the whole eigenspace from the proof on p. 483.
- **Carayol 1987** (2580, 13517–13527): no single printed statement; the conductor claim is assembled from a theorem and a remark, then dualized and twisted. **Carayol** (other key, 21478–21497): applied to the contragredient.
- **Casselman** (21502–21527): the conductor calculation inside the proof of the newvector theorem, with a change of convention.
- **Nekovář** (9516): "Its finite-module proof applies at two."
- **Deligne 1971** (6087–6179): conventions inverted in two places; a level subgroup is shrunk and the conclusion is kept by an argument from the proof.
- **Gaudron–Rémond 2023** (3176, 35751): "the construction in the published proof of Lemma 16.2(1)"; depends in turn on Rémond 2022, whose final text was not obtained.
- **Lin–Yang** (5849–5880): divisor calculations taken with unknown constants, which the manuscript then fixes itself.
- **Milne, Arithmetic Duality Theorems** (6515, 10108): the proofs of II.2.1 and I.7.3.
- **Koenigsmann** (15874): facts (a), (b), (c), (e) in the proof of Proposition 6.
- **Javanpeykar** (2679), **Stacks 0BUL and 0BWJ** (2691, 2828), **Silverman IV.6.4** (14793): a fact from a proof.
- **Matiyasevich** (12600): a change of convention; feeds only the universal-polynomial corollary.

## 3. Sources the manuscript says are wrong

Apart from the inverse-theorem sources in 1a:

- **Baba–Granath** (5823–5830): the printed form is six times the principal one; the corrected form is used.
- **Milne, Shimura varieties notes** (12955): Proposition 8.14 and Theorem 8.17 "require the author's correction"; Theorem 7.4 is used instead.
- **Green–Tao 2008** (29531, 29778): a "false quadratic-extension assertion" and a misprint; relies on an unpublished author notice with no bibliography entry.
- **Silverman, Advanced Topics** (35240): cited "with the corrected formulation"; the manuscript does not say what was corrected.

Every item here and in 1a is a claim by this project that published mathematics is wrong. None has been confirmed by anyone outside the project.

## 4. Steps justified by something outside the paper

Part I has **57** places where the justification is an internal file, a review record, a private commit, or an earlier manuscript version. Some of the arguments may be printed later in Part II; that was not checked. The most consequential:

1. 2258 (and 2162): a height bound whose supporting chain "remains a separately reviewed interface".
2. 2226 and 3309: the Kato, inverse-theorem and Gaussian-prime results carried forward because the Version 15 source is byte-identical; the author "did not reconstruct every internal RC/analytic sublemma".
3. 2022: the Tao–Ziegler adaptation (fixed in PR 98).
4. 2324, 2336, 2460: cover, field and Hom facts from a Version 14 proof file.
5. 3039–3049: support and conductor facts "inherited from" two private proof files; a lemma cited as passages of the Version 14 source.

Once the review records are archived, these pointers lead nowhere.

## 5. Sources whose final text was not obtained

Pan (journal returned 403; arXiv v2 read; the live input is Calegari's survey statement, p. 624), Rémond 2022 (author version only), Washington (replaced by a derivation from Milne), Serre's *Local Fields* (no fresh copy; one formula re-derived to avoid it), Faltings 1983 (the 1986 edition was read), Frei–Tinková (preprint; method rebuilt, no theorem assumed).

## 6. Bibliography housekeeping

- 27 entries are never cited. Twelve are internal supplements, several marked as awaiting review.
- About 25 sources used in Part I have no entry, including Rémond 2017, Bost, Lenstra, Milne's course notes, a Ribet note, and fifteen Stacks tags. Three are not even named (2310, 2396, 3338).
- Duplicated keys: von Känel, Gaudron–Rémond, Voight, and the seven inverse-theorem keys.
- Gross–Zagier is cited with pages 198–199, but the listed paper runs 225–320 (35248).
- Howard's entry mixes a 2004 journal reference with a 2012 arXiv number.
- Many locators are a section, a page, or absent.

## Limits

- No source was opened, so category A is "no visible stretch", not "verified".
- Entries are counted by strongest use; an entry in D or E may also be used as a printed statement elsewhere.
- The per-slice counts reported by the readers sum to 153 of 154; one entry in slice 3 was not assigned a primary category.
- Part II was read only at the places where a source is cited or named.
