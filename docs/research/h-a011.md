## 1. The statement used

### 1.1 Pan, Theorem 1.0.4 (arXiv:1901.07166v2), exactly (STANDARD for v2)

> "ρ : Gal(Q̄/Q) → GL₂(Q̄_p) be a continuous, irreducible representation such that
> - ρ is only ramified at finitely many places,
> - the restriction of ρ at the decomposition group at p is potentially semi-stable in the sense

> "Let p be an odd prime and ρ be as in conjecture 1.0.1. Assume that
> - ρ|Gal(Q̄_p/Q_p) has distinct Hodge-Tate weights,
> - If p = 3, the semi-simplification of ρ̄|Gal(Q̄_p/Q_p) is not of the form η ⊕ ηω for some

### 3.1 H1, continuity (VERIFIED as an argument)

On the open subgroup `G_F`, `ρ₅` is the Tate action tensored with `E`, which is continuous (L512-
513, L519-520). `G_ℚ` is a finite union of cosets `σG_F`, and on each coset `ρ₅(στ) = ρ₅(σ)ρ₅(τ)`.
So `ρ₅` is continuous, and so is its matrix-unit component `r₅ = e₁₁ρ₅e₁₁`. The values lie in
`GL₂(E)` with `E ⊂ Q̄₅`, which is Pan's setting.

### 3.2 H2, irreducibility

V14 L521-527 (VERIFIED as an argument):

- Faltings' semisimplicity and endomorphism theorem give that `V₅(A) ⊗ E` is a semisimple
  `G_F`-module with commutant `End_F(A) ⊗ E = B ⊗ E = M₂(E)`. Here `End⁰_F(A) = B` because `F`
  defines all geometric endomorphisms and `End⁰(A_Q̄) = B` is a hypothesis of the lemma.
- Writing `V₅(A) ⊗ E = r ⊗_E E²` with `r = r₅|G_F`, the commutant is `M₂(End_{G_F}(r))`. So
  `End_{G_F}(r) = E`, after every finite extension of `E` as well. With semisimplicity this makes
  `r` absolutely irreducible.
- Hence `r₅` is irreducible over `Q̄₅`, which is Pan's "irreducible" for a `GL₂(Q̄_p)`-valued `ρ`.

Source: Faltings, *Complements to Mordell*, in *Rational Points* (2nd ed., Vieweg 1986), Theorem
1(a)-(b), p. 204, as V14 L523 cites it (UNCHECKED). The same input is h-leaves row A008. Its
non-circularity against GR14 is h-leaves open check 1, which is unchanged.

### 3.3 H3, finite ramification (VERIFIED as an argument)

- Let `S₀` be a finite set of primes containing 5, the primes ramified in `F`, and the primes
  below the places of bad reduction of `A_F`. It is finite: `A_F` extends to an abelian scheme
  over `O_F[1/N]` for some `N`. This spreading-out is standard and uncited in Version 14.
- For `q ∉ S₀`, every inertia group `I_q ⊂ G_ℚ` lies in `G_F`, because `F/ℚ` is Galois and
  unramified at `q`. On `G_F`, `ρ₅` is the Tate action, which is unramified at places of good
  reduction prime to 5. That is the easy direction of Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich, Serre–Tate, Ann.
  of Math. 88 (1968), Theorem 1, p. 493, which Version 14 cites for the same purpose at L1212
  (UNCHECKED).
- So `r₅` is unramified outside `S₀`.

### 3.4 H4, potential semistability at 5

**What is required (VERIFIED).** Pan requires that "the restriction of `ρ` at the decomposition
group at `p` is potentially semi-stable in the sense of Fontaine". That is, there is a finite
extension `K'/ℚ₅` with `ρ|G_{K'}` semistable (`B_st`-admissible) as a `ℚ₅`-linear representation.
Berger04 recalls the same definition (arXiv:math/0210184, TeX L1258: "a potentially semi-stable
representation `V` of `G_K`, so that" its restriction to some `G_L`, `L/K` finite, is
semi-stable). Fix the place `w` of `F` determined by the fixed embedding `Q̄ ↪ Q̄₅`. Then
`G_{F_w} = G_{ℚ₅} ∩ G_F` is open in the decomposition group `G_{ℚ₅}`. Since all decomposition
groups at 5 are conjugate, nothing depends on the choice.

**The summand lemma** (VERIFIED as an argument). Let `𝐁` be `B_cris` or `B_st` and `K` a finite
extension of `ℚ_p`. For a finite-dimensional `ℚ_p`-representation `V` of `G_K`, put
`D(V) = (𝐁 ⊗_{ℚ_p} V)^{G_K}`. Fontaine calls `V` admissible when `dim_{𝐁^{G_K}} D(V) = dim V`,
or equivalently when the natural map `α_V : 𝐁 ⊗ D(V) → 𝐁 ⊗ V` is an isomorphism. Both `D` and
`α` are additive: `D(V ⊕ W) = D(V) ⊕ D(W)` and `α_{V⊕W} = α_V ⊕ α_W`. Hence, for every `n ≥ 1`:
- `Vⁿ` is admissible iff `V` is, under either definition, because `dim D(Vⁿ) = n·dim D(V)` and
  `α_{Vⁿ} = α_Vⁿ`;
- `r ⊕ r` admissible ⇒ `r` admissible, because `dim D(r) = ½ dim D(r ⊕ r) = dim r`.

No regularity inequality is needed, since the summands are identical. Applied with
`K' ⊇ F_w`: as `ℚ₅[G_{K'}]`-modules,
`V₅(A)^{[E:ℚ₅]} ≅ V₅(A) ⊗_{ℚ₅} E ≅ r₅|G_{K'} ⊕ r₅|G_{K'}` (L515-517). So `V₅(A)|G_{K'}`
semistable (resp. crystalline) ⇒ `r₅|G_{K'}` semistable (resp. crystalline).

**Breuil's corollary (STANDARD; read in the published text).** Breuil, Ann. of Math. 152 (2000),
489-549, Corollaire 5.3.4, p. 547 (also announced as Corollaire 1.6, p. 493):

> "Supposons p ≠ 2 et k ⊆ F̄_p. Soient A une variété abélienne définie sur K et V_p(A) son module
> de Tate tensorisé par Q_p. Alors A a réduction semi-stable (resp. bonne réduction) sur O_K si et
> seulement si V_p(A) est une représentation semi-stable (resp. cristalline) de Gal(K̄/K)."

Its setting (p. 490): `k` is a perfect field of characteristic `p`, `K₀ = W(k)[1/p]`, and `K` is
a finite totally ramified extension of `K₀`. Every finite extension of `ℚ_p` is of this form with
`k` its residue field. The text extraction loses the bar over `F_p`, but the proof of Lemme 5.2.1
treats "Si `k` est fini" and then "Si `k ⊂ F̄_p` n'est pas fini". So finite residue fields are
included (VERIFIED). At `p = 5`, both hypotheses hold.

*Caveat (VERIFIED wording).* The direction this record uses, reduction ⇒ representation, is not
proved in Breuil's paper. The proof says these implications "sont classiques et bien connues (et
dues à Fontaine)" and proves only the converse. The statement is still a numbered statement of a
refereed paper, which is what the success criterion asks for. The primary source for "good
reduction ⇒ crystalline" is Fontaine's Barsotti–Tate paper (Ann. of Math. 115 (1982)), cited as
`[Fo-BT]` by Coleman–Iovita (arXiv:math/9701229, p. 3). That attribution is VERIFIED; the source
is UNCHECKED. Two further published statements agree:

- Bhatt–Morrow–Scholze, Theorem 1.1(i) (arXiv v3; Publ. Math. IHÉS 128 (2018), numbering
  UNCHECKED): for a proper smooth formal scheme over `O_K`, "`H^i_et(𝔛_C, ℚ_p)` is a crystalline
  Galois representation". Its `𝔛_C` is the rigid generic fibre.
- Berger04 §I.3 (TeX L1220-1224): "Coleman-Iovita and Breuil showed that `A` has good reduction
  if and only if `V` is crystalline, and that `A` has semi-stable reduction if and only if `V` is
  semi-stable" (survey; VERIFIED wording).

### 3.5 H5, oddness (V14 L549-559; argument VERIFIED)

- Fix an embedding `F ↪ ℂ` and a complex conjugation `c`. On `H₁(A(ℂ), ℂ) = H^{−1,0} ⊕ H^{0,−1}`,
  the composite `t_cμ_c∘c` is antiholomorphic: `c` exchanges the two summands, and the
  holomorphic `μ_c` preserves them. So the composite is block off-diagonal and has trace 0.
- The trace transfers to `V₅(A) ⊗ E` through the comparison of `A[5ⁿ]` with `H₁(A(ℂ),ℤ)/5ⁿ`,
  compatibly with conjugation (Milne, *Shimura varieties and moduli*, Clay Math. Proc. 4 (2005),
  Thm 6.8 and the preceding paragraph, p. 319; UNCHECKED).
- So `2 tr r₅(c) = tr ρ₅(c) = 0`. Since `ρ₅` is a homomorphism, `r₅(c)² = 1`, and the
  eigenvalues are `1, −1`: `det r₅(c) = −1`.
- Any other complex conjugation is `G_ℚ`-conjugate to `c`, and `det` is a class function. This is
  Pan's oddness.

### 3.6 H6, distinct Hodge–Tate weights

**Primary discharge, V14 L532-547 (VERIFIED as present).** By Scholze's comparison,
`H¹_et(A, ℚ₅)` is de Rham at a place above 5 (Scholze, Forum Math. Pi 1 (2013), Cor. 1.8 and
Thms 9.1, 9.3, with the corrigendum). Its dual is `V₅(A)` (Milne 1986, Thm 15.1(a)), with weights
`0, 0, −1, −1`. De Rham representations are closed under duals and subquotients (Fontaine 1994,
Thm 3.8), so the two identical summands each have weights `0, −1`. The text then descends the
filtered space to `ℚ₅` (L542-547).

Since `V₅(A)` is a `ℚ₅`-representation, its weights are the same at every embedding
`τ : E → Q̄₅`, so `r₅` has weights `{0, −1}` at every `τ` (VERIFIED as an argument). Status of the
sources: Scholze's arXiv statements VERIFIED (h-leaves, h-v14); the journal numbering, the
corrigendum, Milne 1986 and Fontaine 1994 UNCHECKED.

**Independent check that the weights are distinct** (VERIFIED as an argument; it uses neither
Scholze nor the Hodge numbers of `A`).

- `r₅|G_{ℚ₅}` is Hodge–Tate with integer weights `a, b` at each `τ`, because it is potentially
  semistable (§3.4) and pst ⇒ de Rham ⇒ Hodge–Tate. (These inclusions are standard; Berger04,
  TeX L389: "Each category of representations is a subcategory of the one above it", for the
  chain crystalline ⊂ semistable ⊂ pst ⊂ de Rham ⊂ Hodge–Tate.)
- On `G_F`, `(det r₅)² = det_E(V₅(A) ⊗ E) = ε₅²`, because the Weil pairing gives
  `det V₅(A) = ε₅²`. Version 14 uses this at L634-636. So `det r₅ = α·ε₅` with `α² = 1` on `G_F`.
- The weight of `det` is `a + b`. A finite-order character has weight 0, and `ε₅` has weight
  `−1` (Pan's convention). So `a + b = −1`, which is odd, and `a ≠ b`.

This check shows that Pan's distinctness hypothesis does not rest on the Hodge numbers. The exact
values `{0, −1}` are still needed, for the weight-two conclusion (§4), and they come from V14
L532-547.
