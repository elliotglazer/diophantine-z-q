## Main corrections

1. **R2 confuses orbifold orders with finite-cover ramification.** The orders `(4,6,2)` belong to the quotient uniformization. The finite degree-four Atkin–Lehner quotient has ramification `(2,2,2)` at the three distinguished values. A degree-four cover cannot have ramification index six.
2. **R5 omits local orientation factors.** González–Rotger Proposition 5.6 gives `#CM(R) = 2^#{p | D(R)N(R)} h(R)`, not generally `h(R)`. The maximal order of discriminant `-19` has `h(R)=1` but gives four CM points for discriminant-six Shimura data. A single specified orientation branch has `h(R)` points; the whole CM locus does not.
3. **R3's claimed arXiv-v1 Example 30 locator is wrong.** Discriminant six is Example 29; Example 30 treats discriminant fourteen. The journal numbering remains unread. Baba–Granath Proposition 3.6 is a separately checked alternative source for the invariant tuple, not verification of the Lin–Yang citation.
4. **TYZ was renumbered in the journal.** The preprint's Theorem 1.2 is journal Theorem 1.4. Journal Theorem 1.2 is a different Smith rank-zero/BSD result. The journal retains the disputed all-factors-greater-than-one restriction. The convolution used by Proposition N is printed as Lemma 2.9 in the opened v1 and journal texts; Lemma 2.8 is the symmetry lemma.
5. **Pazuki R22 is a consequence, not a literal corollary.** Its metric is Deligne's. Relative to the original Faltings convention used in the transfer estimates, `h_D = h_F + (g/2) log(pi)`, hence the surface shift is **+log(pi)**. The auxiliary bound must dominate `max(1,h_D)`, not just an unshifted `h_F`.
6. **Carayol does cover ramified primes and coefficient characteristic two.** The primary theorem quantifies over finite places and coefficient places of different residue characteristics. For `F=Q`, its extra even-degree totally-real-field condition does not apply.
7. **Pan's arXiv-v2 theorem was read; the JAMS numbering is still unverified.** At `p=3`, the precise local residual exception must be retained. Frobenius and dualization conventions also matter.
8. **Ribet's historical refereeing remains unverified.** The preface and reprint footnote establish publication/reprinting, not peer review. A published Kisin alternative gives a quotient of `J1(N)^m`; the single-`J1(N)` consequence needs the route's Q-simple `B0`.

These findings concern leaf statements, locators, and applications. They do not by themselves refute the main theorem. They do prevent describing this entire literal leaf list as already verified.

## Detailed normalization and application notes

### 1. CM spaces, orientation and NoCM

Use the same moduli space throughout a source application. González–Rotger's count/field statements are about `X0(D,N)`, not an unspecified level-three cover or its full Atkin–Lehner image.

For the maximal order of discriminant -19, f=1 and h=1. Both two and three are inert, so the local factor is four, giving four CM points rather than one. This meets `(f,6)=1`; coprimality therefore does not fix R5. If the NoCM argument uses the unqualified h(R) count, it needs a specified orientation branch or an actual quotient/fiber counting argument.

Likewise, at discriminant -24 the full-quotient coordinate j=-16/27 is rational, whereas its ring class field has degree four over Q. The quotient-image field need not have index at most two in that ring class field. This does not contradict the primary field theorem on its own Shimura curve.

### 2. Theta and height conventions

The invariant tuple in R3 is weighted-projective. Ordinary projective scaling cannot be substituted without adjusting weights. The exact Igusa–Clebsch/J conversion and theta normalization must precede certification of the constant 3779136. The prior 40-digit truncated numerical theta calibration is not an exact proof of the normalization; this audit does not promote it to one.

Pazuki's Corollary1.3(2) uses

- `H_Theta = max(1,h_Theta(A,L))`;
- `H_D = max(1,h_D(A))`;
- `|H_Theta - H_D/2| <= C2(g,r) log(min(H_Theta,H_D)+2)`;
- `C2(g,r)=1000*r^(2g)*(log(r^(2g)))^5`.

At g=r=2 this is exactly the requested `1000*2^4*(log(2^4))^5`. Dropping the absolute value, bounding the minimum by H_D, then using H_D<=X_D gives the leaf's upper bound. These are consequence steps, not the literal corollary. The comparison-to-truncated-height passage uses Lemma5.7.

The squared Deligne metric is `i^(g^2)/(2*pi)^g` times the top-form integral. Relative to the original `2^-g` convention, `h_D=h_F+(g/2)log(pi)`. For surfaces and a bound `h_F<=X`, take `X_D=max(1,X+log(pi))`. The theta height is the paper's archimedean l2/Fubini–Study height of the theta embedding; identify it with the project's chosen coordinate/Weil-height bound, rather than leaving it unspecified.

For Bost, the following remains a **conditional** calculation, not verification of an unread display: if the primary Deligne-normalized lower bound is `h_D >= -(g/2)log(2*pi)`, conversion gives `h_F >= -(g/2)log(2*pi^2) > -(3/2)g`. The original lower bound and its metric must still be checked. Equal-dimension shifts cancel in R20 but do not prove that lemma.

### 4. TYZ's printed restrictions and the counterexamples

Both the opened v1 and journal texts retain nonordered decompositions with all factors greater than one. The journal renumbers the introductory theorem but does not remove that restriction. The proofs retain the d1=1 contribution producing Z(n); the recursion separately subtracts d1>1 terms. Those are different uses. Intermediate ordered triple sums do not justify counting permutations of the final class-one blocks.

In both alternatives, d0 is the exceptional block: `(d0 mod 8, d1 mod 8)` is `(5,1)` or `(7,3)`.
