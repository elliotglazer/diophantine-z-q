## Verdict

**Correct after the listed repairs.** Theorem NoCM (note §4.1.4) is true: for every
`u ∈ ℚ \ 𝒞`, no point of `X⁶` over `t(u)` is a CM point. So the fibre surface `A` in the proof of
Theorem 4.1 always has `End⁰(A_ℚ̄) = B`, and the paragraph "CM fibers" (lines 581-631) can be
replaced by a short lemma.

- **What was checked.** Every mathematical step of the note was checked. The coordinate
  identities, Elkies' model with its twist constants, the reciprocity and counting inputs, the case
  split and the interface with the proof of Theorem 4.1 are all correct.
- **The repairs.** All of them are about citations or wording; none changes the argument (§7).
  1. The two sources the note cites for the CM inputs each have a misstatement that must not be
     copied. GR's non-emptiness criterion in Prop. 5.6 is wrong when `(f, 6) > 1`. Saia's
     restatement of the Jordan/GR field-of-moduli theorem has its two cases swapped. The note uses
     neither statement.
  2. Shimura's reciprocity law is attributed by GR to Main Theorem II of Shimura 1967, not Main
     Theorem I.
  3. Elkies determines the twist constants in §3.2 of the arXiv text, not §3.3.
  4. The nonvanishing of `H(u)` is not at manuscript line 541.
  5. The line range to be replaced overlaps the range to be kept.
- **A strengthening.** The twist constants `c₀ = -1`, `c₁ = 3` of Elkies' model follow from Shimura
  reciprocity at the three elliptic points alone (§2.3). So Elkies' computation becomes a
  cross-check rather than an input.
- **A shorter proof.** GR Thm. 5.12 gives a two-case proof of NoCM that needs neither the
  ramification of ring class fields nor the Case C argument (§7.2).
- **Numerical constants.** `H₆(S;R)` is unchanged. `SixContactEstimate` (`Hypotheses/Height.lean`)
  and `SixContactMin` (`Hypotheses/HeightMin.lean` on `main`) need no change.

### 1.5 Nonvanishing (VERIFIED)

For `u ∈ ℚ \ 𝒞` we need `F(u)G(u)H(u) ≠ 0`.

- `disc G = -10800`, so `G(u) ≠ 0` (line 541).

also checked numerically on five random rational `u`.

## 2. Elkies' model of X⁶ → X*(6)

### 2.1 The shape of the model (VERIFIED)

The Atkin–Lehner group `W ≅ (ℤ/2)²` acts on the canonical model of `X⁶` over `ℚ` (STANDARD: GR §5,
"as a natural subgroup of the group of automorphisms of `X₀(D,N)` over `ℚ`").

So `ℚ(X⁶)/ℚ(t)` is a `W`-Galois biquadratic extension with constant field `ℚ`. It is branched only
over `t = 0, 1, ∞`, with ramification index 2 and stabilizers `⟨w₆⟩, ⟨w₂⟩, ⟨w₃⟩` (§1.3).

**The intermediate fields.** The fixed field of `w_m` corresponds to `X/⟨w_m⟩`. This curve is
branched over `X*` exactly at the two elliptic values whose stabilizer is not `⟨w_m⟩`. A quadratic
extension of `ℚ(t)` branched exactly at two of `0, 1, ∞` is generated by `√(c·t)`, `√(c(t-1))` or
`√(c·t(t-1))`, according to the branch pair. Therefore:

- `ℚ(X⁶) = ℚ(t)(√(c₀t), √(c₁(t-1)))` for some `c₀, c₁ ∈ ℚ^×/ℚ^{×2}`.
- With `y₁ = √(c₀t)` and `y₂ = √(c₁(t-1))`:
  - `w₂` fixes `y₁` and negates `y₂`;
  - `w₆` negates `y₁` and fixes `y₂`;
  - `w₃` negates both.

This agrees with the note §4.1.2 and with Elkies §3.1, who states the same shape.

### 2.2 The constants in Elkies (VERIFIED against the TeX)

Elkies §3.1 announces `c₀ = -1`, `c₁ = 3` and the conic `X² + Y² + 3Z² = 0`. He remarks that the
conic alone does not determine `c₀, c₁`.

He determines them in the subsection on `X₀*(l)`, `l = 5, 7, 13`. That is §3.2 of the arXiv text;
§3.3 is about supersingular points (repair R3). The method is to require that the genus-one curves
`𝒳₀(l)` have Jacobians of conductor `6l`. The adjoined square roots are:

- `3t(1-t)` for `l = 5`;
- `-t` for `l = 7`;
- `3(t-1)` for `l = 13`.

These are consistent with `c₀ = -1`, `c₁ = 3`.

### 2.3 An independent derivation of c₀ = -1, c₁ = 3 (VERIFIED, `check.py` part B)

The constants follow from Shimura reciprocity at the three elliptic points. Elkies' table is not
used.

- **Over `t = 1`.** The two points are `y₂ = 0`, `y₁ = ±√c₀`. They are the `ℤ[i]`-CM points, and
  `h(-4) = 1`. By (S) below, `ℚ(x) ⊆ ℚ(i)`. So `c₀ ∈ {1, -1}` modulo squares.
- **Over `t = ∞`.** `(y₂/y₁)² = c₁(t-1)/(c₀t) → c₁/c₀`. These are the `ℤ[ζ₃]`-CM points, and
  `h(-3) = 1`. So `ℚ(√(c₁/c₀)) ⊆ ℚ(√-3)`.
- **Over `t = 0`.** The points are `y₁ = 0`, `y₂ = ±√(-c₁)`. They are the `ℤ[√-6]`-CM points.
  By (S), `K·ℚ(x) = H₋₂₄ = ℚ(√-6, √2)`, which has degree 4. So `-c₁ ∈ {2, -3}` modulo squares.

Among `c₀, c₁ ∈ {±1, ±2, ±3, ±6}`, the only solution is `(c₀, c₁) = (-1, 3)`. Constants with
other prime factors are excluded by the same conditions, since every residue field above has to
lie in a field unramified outside `{2, 3}`.

Two consistency checks:

- `X⁶(ℝ) = ∅` (Shimura 1975; GR Prop. 5.3) forces `c₀ < 0 < c₁`. With only reciprocity at
  `t = 0` and `t = ∞` used, the candidates are `(-1, 3)` and `(6, -2)`; real points exclude the
  second, and so does `t = 1`.
- The note's own rederivation (§4.1.2) uses the class-number-one values `-19, -43, -67, -163`
  from Elkies' table. Two of those (`-67, -163`) are only numerical in Elkies. The derivation above
  avoids the table altogether.

### 2.4 The fibre over t(u) (VERIFIED)

With these constants, `y₂² + 3y₁² + 3 = 0`. Over `t(u)` we have `-t(u) = (iH/G³)²` and
`3(t(u) - 1) = (540iF/G³)²`. So:

- The fibre consists of the four points `(±iH/G³, ±540iF/G³)`. They are distinct and none is a
  branch point (§1.5), so they form a free `W`-orbit.
- Each point has residue field exactly `ℚ(i)`.
- `σ ∈ Gal(ℚ̄/ℚ)` fixes every point if `σ(i) = i`. Otherwise it negates both coordinates, i.e. it
  acts as `w₃`. In particular complex conjugation acts as `w₃`.

**A geometric consequence (VERIFIED, `check.py` part F).** `u ↦ (iH/G³, 540iF/G³)` is a lift of
`P¹_u → X*` to `X⁶`, defined over `ℚ(i)`. In a parameter of the conic it has degree 3.

- It is totally ramified over the two `D = -3` points: the roots of `G`, where `J` has index 6
  and `X⁶ → X*` has index 2.
- It is unramified elsewhere. Riemann–Hurwitz: `-2 = 3(-2) + 2·2`.
- So it is geometrically the cyclic quotient by the rotation group `C₃`, as the note's Remark (a)
  says.
- The six contacts map without ramification to the two `D = -4` points. This reproduces the
  manuscript's statement that the normalized cover is branched exactly at the contacts with index
  2 (lines 517-523).

## 3. Shimura reciprocity and the structure of CM(𝔬)

Notation: `𝔬 ⊂ K` is an order of conductor `f`, `H_𝔬 = K(f)` is its ring class field, and `CM(𝔬)`
is the set of points of `X⁶ = X₀(6,1)` with CM by `𝔬`.

GR define CM points by normalized optimal embeddings of `𝔬` into `𝒪`, taken modulo `𝒪¹`. For
`N = 1` this agrees with the note's definition `End_𝒪(A_x) = 𝔬`, because
`End_𝒪(A_x) = K ∩ 𝒪` for the embedding fixing the period. The remark in Saia following their
restatement of Jordan and GR also notes that the two conventions agree at level 1.

### 3.1 (S): K·ℚ(x) = K(f) (STANDARD)

The statement is GR Thm. 5.8 (1), with the Galois action of Thm. 5.8 (2): `x^{σ_𝔞} = Φ(β⁻¹x_q)`.
It applies to `X₀(D,N)` for every `N` coprime to `D`, here `N = 1`, with no further condition.

- GR attribute it to Shimura 1967, **Main Theorem II**, and to Shimura 1970. Shimura 1967 Main
  Theorem I is cited there only for the existence of the canonical model.
- The note cites Main Theorem I. Saia cites "Main Thm. 1".
- The theorem numbering in Shimura's paper was not checked here. Cite GR Thm. 5.8, or "Shimura
  1967, §3 (Main Theorems I-II)" (repair R2).

The content was also checked directly in the adelic form: the stabilizer of `[z, 1]` under
`rec(s)` is `K^×𝔬̂^×`. This uses only that `ℚ` has class number one and `nrd(𝒪̂^×) = ℤ̂^×`. That is
the version for the connected curve `𝒪¹\ℍ` used here.

### 3.2 (Emb): local embedding condition (STANDARD and VERIFIED)

At `p | 6` the algebra `B_p` is a division algebra, so `K_p` must be a field: `p` is not split in
`K`. The unique maximal order is `𝒪_p = {x : nrd x ∈ ℤ_p}`, and
`𝒪_p ∩ K_p = 𝒪_{K_p}`. So an optimal embedding forces `p ∤ f`.

Reference: Vignéras, LNM 800, Ch. II §1 and Ch. III §5. The note's citation is correct.

**A caution about the sources (repair R1(a)).** GR Prop. 5.6 states the criterion "CM(R) is
nonempty iff `DN/(D(R)N*(R))` divides `disc R`", with their Eichler symbol `(R/p) = 1` for
`p | f`. Read literally, at `N = 1`, this accepts `p | (f, D)`. For example, it accepts
`R = ℤ[3i]` in discriminant 6, which has no optimal embedding. Saia's Prop. "number of CM points"
also omits the hypothesis `(f, D) = 1`.

The count `2^b h(𝔬)` is correct only together with (Emb). The note proves `(f, 6) = 1` and
"no `p | 6` splits" from (Emb) and does not rely on either criterion, so the argument is
unaffected. The audit record for (JGR) should, however, state the hypothesis `(f, 6) = 1`
explicitly.

### 3.3 (JGR): the structure of CM(𝔬) (STANDARD)

The statement is GR Prop. 5.6 with `N = 1`. `W(R) = ⟨w_p : p | 6 inert in K⟩` is the note's
`W'`. Then:

- `W(R) × Gal(H_R/K)` acts freely and transitively on `CM(R)`;
- `#CM(R) = 2^b h(R)`.

The other parts of the claim:

- **`W` preserves `CM(R)`.** For `p` ramified in `K`, GR Lemma 5.9 shows that `w_p` acts on
  `CM(R)` as `σ_𝔭`. Together with Prop. 5.6 this covers all of `W`.
- **The adelic description.** It was rederived independently: for `(f, 6) = 1`, `CM(𝔬)` is
  `K^×\K̂^×/𝔬̂^×` times `∏_{p inert}{1, ϖ_p}`. This uses that `B_p^× = K_p^× 𝒪_p^×` when `K_p` is
  ramified, and that `[B_p^× : K_p^× 𝒪_p^×] = 2` when `K_p` is unramified.
- **Agreement with the signature.** The count matches the orbifold signature of `X⁶`: there are 2
  points with `D = -4` and 2 with `D = -3`.

Jordan's thesis (`f = 1`) was not consulted (UNCHECKED), but GR cover every `f`.

**A caution about Saia (repair R1(b)).** Saia's restatement of the field-of-moduli theorem
("Theorem [Jordan, González–Rotger]") has its two cases interchanged. It says
`ℚ(x) = K(f)` when `D(K) = 1`. GR Thm. 5.12 says `ℚ(x) = H_R` when `D(R) ≠ 1`, and index 2 when
`D(R) = 1`. GR's version is right:

- Elkies' `D = -19` point has `D(R) = 6`, and its fibre field is `K = H_R` (`check.py` part E).
- The `D = -24` points have `D(R) = 1`, and `ℚ(x) = ℚ(√-3)` has index 2 in `H₋₂₄`.

Saia's later text (the involution fixing a vertex "forces `D(K) = 1`") follows GR, so this is a
misprint. The note does not use that statement. It cites Saia only for (S) and for the count. It
should cite GR rather than Saia for both.

### 3.4 The ramified Atkin–Lehner rule (STANDARD)

For `p | 6` ramified in `K` and `p ∤ f`, `σ_𝔭(x) = w_p(x)`. This is GR Lemma 5.9, and it is the
rule behind the note's Route C4.

### 3.5 h(𝔬) ≤ 2 (VERIFIED)

`ℚ(x) = ℚ(i)` by §2.4, so `K(f) = K·ℚ(x) = K(i)` and `h(𝔬) = [K(f):K] ≤ 2`.

## 4. The case split

By (Emb), each of 2 and 3 is inert or ramified in `K`, and `(f, 6) = 1`.

**Exhaustiveness (VERIFIED).** The cases "2 inert" (A), "2 and 3 ramified" (B) and "2 ramified, 3
inert" (C) cover all four combinations. The suggested missing cases are excluded by (Emb):

- 3 split;
- 2 split;
- `2 | f` or `3 | f`.

The elliptic orders `ℤ[i]` and `ℤ[ζ₃]` fall under C and A. Their points lie over `t = 1` and
`t = ∞`, which `t(u)` avoids.

**Case A (VERIFIED).**

- 2 is unramified in `K` and, since `2 ∤ f`, unramified in `K(f)/K`. The reason is that ring class
  fields are unramified outside `f` (STANDARD: Cox, §9).
- `ℚ(i) = ℚ(x) ⊆ K(x) = K(f)`, and 2 ramifies in `ℚ(i)`. Contradiction.

**Case B (VERIFIED).**

- Here `b = 0`, so `#CM(𝔬) = h(𝔬) ≤ 2`.
- `W·x ⊆ CM(𝔬)` has four elements, because `W` acts freely on the fibre over `t(u)`. Contradiction.

**Case C (VERIFIED).**

- Here `W' = {1, w₃}`. For `σ ∈ Gal(ℚ̄/K)`, §2.4 gives `σx ∈ {x, w₃x}`, and (S) gives
  `σx = σ_𝔞x`.
- Freeness of `W' × Pic(𝔬)` forces `σ_𝔞 = 1` for every `σ`. Since the Artin map is onto,
  `h(𝔬) = 1`.
- Then `#CM(𝔬) = 2 < 4`. Contradiction.
- Alternatively, by GR Lemma 5.9, `σ_{𝔭₂}` acts on `x` as `w₂ ∉ {1, w₃}`.

**The count `2^b h(𝔬)` on the table (VERIFIED, `check.py` part E).** All 24 non-elliptic rows of
Elkies' Table 1 satisfy (Emb) and `2^b h(𝔬) = 4`, i.e. each `CM(𝔬)` is a single free `W`-orbit
lying over a rational point of `X*`. Class numbers were computed by counting reduced forms. For
example, `-75, -100, -147` are the orders of conductor 5, 5, 7 with `h = 2` and `b = 1`.

## 5. The interface with the proof of Theorem 4.1

**Where the fibre surface lives (VERIFIED by reading lines 510-580).**

- The surface `A` is a fibre of the universal abelian scheme over `Y₃`, at a point of the
  normalized pullback lying over `u`.
- `A` is defined over the splitting field `K` of that fibre. That field has degree up to `324!` and
  is generally not `ℚ`.

manuscript: the point of the normalized cover, the field `K`, the model `A/K` and its conjugates
(lines 532-536).

`M₂(k) = B ⊗ k` is `k`, so `End_𝒪(A)` is an order in `k`.

### 7.2 Corrected statement, with a shorter proof

**Lemma (no CM fibres).** Let `u ∈ ℚ \ 𝒞`, and let `x ∈ X⁶(ℚ̄)` lie over `j = J(u)`. Then `x` is
not a CM point. Hence `End⁰(A_ℚ̄) = B` for every fibre surface `A` in the proof of Theorem 4.1.

*Proof.* The proof has three steps.

1. **Coordinates.** Put `t = 1 + 27j/16`. By Baba–Granath (lines 421-422 and 908-909), `t` takes
   the values `0, 1, ∞` at the CM points of discriminants `-24, -4, -3`.
2. **The model.** The function field of `X⁶` is `ℚ(t)(√(-t), √(3(t-1)))`, with `w₂, w₆, w₃`
   negating `√(3(t-1))`, `√(-t)` and both, respectively. The shape is forced by the action of `W`
   over `ℚ`. The constants are forced by Shimura reciprocity at the three elliptic points (§2.3),
   in agreement with Elkies §3.
3. **The fibre.** `t(u) = H²/G⁶` and `t(u) - 1 = -3(180F/G³)²`, with `FGH(u) ≠ 0`. So the four
   points over `t(u)` are `(±iH/G³, ±540iF/G³)`. They form a free `W`-orbit with residue field
   `ℚ(i)`.

Suppose `x ∈ CM(R)` with `R ⊂ K`. By (Emb), `(f, 6) = 1` and neither 2 nor 3 splits in `K`. Let
`D(R)` be the product of the primes `p | 6` inert in `K`.

- **If `D(R) ≠ 1`.** By GR Thm. 5.12, `ℚ(x) = H_R ⊇ K`. So `K = ℚ(i)` and `h(R) = 1`. Since
  `2 ∤ f`, `R = ℤ[i]`. Then `x` lies over `t = 1`, i.e. `F(u) = 0`, which is excluded.
- **If `D(R) = 1`.** By GR Thm. 5.12, `[H_R : ℚ(x)] = 2`, so `h(R) = 2`. By GR Prop. 5.6,
  `#CM(R) = h(R) = 2`. But `W·x ⊆ CM(R)` has four elements. Contradiction. ∎
